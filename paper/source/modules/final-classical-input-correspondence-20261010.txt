1. Finding and scope

For the actual coefficient class in distribution-medium-completion-20261010.txt
lines 349--380 and 463--483, the named classical spectral statements exist
with the required quantifiers. I found no missing spectral inequality in
that interface. This finding is more precise than a blanket completion:

2. Exact normalization and the moving integer a

The relevant ABL source is Assing--Blomer--Li, Uniform Titchmarsh divisor
problems, arXiv:2005.13915v1, (3.1)--(3.8), Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2, PDF pp.7--11.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.13915v1
Saved primary extraction:
external-source-audit-20261009/assing-blomer-li-2005.13915v1-large-a-20261009.txt
especially lines 575--1010. The paper's variables correspond to
 q_ABL=1, r_ABL=z, s_ABL=1, Q_ABL=z, Q_bad=z, Q_good=1, conductor=1.
The dispersion factor d in E2=sum_d E3(d)/d is NOT q_ABL or a level.

Use R(n)=sqrt(|n|)*rho_ABL(n), the DI normalization. At level z the scaling
matrices are sigma_infinity=Id and sigma_1=[[sqrt(z),0],[sqrt(z),1/sqrt(z)]].
Drappeau Lemma 4.3 (PDF p.11) gives the Kloosterman cusp conversion; in this
case its phase is e(ay/z). Reversing that conversion gives e(-ay/z) on the
n coefficient of the ordinary Kloosterman sum in the medium argument.
https://drappeau.perso.math.cnrs.fr/documents/divtm-siegel.pdf

Write y=nu*n, nu|a^infinity, (n,a)=1. In ABL Lemma 3.1 the exceptional
integer K is 1 for EVERY oldform dilation because (a,z)=1. Thus
 R_v(a*nu*n)=lambda_v(a*nu)*e(n*(a*nu-1)/z)*R_v(n).
Multiplication by the retained cusp phase e(-a*nu*n/z) leaves EXACTLY
e(-n/z). Therefore the transformed functional is
 beta_nu(n,z)=1_((n,a)=1)*beta(nu*n,z)*e(-n/z),
common to all dilations, and independent of height and future m,c variables.
The negative sign version is its corresponding signed identity. This is
an algebraic identity, with no small-a expansion and no frozen-a constant.

One must not pretend lambda_v is common to an oldform packet. ABL (3.3)
instead writes the orthonormal packet using coefficients xi_g(v). Its
Frobenius norm is z^epsilon after divisor losses. Each normalized dilation
has norm one, so Cauchy bounds evaluation on it by the norm of the same
linear functional. Bounding lambda_v then costs (|a|*nu)^(7/64+epsilon).
In squared norms this is (|a|*nu)^theta, theta=7/32. The unchanged m
functional can now use the level average. This is the packet reasoning in
fixed-d-abl-packet-input-audit-20261010.txt lines 117--157, and agrees with
the printed ABL Lemma 3.2 rather than assuming a general n-removal theorem.

ABL Lemma 3.2 explicitly includes the holomorphic and continuous quadratic
forms, without the a and nu powers in those two cases. Its continuous
proof invokes the character-pair oldclasses with theta=0. Thus the actual
cumulative cusp-summed continuous form has the SAME t-independent beta_nu
and NO additional power of the height. This is not a claim that individual
Eisenstein series are square-integrable cusp forms. Young's formal
Plancherel basis identity (Prop.8.2, arXiv:1710.03624v2) matches this use.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.03624v2

Although ABL initially writes a length >=1, its algebra is valid also for
the endpoint block 1/2<=Y<1. Every nonempty transformed interval has actual
length Y/nu>=1/2; the regular theorem below expressly allows that length.
The possible integer n=1 must stay in its actual interval, not be moved
to (1,2]. No source claim for an empty sub-unit integer interval is needed.

3. Exceptional norms, complex weights and intervals

Pascadi, arXiv:2404.04239v3, gives the needed classical formulations:
https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04239v3
Saved primary TeX-preserving extraction:
external-source-audit-20261009/pascadi-2404.04239v3-classical-spectral-inputs-20261009.txt
Theorem A: lines 512--556; Theorem J: lines 2886--2943.

Let tau_j=2*sqrt(1/4-lambda_j), so tau_j<=theta=7/32. Theorem A at level z
and cusp 1, whose mu is 1/z, allows ANY complex n sequence and any scaling
matrix. With L=Y/nu and X0=1+Z/L, z~Z, its allowed X range includes X0:
 sum_exc X0^tau_j*|sum_(n~L)b_n R_j,1(n)|^2
       <<(zL)^epsilon*(1+L/z)*sum_n|b_n|^2.
Hence b may depend on z. No level-averaged arbitrary-coefficient version
is being inferred here. The stronger z^2/L^3 range in the source is unused.

Theorem J, with sigma_infinity=Id, states the level average for e(m*omega),
uniformly in ONE common omega, for X<<max(M,Z^2/M). Its proof explicitly
records the extension from DI's phase-zero printed statement. Consequently
the correct citation is this explicit uniform-phase theorem, not the claim
that DI's original printed special case itself contained all quantifiers.

Here is the additional partial-interval derivation, rather than an assumed
arbitrary-weight extension. For I contained in (M,2M], choose integer
P>4M. On residues modulo P write the exact finite expansion
 1_I(m)=sum_(0<=k<P)c_k*e(k*m/P),
where sum_k|c_k|=O(log(2+M)). Minkowski in the Hilbert norm indexed by
(z,j), followed by Theorem J at each omega+k/P, gives
 sum_(z~Z,exc) X^tau_j*|sum_(m in I)e(m*omega)R_j,infty(m)|^2
       <<(ZM)^epsilon*(Z+M)*M.
The extra logarithmic square is absorbed by a selectable epsilon. The same
I must be used for all z; this proof does not interchange a level-dependent
maximum with a spectral sum. A common I of bounded size is immediate.

For larger X, tau_j<=theta gives the continuation factor
 1+(M*X/(M+Z)^2)^theta.
Using X=(1+X_kernel/sqrt(X0))^2 and the n-side X0 split gives the exceptional
term in medium equation (24). The positive imaginary cosh weights are
bounded above and below in this exceptional range, so their omission only
changes constants. No arbitrary level-dependent m coefficients are used.

4. Regular spectrum and the summation over height

Drappeau Proposition 4.7, PDF pp.15--16, explicitly states the three
holomorphic/Maass/continuous cumulative inequalities for T>=1, N>=1/2
and arbitrary complex coefficients in the sqrt(n)*rho normalization.
The continuous measure is dt/cosh(pi*t), with a cusp sum; the bound is
 (T^2+q0^(1/2)*mu(cusp)*N^(1+epsilon))*||b||_2^2.
Here q0=1 and mu(cusp)=1/z. This matches ABL (3.2),(3.8), including its
continuous convention. Pascadi Lemma H is the DI-normalized formulation.

Pascadi Lemma D (saved lines 2400--2426) supplies both transform bounds
 |H(t)| << (1+|log Y|)/(1+Y),
 |H(t)| << |t|^(-5/2)+Y*|t|^(-3) for |t|>=1.
The local regular proof first uses cumulative norms up to max(1,2Y), then
dyadic shells. The two norm factors together grow by at most 2^(2j),
while these two transform terms leave 2^(-j/2) and 2^(-j). Thus the shell
sum converges, with no extra hidden power of a or the spectral height.
At t=0 use the limiting transform; for holomorphic forms use height k-1.

At each level both cusp densities are 1/z. Cauchy in z costs sqrt(Z),
not Z. Carrying these factors through gives the first term of medium
(24); combining section 3 gives its second term. The needed arithmetic
singleton is s=1, not a claim for general r,s,q in a larger theorem.
The explicit polynomial calculation is in
spectral-regular-polynomial-derivation-20261010.txt lines 55--155.

5. Lemma 4.6: why this replacement actually pays for the large phase

The original problematic L23 source is retained at
external-source-audit/l23-v1-joint-current.txt lines 1697--1795.
Its proof replaces a reciprocity factor by 1 plus an error of size
(|a|/x)*log(x)^O(1). Such an error cannot be discarded uniformly when
a is of order x. The current argument uses the exact factor instead.

In medium lines 299--310 the actual normalization is
 E2=sum_(d~D)E3(d)/d,
and exact reciprocity leaves e(a*h/(q*d*w1*w2*n1)). With
 H=Q*D*Rcal^2*log(x)^5/M, x=N*M,
its dimensionless amplitude on q~q0~Q is
 (a/x)*(Q/q0)*(D/d)*log(x)^5.
After normalizing q,w1,w2,n1,h to bounded intervals, any mixed derivative
using each of those five coordinates at most once is bounded by
O((1+|a|/x*log(x)^5)^5). Iterated partial summation therefore costs at
most x^(5*kappa+o(1)). This is an explicit fixed power, not an arbitrarily
small epsilon or a Taylor remainder. Endpoint rectangles are fixed before
the later spectral sums; the resulting h coefficient is independent of
their other variables. There is no arbitrary q coefficient left over.

The current note also keeps the |a|/x from the returned spectral polynomial:
 |W_bare| << N^2/Q*x^epsilon
      *{D0^(1/2)+[(1+|a|/x)*D1+D2]^(1/4)},
where D0<=x^(-8*sigma), D1,D2<=x^(-15*sigma). Thus the full exponents are
 -4*sigma+5*kappa+epsilon,
 -15*sigma/4+21*kappa/4+epsilon.
At kappa<=sigma/100, allowing sigma/10 for every other selectable loss
including ONE normalizing D factor, the respective savings are at least
 (77/20)*sigma=3.85*sigma and (1439/400)*sigma=3.5975*sigma.
Both exceed sigma/2. The harmonic sum sum_(d~D)1/d=O(1) introduces no
second D factor; the phase is not paid a second time. This checks the
claimed narrow rescue, including its actual a dependence, directly.

6. Necessary boundaries and meaning of this finding

The source correspondence would fail as written if (a,z)=1 were removed:
the ABL integer K can then depend on the dilation, destroying the simple
common beta_nu used above. It also does not justify coefficients depending
arbitrarily on spectral height, a level-dependent choice of m interval,
or an arbitrary q-dependent Lambda before the bare-sum reduction.

The current medium note explicitly retains these restrictions and charges
the phase as above. Within that class I found a precise correspondence
to established primary spectral results, rather than a circular invocation
of the desired moving-residue estimate. This audit has not re-opened the
separately replaced long-variable cleanup and has not independently rerun
the numerical certificates or verified the entire global distribution
assembly. It changes no theorem constants or main-manuscript claims.
