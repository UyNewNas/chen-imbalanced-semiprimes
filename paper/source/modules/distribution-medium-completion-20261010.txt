Completion of the local medium convolution theorem, with a Poisson-tail repair
10 October 2026. New proof; the old records, manuscript and state are unchanged.

1. The theorem actually established

Fix theta=7/32, 0<sigma<1/100, c0>0, a fixed divisor order B, and
0<=kappa<=sigma/100. Put x=NM and assume
 c0*x<=|a|<=x^(1+kappa),
 Q1<N*x^(-sigma),
 N^2*Q2*Q3^2<x^(1-8sigma),
 |a|^theta*N*Q1*Q2^5*Q3^2<x^(2-15sigma),
 N^(1+2theta)*Q1*Q2^(5-2theta)*Q3^(2+2theta)<x^(2-15sigma).  (1)
All lengths are at least one, with fixed endpoint multiples allowed using
the strict margins. A bounded nominal group may carry delta_1.

Let alpha be one-bounded, supported on one interval in [N,2N] and on
Pminus(n)>z=x^(1/(loglog x)^3). Assume full auxiliary Siegel--Walfisz:
 for each A>0 and every k,q>=1, (b,q)=1,
 |sum_(n=b mod q,(n,k)=1)alpha_n
   -phi(q)^(-1)sum_((n,kq)=1)alpha_n|
       <=C_A*N*tau(k)^C0*log(x)^(-A),                       (2)
with fixed C0 and uniform family constants. Alpha is fixed while the
moduli are summed. Let |beta_m|<=tau(m)^B, m in [M,2M]. Let gamma_i be
three separate one-bounded sequences on qi~Qi with (qi,a)=1. Define
 Delta_l=sum_(n,m)alpha_n beta_m
       [1_(nm=a mod l)-1_((nm,l)=1)/phi(l)].
Then, relative to the finite list of established classical inputs in
section 10, for every A>0,
 |sum_(q1,q2,q3)gamma_1(q1)gamma_2(q2)gamma_3(q3)Delta_(q1q2q3)|
                         <<_A x/log(x)^A.                  (3)

The proof below derives (3); it does not assume preliminary moving-a
dispersion, a bare L51 bound, or an unrestricted large-residue theorem as
additional analytic inputs. It retains the exact coprime mean. Its
constant is uniform in a and all coefficient families with the stated
uniform bounds. The assertion is a signed factorized-modulus estimate;
it is not an absolute discrepancy sum over arbitrary moduli.

For the current application alpha is an individual chart-rough 1 or mu
slot with an individual interval and a fixed-polylogarithmic Mellin twist.
The explicit rough-SW derivation supplies (2) with C0=1 from classical
Mobius SW. Beta's order is fixed by the finite number of remaining HB
slots. The currently adopted elementary q=1 alternative also requires
kappa<=1/(2*K_mom), with the already fixed maximum moment order; this
only narrows the application of (3), whose more general proof may retain
classical Shiu at q=1. No new constraint on K,c,b follows here.

2. A small omitted specification and its constructive repair

The preliminary notes call the majorant merely a fixed smooth function,
then truncate Poisson at H=(modulus/M)*log(x)^5 with a power-small tail.
An arbitrary C-infinity majorant supplies Schwartz decay, which alone
does not prove that stated power-small tail at this logarithmic cutoff.
Maynard Lemma 13.4 expressly distinguishes its general-smooth cutoff
from its derivative-controlled cutoff. The free dispersion majorant
must be chosen in the latter class. This is a specification repair,
not a counterexample to the intended local theorem.

Here is a sufficient choice with no new external theorem. Choose positive
lengths a_j=c/j^2 with sum_j a_j=1/2. Let u_j be the uniform probability
density on [-a_j/2,a_j/2] and let phi be their infinite convolution.
This probability measure has support [-1/4,1/4]. Differentiating the
first j box factors as finite signed measures gives total variation
at most prod_(i<=j)(2/a_i)=C^j*(j!)^2. The remaining convolution is a
probability measure. Additional undifferentiated box factors give
continuous densities, so this also proves the required differentiability
of the final convolution to every fixed order. Set
                 psi=1_[3/4,9/4]*phi.
Then 0<=psi<=1, psi=1 on [1,2], support(psi) subset [1/2,5/2], and
                 ||psi^(j)||_infinity<=C^j*(j!)^2.         (4)
The construction and its constants are fixed, independently of x and a.

Integrating by parts j times and choosing j proportional to sqrt(|t|)
in (4), with j!<=j^j, gives
                 |psihat(t)|<=C1*exp(-c1*sqrt(|t|)).        (5)
For any polynomial-sized modulus l and scale M, exact Poisson followed
by (5), with H>=C2*l*log(x)^5/M, gives
 (M/l)*sum_(|h|>H)|psihat(hM/l)|=O_D(x^(-D))               (6)
for every fixed D. A decreasing-integral comparison bounds the left
side by a polynomial prefactor times exp(-c2*log(x)^(5/2)). If H<1,
the first omitted integer already has |h|M/l>c*log(x)^5, so (6) still
holds. This argument also allows polynomial l>x; no truncated source
lemma with l<=x is being overextended.

Use this same psi for the positive q and m majorants in dispersion.
E1 needs only its fixed first few seminorms after completion; E2's five
coordinate derivatives also use only fixed seminorms. Thus (4)--(6)
validate their unchanged logarithmic H cutoffs and introduce no new
power loss. The smooth majorants used much later in the spectral return
may remain ordinary C-infinity: there the cutoff is x^epsilon and its
tail is paid by a fixed derivative order. These are distinct operations.

3. Preliminary lengths, smooth-factor deletion, and beta normalization

Write n=log_x N, q=log_x Q1, u=log_x Q2, v=log_x Q3. From (1),
 n>sigma, n<=1/2-4sigma,
 q+u+v<7/10-13sigma/2.                                   (7)
The last follows from q<n-sigma, 2n+u+2v<1-8sigma,
and u<(2-15sigma)/5. In particular the complete modulus is eventually
at most x^(4/5), and both N and M are fixed positive powers of x.

Put y=x^(1/loglog x) and split q2=u0*s, q3=v0*t into their z-rough
and z-smooth parts. Terms with s>y or t>y are negligible. Indeed, the
collapsed product coefficient is divisor bounded. Primitive Shiu for
integers of size x, with modulus l<=x^(4/5), bounds count plus coprime
mean by x*tau(l)^C*log(x)^C/l, uniformly in the residue represented by a.
The modulus multiplicity is included in this fixed C. Rankin with
xi=1/log z gives
 sum_(l<=x^(4/5),sm(l;z)>=y)tau(l)^C/l
   <<exp(-(loglog x)^2+O_C(loglog x)).                    (8)
This is stronger than every requested logarithmic saving. A pointwise
x^epsilon divisor bound would not suffice in (8), and is not used.

Dyadically fix s,t,u0,v0 and their products d=st~D, w=u0*v0~Rcal.
There are a fixed power of log x such blocks,
 D<=y^2=x^o(1), D*Rcal comparable to Q2*Q3.
Define literally
 Lambda_d(w)=sum_(st=d,u0*v0=w)gamma_2(u0*s)gamma_3(v0*t)
                  *1_(all the fixed intervals/supports).
It is independent of q1 and |Lambda_d(w)|<=tau(d*w).
All d in this dyadic block remain inside ONE preliminary dispersion
estimate. We never sum D separate arbitrary-logarithmic remainders.
With Q=Q1,Y=QD and delta=sigma/3, (7) gives eventually
 N,M>=x^delta, Y<=N*x^(-delta), Y*Rcal<=x^(1-delta).        (9)

It suffices initially to prove (3) for one-bounded beta. For general
beta, truncate at tau(m)<=log(x)^C and normalize by log(x)^(BC).
Alpha and (2) remain unchanged. The discarded coefficient at v=nm is
at most log(x)^(-C)*tau(v)^(B+2). For v!=a, summing the three-factor
modulus coefficient over l|v-a costs at most tau(|v-a|)^3. Fixed shifted
moments therefore make the discarded count O(x*log(x)^(E-C)), E fixed
independently of C. The coprime mean has the same form by ordinary
divisor means and sum_l |rho(l)|/phi(l)<<log(x)^O(1).
The exact v=a instead costs x^(4/5+o(1)); never write tau(0).
Choose C first, then the one-bounded theorem's log exponent to pay BC.
The current elementary mixed-moment lemma supplies this q=1 cleanup
under its additional fixed kappa cap, and is independent of log precision.

4. Shifted progressions and the exact-product order

The nonprimitive shifted progression bound used here is, for fixed
C,K,delta and I of length at most L in [-x^K,x^K],
 sum_(v in I,v=r mod f,v!=0)tau(|v|)^C
       <<(L/f)*tau(f)^C*log(x)^O(1), f<=L*x^(-delta).       (10)
To derive it put g=(r,f), v=g*w and f'=f/g. The w progression is
primitive and has L/g divided by f' equal to L/f>=x^delta.
Values |w|<=f'*x^(delta/2) cost x^(3delta/4) by a fixed divisor bound.
For the rest, split signs and dyadic sizes; cover by intervals of length
H comparable to the smaller of their scale and L/g. They satisfy
H>=c*f'*x^(delta/2), H>=c*x^(delta/2), and location X<=C*x^K.
Primitive Shiu applies with fixed alpha,beta<=delta/(8K), giving
(L/f)*tau(g)^C*log(x)^O(1). This proves (10), including a translated
interval at a polynomially large shift. Cauchy gives the mixed version
for tau(v)^B*tau(|v-a|)^C on a progression v=r mod f, v!=a.

Delete nm=a from every original congruence indicator BEFORE Cauchy;
leave its coprime mean unchanged. The first-moment correction is
O(Y*Rcal*x^o(1)), power-small by (9). The sequences are unchanged;
no m-dependent SW hypothesis is introduced. The square has unchanged
pure-mean S1, singly deleted S2', and doubly deleted S3'.

In S2' removal of (m,s)>1 uses a rough common factor f>z. The r-sum
contributes Rcal/f times a divisor power; collapsing v=mn with f|m
and applying (10) supplies x/f times a fixed logarithmic/divisor cost.
Thus the error is V*log(x)^C/z, V=MN^2/Y, by the divisor tail
sum_(f>z)tau(f)^C/f^2<<log(z)^O(1)/z. Only afterwards restore nm=a
for the one-congruence Poisson step. Its cost is Rcal*N*x^o(1).

In S3' first discard large coefficients or large tau(mn_i-a), using
(10) at f=1 and the n2 progression length O(N/Y+1). The error is
(x*N/Y+x)*log(x)^(C0-Ccut); C0 is fixed before Ccut. Under the remaining
logarithmic cutoffs, the exclusions (r,s)>1, (n1,n2)>1 and (s,ell)>1,
ell=qd, cost V*log(x)^C/z+x*log(x)^C. The counting reasons are concrete:
 * a common rough f in r,s gives n1=n2 mod ell*f, since m is a unit;
 * a common rough f in n1,n2 is coprime to ell; one n1 multiple count
   and one progression count give the two factors 1/f;
 * f|(s,ell) forces mn2=a mod ell*f, again one primitive class.
Their +1 terms contribute x*log(x)^C, absorbed by Y/N<=x^(-delta).
The close-pair restriction |n1-n2|>=N/log(x)^Csep costs
V*log(x)^(C-Csep)+x*log(x)^C. Choose Csep after Ccut. Restore large
coefficient cutoffs while the shifts are still nonzero.

Now n1!=n2. A single equality mn1=a leaves x^o(1) choices of m,n1,
O(N/Y+1) choices of n2 for each q,d, and only x^o(1) possibilities
for the other rough modulus dividing the NONZERO mn2-a. Its total
restoration cost is Rcal*(N+Y)*x^o(1)=Rcal*N*x^o(1). Both equalities
cannot hold. Restore these single zeros now, immediately before CRT
and full smooth Poisson. A premature double-zero restoration, of
size Y*Rcal^2*x^o(1), is neither made nor covered by (9).

5. The exact principal terms and their positive variance

Fix q,d; let ell=qd, eta_r=Lambda_d(r),
 B_r=sum_((n,ell*r)=1)alpha_n,
 A_r(b)=sum_(n=b mod ell,(n,r)=1)alpha_n, (b,ell)=1,
 d_r(b)=A_r(b)-B_r/phi(ell),
 c_ell(r,s)=phi(ell*r*s)/(ell*r*s*phi(ell*r)*phi(ell*s)).
With h0=integral psi and w_q=psi(q/Q), the pure mean is
 MT=M*h0*sum_(q,d)w_q*sum_(r,s)eta_r*conj(eta_s)
                                            *c_ell(r,s)*B_r*conj(B_s).
The S1 counting error is O(N^2*log(x)^C/Y), by Mobius inversion and
ordinary divisor means. It is V times a fixed inverse power by (9).

For any common multiple L of ell and its finitely many supported r,
 c_ell(r,s)=(1/L)*sum_(v mod L)
    [1_((v,ell*r)=1)/phi(ell*r)]*[1_((v,ell*s)=1)/phi(ell*s)].  (11)
This is an exact positive Gram kernel. L is never used as an error scale.
In the restored S2 zero mode the coefficient is
1/(ell*r*phi(ell*s)); its ratio to c_ell(r,s) is 1+O(log(x)/z).
Hence S2'=MT+M*E1+O_A(V/log(x)^A+Rcal*N*x^o(1)).

On the S3 clean CRT set (r,s)=(s,ell)=1,
                  phi(ell)*c_ell(r,s)=1/(ell*r*s)          (12)
exactly, even if r shares prime factors with ell. The h=0 CRT term
may have its rough common-factor and close-pair exclusions restored;
ordinary progression counts give the same V/log(x)^A errors, with
own conditions (n1,ell*r)=(n2,ell*s)=1 retained. It becomes MT+P, where
 P=M*h0*sum_(q,d)w_q*phi(ell)*sum_(r,s)eta_r*conj(eta_s)*c_ell(r,s)
                                       *sum_b d_r(b)*conj(d_s(b))>=0.   (13)
The equality follows from sum_b A_r(b)=B_r; positivity follows from
(11). Thus the three main terms leave P, not zero.

For any fixed w and A, auxiliary SW (2) and the primitive character
large sieve imply
 sum_(ell<=8Y)tau(ell)^w*sum_b |d_r(b)|^2
                 <<tau(r)^(2C0)*N^2/log(x)^A.              (14)
Here is the essential derivation, including the imprimitive characters.
Normalize alpha*1_((n,r)=1) by tau(r)^C0; auxiliary SW constants stay
uniform because tau(kr)<=tau(k)tau(r). Character orthogonality gives
variance phi(l)^(-1)*sum_(chi!=principal)|sum alpha_n chi(n)|^2.
Write l=f*k with primitive conductor f>1 and
chi(n)=chi_star(n)*1_((n,k)=1). Since phi(fk)>=phi(f)phi(k), the
large sieve on dyadic f~R0 for a FIXED k gives
 O(N/phi(k)*(N/R0+R0)). Summing f>log(x)^b and k gives
O(N^2*log(x)^2/log(x)^b+N*Y). Small conductors are bounded by (2),
with the explicit cost tau(k)^C0; their total is
O(N^2*log(x)^(3b+C1-2Bsw)), C1 fixed before Bsw. Choose b, Bsw last.
For the weight tau(l)^w use variance<=O(N^2/l), ordinary divisor
means, and Cauchy in l with the unweighted arbitrarily strong saving.
This proves (14) since Y<=N*x^(-delta), without an x^o(1) on a log error.

Use Cauchy on the O(Rcal) terms in (11), phi(ell*r)^(-1)<<log(x)/(Y*Rcal),
and the at most tau(ell) representations ell=qd. With a fixed common
divisor order B0 dominating C0 and the actual Lambda bound,
 P<<M*log(x)^2/(Y*Rcal)*sum_(r~Rcal)tau(r)^(2B0)
         *sum_(ell<=8Y)tau(ell)^(2B0+1)*sum_b|d_r(b)|^2
   <<MN^2/(Y*log(x)^A).                                  (15)
The last step keeps tau(r)^(2C0) from (14) and sums it by an ordinary
divisor MEAN. This is uniform in r, not an unproved filter-stability claim.

Outer Cauchy has multiplier M*Y*log(x)^C. Equations (13)--(15), the two
Poisson remainders, and section 4 prove
 |S_block|<<_A x/log(x)^A
    +M*sqrt(Y)*log(x)^C*(sqrt(|E1|)+sqrt(|E2|)).            (16)
The single-zero cost becomes sqrt(MY*Rcal*N)*x^o(1)
=sqrt(x*Y*Rcal)*x^o(1)<=x^(1-delta/2+o(1)). This verifies its uniform
absorption in the logarithmic term. Equation (6) pays all Fourier tails.

6. E1: complete inversion sums and explicit scale margins

For a fixed smooth w, complete inverse phases satisfy
 |sum_((n,l)=1)w(n/N)e(A*inverse(n)/l)|
   <<tau(l)^2*sqrt(l)+(N/l)*gcd(A,l).                      (17)
Poisson gives N/l times complete Kloosterman sums. The zero frequency
is the displayed Ramanujan term. For the other frequencies use Weil,
sqrt(gcd(k,A,l))<=sum_(d|gcd(A,l),d|k)sqrt(d), and
sum_(k!=0,d|k)(1+|k|/V)^(-2)<=2V/d. This proves (17) for every A.

Cauchy on the actual E1 sum, with its original unconstrained r,s support,
reduces the phase to A=a*(h*r'-h'*r), l=q*r*r'. On primitive moduli
gcd(A,l)=gcd(h*r'-h'*r,l). Write j=h*r'-h'*r. The j=0 tuples number
O(R*H*x^o(1)); no tau(0) is used. For j!=0,
sum_(q~Q)gcd(j,q)<=3Q*tau(|j|),
sum_(|h|<=H,h!=0)gcd(h,r)<=2H*tau(r),
sum_(r,r'~R)gcd(r,r')^2<<R^3.
Factoring gcd(r,r') in j shows that these bound the Ramanujan term.
The complete result, for H=O(QR/M*log(x)^5), is
 |Z_E1|^2<<x^epsilon*N^4/Q^2
                 *[NQ/x+NQ^(5/2)R^3/x^2+N^2*Q*R/x^2].    (18)
Every epsilon here is freely chosen before x; no log remainder is involved.

For fixed d use Q'=d*Q1, R=Rcal and restrict q' to multiples of d in
the permitted bounded q'-dependent coefficient. This substitution is
allowed in E1 only. Absorb Lambda's fixed divisor bound at this power
stage. All three brackets have a strict power gap from (1). Explicitly,
 n+q<1-9sigma, 2n+q+u+v<17/10-29sigma/2,
 n+(5/2)q+3u+3v
  =(35/16)(q-n)+(23/16)(2n+u+2v)
     +(5/16)(n+q+5u+2v)-v/2
  <1021/512-(147/8)sigma+O(1/log x).                      (19)
The final line uses |a|>=c0*x and theta=7/32. All exponents of D cost
only genuine vanishing subpowers. Formula (18) therefore gives, with
spare power, |E1(d)|<<N^2/(d*Q1)*x^(-sigma+o(1)). Summing all d in
the dyadic block uses sum_d 1/d=O(1); then pay one D=x^o(1) to get
                  |E1|<<N^2/(D*Q1)*x^(-sigma/2).          (20)

7. E2: the exact phase, coefficient class and spectral return

For fixed d the actual normalization is E2=sum_(d~D)E3(d)/d, and
E3 has denominator q*w1*w2, with w_i~Rcal. Its two phases satisfy
 e(ah*inverse(n1*w2)/(q*d*w1)+ah*inverse(n2*q*d*w1)/w2)
  =e(ah*(n1-n2)*inverse(n2*q*d*w1)/(n1*w2))
                 *e(ah/(q*d*w1*w2*n1)).                  (21)
All inverses are licensed by the clean CRT coprimalities. For
H=QD*Rcal^2*log(x)^5/M, the last phase has normalized amplitude
(a/x)*(Q/q0)*(D/d)*log(x)^5 on each q0~Q block. Five-coordinate
partial summation, in q,w1,w2,n1,h, consequently costs at most
                         x^(5kappa+o(1)).                (22)
This charge is paid once. The resulting endpoints are frozen scalars,
and the h weight is independent of all remaining summation variables.
The smooth q support is split into a bounded number of common intervals;
there is no arbitrary q weight in the resulting bare sum.

Partition the rough coprime pairs (n1*w2,n2) by the named coprime-coloring
lemma into exp(O((loglog x)^4)) cross-coprime classes. Expand both copies
of Lambda_d independently, including both st=d choices. Put S=rough Q2
length, R=rough Q3 length. Product windows on w1,w2 are differences of
two product upper cutoffs. Non-Hermitian polarization makes the two
R, the two S and the two n coefficients diagonal at cost at most 4^3.
No SW is reapplied after polarization. The fixed signed h interval
still supplies only one scalar one-bounded coefficient omega_h.

The bare sum is reduced by f=(n1-n2)/(dq), then positive Cauchy, to a
smooth reciprocal kernel with outside factor
                         P=N/(Q*R*S^(3/2)),
up to subpowers, and variables b=n2*r1, c=n1*r2*s2. Only after Cauchy
is f enlarged to O(N/Q); a favorable d^(-1/2) is dropped. Fixed smooth
majorants remove the b,c arithmetic coefficients at a divisor cost.
After squaring, the kernel coefficient is dominated by the positive
count of z=s1*s2, y=f*(h1*s1-h2*s2)!=0; (a,z)=1 is retained.
A unit phase for each y,z aligns the kernel, leaving no dependence on
the future completed m variable. The separate zero difference costs
x^epsilon*H*S*Bspec*F*Cspec.

For nu>=1 its actual counting norm obeys
 B_nu^2<=x^epsilon*(1+H/S)*(HS)^2*F/nu.                    (23)
Indeed fix z,y and use Cauchy over the divisor-bounded decompositions
(s1,s2,f,k); then count colliding h pairs with nonzero k. Equal pairs
cost (HS)^2*F/nu. For unequal pairs put g=gcd(s1,s2), s1=g*u,s2=g*v;
the differences are m1=j*v,m2=j*u with nonzero |j|<=2Hg/S. Nonzero
multiples k/g of nu/(c*gcd(g,nu/c)), c|nu, number O(HSc*gcd(g,nu/c)/(g*nu)).
Factoring the remaining nonzero h2*v costs only a divisor factor.
The total unequal contribution is
x^epsilon*H^3*S*F/nu*sum_(g<=2S)gcd(g,nu)/g,
and that sum is <=tau(nu)*(1+log(2S)). This proves (23). Counting
nonzero multiples from zero has no +1. Beyond the nonzero y support
B_nu=0. The actual H/S<=x^o(1)*(Q/N)*D0<1, D0=N^2*R^2*S/x.

For completeness the exact spectral return used at this point is
written here, to avoid assuming the desired bare L51 estimate. At generic
scales C,D,U,Z with (a,z)=1, Poisson in b gives
 (D/c)*sum_m psihat(mD/c)*S(m*inverse(z),a*y;c).
The m=0 term contributes D^2*U*Z*B_all^2 to the squared bound. Truncate
nonzero m at the GLOBAL cutoff (C/D)*x^epsilon, split common dyadic m
intervals, and separate the smooth amplitude on the fixed normalized
coordinates. The change c/C=sqrt((m/M)*(y/U)/(z/Z))/t cancels a exactly;
its Fourier norm costs a selectable small power only. It leaves a common
m interval and a common additive phase at every level z.

At level z and cusps infinity,1 the phase conversion is e(-a*y/z).
The coprime normalized ABL packet has transformed coefficient
 beta_nu(n,z)=1_((n,a)=1)*beta(nu*n,z)*e(-n/z), cost (|a|nu)^(theta/2).
The transformation is common to every oldform dilation, since (a,z)=1.
The continuous part uses its formal Plancherel quadratic form, not an
L2 vector for one Eisenstein series. The regular cumulative large sieve,
fixed-level exceptional estimate F and common-interval exceptional J
listed in section 10 therefore apply with these precise coefficients.

Their algebra yields, with actual common m scale V,
 |L_V|^2<<x^epsilon*P0*{
   sum_nu (|a|nu)^theta*(U/nu+Z+T0)*(V+Z+T0)*B_nu^2
       +(C^2*Z)^theta*(U+Z)^(1-theta)*(V+Z)^(1-2theta)*B_all^2},
 P0=V*C^4*Z/(|a|*V*U+C^2*Z), T0=|a|*V*U/C^2.             (24)
Here the regular transform is summed over heights max(1,2Y0), then
dyadic shells: its high-height bounds t^(-5/2)+Y0*t^(-3) multiplied
by the two cumulative norm factors grow as 2^(-j/2)+2^(-j), a convergent
sum. The level Cauchy costs sqrt(Z), not Z. For exceptional forms use
1+X^tau<=2*(1+X/sqrt(X0))^tau*(1+X0)^(tau/2), X0=1+Z/(U/nu).
The packet power cancels |a|nu only in the improved exceptional term.
These operations give exactly (24) from the stated classical norms.

Multiplying by (D/C)^2 and using
 Z*(L+Z+T0)*(V+Z+T0)/(Z+T0)<=(L+Z)*(V+Z)+Z*T0
for L=U/nu, V<=C/D, gives the returned squared polynomial
 sum_nu (|a|nu)^theta*[C*(U/nu+Z)*(C+D*Z)+|a|*U*Z]*B_nu^2
 +[C*(C*D*sqrt(Z))^(2theta)*(U+Z)^(1-theta)*(C+D*Z)^(1-2theta)
                                   +D^2*U*Z]*B_all^2.     (25)
The actual cutoff V<=x^epsilon*C/D costs at most x^(2epsilon) here.
It is valid also when C/D<1/2: that real value is only a comparison
point, and spectral estimates are invoked on nonempty actual intervals.

Now substitute Bspec=N*R, Cspec=N*R*S, Z=S^2,
U=N*R^2*S^3/M, F=N/Q, H=x^o(1)*Q*R^2*S^2/M, D=Bspec.
Apply (25) first on actual dyadic blocks and then enlarge positive
scales. Since U/Z=D0<1, Bspec*Z>=Cspec, Bspec^2<x, and
Cspec*Bspec*Z=x*U, equations (23)--(25) and the two square roots give
 |W_bare|<<N^2/Q*x^epsilon*
     {D0^(1/2)+[(1+|a|/x)*D1+D2]^(1/4)},                  (26)
 D1=|a|^theta*N*Q*R^2*S^5/x^2,

Its logarithm is O((log x)^theta) by splitting primes at log x.
Thus neither nu nor the zero Poisson mode was silently dropped.
