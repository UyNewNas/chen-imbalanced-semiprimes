Rosser raw-density primary-source interface check (2026-10-09)

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
This checks the classical density input in own-lower-compiler-chain-audit-20261009.tex, not the prime-distribution theorem or the complete Chen argument. The convergence hypothesis is discharged for kappa=1, beta=2. The actual cubic raw weights have the needed density equality on compact s>=2; the project's separate support-invariance argument handles upper 1<s<2. A citation qualification is necessary: Motohashi's K_nu includes presieving and must not, by itself, be identified with the unpresieved raw density. The Suzuki route below supplies the missing literal identification. No remaining gap was found in this narrowly checked classical interface.

1. The convergence premise is genuinely discharged
--------------------------------------------------
Locations: S pp18,22,31,33,36,60--65,68--69; equations (9.1)--(9.2), Propositions 9.4, 10.6, 10.8(ii), 10.14, 11.4 and 11.8.

Prop 9.4 is conditional, not an independent convergence proof. Prop 11.8 requires beta in J and beta>rho_tilde. For kappa=1, J=(1,infinity), q(s)=r_(1,1)(s)=s-1, rho=1. Meanwhile p(s)=r_(1,-1)(s) has no positive zero (10.14) and is eventually positive (10.6); hence p(1)>0. By the definition on p60,

  D(2)=p(1)q(2)+q(1)p(2)=p(1)>0.

The criterion on p62 is D(s)>0 iff s>rho_tilde for s in J intersect (rho,infinity). Consequently 2>rho_tilde strictly. Merely quoting rho_tilde<=rho+1 would not suffice. Prop 11.8 therefore gives convergence on I+=(1,infinity), I-=[2,infinity); Prop 9.4 then gives compact uniform convergence. Its proof is noncircular: auxiliary DDE solutions dominate finite partial sums in (11.11), and convergence is concluded on p68 before the remaining properties are established.

2. Matching the actual weights and obtaining equality
----------------------------------------------------
Locations: S pp8--10,13,71,79,97--98; (6.1), Lemmas 6.1--6.2, Definition 8.2, Lemma 14.7 and (14.29). Section 12 fixes beta=rho+1, which is exactly 2 here.

With d=p1...pr in decreasing prime order, (6.1) rejects p1...pj*p_j^2>=R at odd j for upper weights and even j for lower weights. Thus its lambda=mu*sigma is the same raw cubic support used by the compiler, not an existential optimized weight. The project's extra lower condition p1^2<R is automatic for p1<y and s=log R/log y>=2. Section 6's standing s>=beta means this check only invokes the raw lemma for s>=2.

The density hypothesis is 0<=g(p)<1 and
  product_[w<=p<z](1-g(p))^(-1) <= (log z/log w)*(1+K/log w)
with a fixed K>=2. For g0(2)=0, g0(p)=1/(p-1) at odd p, Mertens gives this. Deleting primes dividing the even target preserves the same upper product bound.

Lemma 14.7 is one-sided. At kappa=1 one may, for example, fix Delta=1/2 and the auxiliary exponent d=15 (>14); on compact s>=2 its error is o(1). Its actual statements supply
  V0^+/V0 <= F+o(1),    V0^-/V0 >= f-o(1).
Do not subtract two instances of these inequalities as if they were equalities.

Instead use the exact first-exit identity (14.29). Each exit term is positive. Fixed-depth prime harmonic summation for g0 gives the limits a_j(s) from (9.1)--(9.2). For every fixed J,
  liminf V0^+/V0 >= 1+sum_(odd j<=J) a_j(s),
  limsup V0^-/V0 <= 1-sum_(even j<=J) a_j(s).
Send J to infinity using the convergence already proved in part 1. Combined with Lemma 14.7 these give both equalities, compactly on the stated domains. This proves, rather than assumes, that the function used in subtree subtraction is the actual raw density.

For moving N the compiler's positive-exit deletion comparison is still needed for the reverse limits. A surviving prefix term has ratio to its g0 counterpart equal to
  product_[p_last<=p<y, p|N](1-g0(p)) <= 1.
For each fixed depth p_last is a fixed positive power of N, so this factor tends uniformly to 1 and the removed finite-depth prime mass tends to zero. Fixed-depth limits followed by the convergent positive series complete the uniform argument. This is a project reduction, not an extra statement attributed to either book.

3. Normalization and the exact limitation of Motohashi
-----------------------------------------------------
Locations: M pp58,60--61,71,75,78 (PDF pp70,72--73,83,87,90).
Equation (3.1.10) defines
  V(z) K_nu(y,z;delta) = V(z1) sum_[d|P(z1,z)] mu(d) rho_nu(d) delta(d)/d.
Thus this is a presieved density. The proof in Section 3.3 establishes its limit, rather than leaving the heuristic (3.1.13) as an assumption. Its hypotheses are (3.1.1), (3.1.2), L=O(log y/(log log y)^5), with z1=exp(log y/(log log y)^2). The displayed conclusion on p78 is uniform for bounded s>=2. Theorem 9 gives a count inequality, not a new identity for unpresieved weights.

Equation (3.2.10) supplies the standard normalization: sF(s)=2 exp(gamma) initially, f(s)=0 initially, and (sF)'=f(s-1), (sf)'=F(s-1). This can be used independently of the K_nu identification: Suzuki gives B=0 at beta=2 and a common initial constant A, so linearity of the DDE makes both solutions scalar multiples of these normalized functions. Both tend to 1 at infinity; hence A=2 exp(gamma). Therefore F=2 exp(gamma)*Fbar and f=2 exp(gamma)*fbar in the compiler's notation.

4. Low s is an algebraic support extension, not a broader citation
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For an upper raw weight every supported nonunit has p1^3<R. Thus, if 1<s<=3, replacing cutoff y=R^(1/s) by R^(1/3) leaves exactly the same supported coefficients. Mertens, uniformly after the target-prime deletion argument, gives
  V_N(R^(1/3))/V_N(y) -> 3/s.
The already justified equality at s=3 yields V_N^+(R,y)/V_N(y)->(3/s)F(3)=2 exp(gamma)/s. No lower density below s=2 is claimed or needed. Marked prefix primes are at least the residual cutoff, and are coprime to N; they therefore do not add a missing factor to the strictly smaller residual-prime product.
