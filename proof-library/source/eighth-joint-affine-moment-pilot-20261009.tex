\subsection{A common-affine AM--GM moment bound}
\label{sec:eighth-joint-affine-moment-pilot-20261009}

Let $T$ be a physical eight-simplex, let $g\ge0$ be affine with
$\int_Tg>0$, and let $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{10}$ be strictly positive
affine factors on its closure. All these conditions hold for the
frozen unequal-parent winner9 seed used in this experiment. Put
\[
 d\nu=\frac{g\,dt}{\int_Tg},\qquad
 m_j=E_\nu\ell_j,\qquad a_j=\ell_j/m_j,
 \qquad P=\frac1{10}\sum_{j=1}^{10}a_j.
\]
Thus $E_\nu P=1$. The ordinary weighted Jensen bound for the density
$4g/\prod_j\ell_j$ is
\[
 J_T=\frac{4\int_Tg}{\prod_jm_j}.
\]

\paragraph{Direction, expansion and positive remainder.}
AM--GM gives $\prod_ja_j\le P^{10}$, so the reciprocal direction is
\[
 \prod_j\ell_j^{-1}\ge(\prod_jm_j)^{-1}P^{-10}.
\]
Let $U=\max_{v\in\operatorname{Vert}(T)}P(v)\ge1$ and
$Y=1-P/U$. Positivity and affinity give $0\le Y\le q<1$ throughout
the closed simplex. The binomial series converges uniformly there.
Writing $\mu_Y=E_\nu Y=1-1/U$ and $c_k=\binom{9+k}{k}$,
\[
 E_\nu P^{-10}-1
 =U^{-10}\sum_{k\ge2}c_k
       \bigl(E_\nu Y^k-\mu_Y^k\bigr).
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed $U^{-10}(1-\mu_Y)^{-10}=1$; the constant and linear terms
cancel exactly. Every summand is nonnegative by convexity of $y^k$.
For any fixed integer $p\ge2$ define the finite rational quantity
\[
 T_p=U^{-10}\sum_{k=2}^pc_k
       \bigl(E_\nu Y^k-\mu_Y^k\bigr)\ge0.
 \tag{2}
\]
No estimate of an alternating or signed remainder is required. We obtain
\[
 4\int_T\frac{g}{\prod_j\ell_j}\ge J_T(1+T_p).
 \tag{3}
\]
This conclusion does not assume independence of the factors. It is a
different lower bound from the existing factorwise log6--exp3 bound;
the two increments cannot simply be added.

\paragraph{Exact gap-weighted moments and integer protocol.}
Write the nine vertex gaps as $g_i$, with $G=\sum_i g_i>0$.
The barycentric law under $\nu$ is the mixture with weights $g_i/G$
of Dirichlet laws having parameter two at vertex $i$ and one at the
other eight vertices. For an affine $Y$ with vertex values $y_i$,
\[
 E_\nu Y^k=
 \frac{\sum_i g_i h_k(y_0,\ldots,y_8,y_i)}
      {G\binom{9+k}{k}},
 \tag{4}
\]
where $h_k$ is complete homogeneous. Individual $g_i=0$ are permitted.
This is the nine-vertex, total-parameter-ten formula.

For the implementation, scale the gaps to nonnegative integers $g_i$.
For factor $j$ scale its vertex values to integers $l_{ji}$ with common
denominator $d_j$, and set
\[
 n_j=G\sum_i l_{ji}+\sum_i g_i l_{ji},\qquad
 m_j=\frac{n_j}{10Gd_j}.
\]
Taking $L=\operatorname{lcm}_j n_j$, the exact vertex values of $P$ are
\[
 P_i=p_i/L,\qquad p_i=G\sum_j l_{ji}(L/n_j).
\]
The source checks the integer identity
$\sum_i(G+g_i)p_i=10GL$, which is precisely $E_\nu P=1$.
Let $B=\max_i p_i$ and $y_i=B-p_i$; now $Y_i=y_i/B$ and $U=B/L$.
The author implementation forms the nine-node coefficients of
$\prod_i(1-y_i z)^{-1}$ and convolves them with the weighted powers
$\sum_i g_i y_i^k$. This equals the duplicated-node numerator in (4)
without calling the independent Newton-identity implementation.

All quantities in (2) are exact rational numbers. The stored protocol is
\[
 t_p=2^{-80}\lfloor2^{80}T_p\rfloor,\qquad
 b_p=2^{-96}\lfloor2^{96}J_T(1+t_p)\rfloor.
\]
Given the current certified log bound $b_{\log}$ for this same simplex,
retain $\max(b_{\log},b_p)$ and record only
$\max(b_{\log},b_p)-b_{\log}$. The mean gaps, all ten means and the
exact old $J_T$ are reconstructed from the frozen physical vertices;
the old geometry and volume certificates are inputs, not newly replayed
by this author calculation.

\paragraph{Completed bounded experiment.}
An initial deterministic sample of128 evenly spaced simplex indices
tested degrees6 and10. Each improved four saved log floors; its
degree10 increment was approximately $1.5011\cdot10^{-14}$.
The subsequent complete run used degree10 on all $11{,}026$ original
winner9 seed simplices, once, and finished in31.88 seconds.
The current log6--exp3 total and the common-affine-only total are
respectively
\[
 B_{\log}=\frac{475182339168330434688559}{2^{96}},\qquad
 B_{P10}=\frac{471629548845801509674777}{2^{96}}.
\]
Thus the common-affine bound cannot replace the whole old sum.
Taking the maximum separately on every simplex improves304 terms and
gives
\[
 B_{\max}=\frac{237592771576150817599263}{2^{95}},\qquad
 B_{\max}-B_{\log}
 =\frac{3203983971200509967}{2^{96}}
 \simeq4.04399631334\cdot10^{-11}>0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Every candidate $T_p$ floor and every replacement floor is saved in the
new compressed witness, keyed by the original simplex index. This is
an author exact certificate, with a separate independent replay to be
attributed if completed. No new domain, cap, sieve weight, $K$, $c$ or
imbalance exponent is adopted by this experiment.

\paragraph{A structural limitation and an uncomputed possible refinement.}
The common-affine compression discards directions which cancel in the
sum of the normalized factors. For example, take
$(a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots,a_{10})=(1+t,1-t,1,\ldots,1)$ with
$E_\nu t=0$ and $|t|<1$. Then $P\equiv1$ and every $T_p=0$, while
the reciprocal product is $(1-t^2)^{-1}$ and the factorwise logarithmic
gap is positive unless $t$ is constant. Therefore no general dominance
over the log method follows, even with unlimited degree. Conversely,
if all $a_j$ coincide, AM--GM is exact; this explains why a common-mode
bound is mathematically distinct rather than redundant.

One possible next inequality, not evaluated or counted in (5), retains
the lost directions. Let
$V_j=\max_{v}\{a_j(v),P(v)\}$. The Hessian of $-\log x$ on the segment
from $P$ to $a_j$ is at least $V_j^{-2}$, so
\[
 -\log a_j+\log P+(a_j-P)/P
 \ge\frac{(a_j-P)^2}{2V_j^2}.
\]
The linear terms cancel pointwise when summed. Exponentiating and using
$e^z\ge1+z$ for the resulting nonnegative sum yields
\[
 E_\nu\prod_j a_j^{-1}
 \ge 1+T_p+
 U^{-10}\sum_j\frac{E_\nu(a_j-P)^2}{2V_j^2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The additional moments in (6) are only degree two. This is a new
pointwise-derived refinement of the common-affine bound, not permission
to add the old log increment. Its usefulness and actual numerical
gain remain untested in this report. The small gain in (5) alone does
not bound the potential of (6), other domains, or different estimates.
