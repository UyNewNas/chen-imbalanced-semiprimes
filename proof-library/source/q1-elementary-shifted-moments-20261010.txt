Elementary shifted divisor moments for three actual cleanup steps
10 October 2026. New local proof and parameter refinement; old files frozen.

Purpose and exact change

This note replaces the q=1 shifted-moment use of Shiu's short-interval
theorem in three places of the current restricted prime-product proof:
beta-only truncation, the geometric Fourier tail, and the boundary/freezing
cleanup for two long variables. It does NOT replace the q>1 progression
moment in preliminary dispersion, the spectral theorem, or the medium
convolution estimate. The global K,c,b and every numerical allowance stay
unchanged. The adopted context is positive-pair-deletion-improvement-
assembly-20261010.json, with K6.7617368,c1.96131568,b.74821909.

The price is an additional fixed upper bound on the moving-residue exponent
kappa. This is permissible for the actual near-target application after a
correspondingly narrower complete-product trim. It is not a proof of an
old statement for all polynomially large shifts. The improvement is a
smaller analytic dependency boundary, not a new distribution exponent.

1. An elementary short-interval divisor bound

For integer k>=2, let d_k(n) be the number of ordered k-tuples of positive
integers with product n. If I is an interval in [1,Z], Z>=2, of length Y,
then

 sum_(n in I) d_k(n)
 <= k*[Y*(1+log Z)^(k-1)+Z^(1-1/k)*(1+log Z)^(k-2)].       (E1)

Proof. For every ordered tuple choose a largest coordinate (use the first
one in a tie). If its value is v and the product of the other coordinates
is d, then v>=n^(1/k), so d<=n^(1-1/k)<=D=Z^(1-1/k).
There are at most k choices of its position. For each ordered remaining
tuple of product d, the condition dv in I permits at most Y/d+1 integers
v. Dropping the requirement that v be largest only increases the count.
Therefore the left side is at most

 k*sum_(d<=D) d_(k-1)(d)*(Y/d+1).

The elementary product expansions give, for j>=1,

 sum_(d<=D) d_j(d)/d <= (1+log D)^j,
 sum_(d<=D) d_j(d) <= D*(1+log D)^(j-1).

For the second inequality, sum the last factor with floor(D/product)
and then use the first inequality for the other j-1 factors; j=1 is
immediate. Substitution proves (E1). All endpoints and locations of I
are allowed; no coprimality condition or arithmetic progression is used.

For any nonnegative integer e,
                 tau(n)^e <= d_(2^e)(n).                  (E2)
One prime-power proof: an e-tuple of integers in {0,...,v} determines,
for each position 1,...,v, the subset of coordinates reaching that
position. Its histogram is a weak composition of v into 2^e parts and
determines the original tuple. This injects (v+1)^e possibilities into
the binomial coefficient d_(2^e)(p^v). Multiply over prime powers.
For e=0 use d_1=1. Increasing k can only increase d_k(n), by appending
unit coordinates.

2. A uniform mixed moment at a near-linear moving shift

Fix integers R,S>=0, C>=1, and set
                 K=2^(2*max(R,S,1)).
Fix 0<=kappa<=1/(2K). Uniformly for real x>=2, integer a with
|a|<=C*x^(1+kappa), and any interval J in [1,C*x], one has

 sum_(b in J,b!=a) tau(b)^R*tau(|b-a|)^S
                    <<_(C,R,S) x*(1+log x)^(K-1).          (E3)

Proof. The nonzero integers |b-a| lie, with multiplicity at most two,
in at most two positive intervals, each of length at most C*x and with
upper endpoints at most C'*x^(1+kappa). By (E1),(E2),

 sum_(b in J,b!=a) tau(|b-a|)^(2S)
 << x*(1+log x)^(K-1)
       + x^((1+kappa)*(1-1/K))*(1+log x)^(K-2).

The exponent satisfies

 (1+kappa)*(1-1/K)
 <= 1-1/(2K)-1/(2K^2) < 1-1/(2K).                        (E4)

Thus this second moment is O(x*(1+log x)^(K-1)). The unshifted
second moment is bounded in the same way, or directly from
sum_(b<=Cx)d_K(b). Cauchy--Schwarz proves (E3). For negative a
there may be only one image interval; the same bound applies. Deleting
b=a BEFORE using tau is essential. No tau(0) expression occurs.

The logarithmic exponent K-1 is deliberately very coarse but fixed.
Only its independence of the requested accuracy matters. This proof is
uniform in the positions and endpoints of all intervals and in a within
the stated range. An extra fixed logarithmic coefficient factor is
handled by multiplying the right side by that same logarithmic factor.

3. The actual three replacements, with every loss in its proper place

(i) Beta-only truncation. In lichtman-medium-rough-sw-explicit-20261010.txt,
(M4)--(M5), the discarded nonzero products are bounded by

 (log x)^(-Ccut)*sum_(b~x,b!=T)
                   tau(b)^(B_beta+2)*tau(|b-T|)^(J0+1).

Apply (E3) with R=B_beta+2 and S=J0+1. Both orders are fixed before
Ccut; B_beta depends on the finite HB slot count and J0 on the actual
modulus coefficient class. The result is x*(log x)^(E-Ccut), where E
is independent of Ccut. Choose Ccut after E and the requested accuracy.
The retained beta normalization, unchanged alpha SW, and exact coprime
mean argument remain precisely as before. At b=T keep the separate
Dtot*x^o(1) first-moment bound. Neither (E3) nor the proof of (M5)
licenses deleting nm=T from alpha/beta before invoking distribution.

(ii) Geometric Fourier tail. In lichtman-mellin-separation-explicit-
20261010.txt, section6, the total modulus mass for a nonzero complete
product v-T is bounded by a fixed power tau(|v-T|)^(J+1). Collapsing
the finitely many HB variables costs a fixed power tau(v)^C0. Apply
(E3) with those R,S to obtain x*log^E x. Multiply by the pointwise
Fourier-tail error only AFTER this complete modulus bound. A larger
fixed frequency cutoff power makes the error arbitrarily log-small.
The exact v=T term retains its T^(theta_star+o(1)) bound. The mean
still uses the reciprocal-totient divisor sum, not a factor T^theta_star.
The integral Fourier norm and the separate retained arithmetic phase
are unchanged; no high-frequency arithmetic hypothesis is added.

(iii) Two-long roughness and interval cleanup. In lichtman-hb-long-
branches-20261009.txt, section5, the boundary set has

 #E << x*log^(1-B) x + (x/U)*y^O(1),

with U>=x^nu for fixed nu>0 and y=x^(1/loglog x). The outer Cauchy
step requires a complete sum

 sum_all_tuples |weight(tuple)|^2*tau(|b-T|)^(2J), b!=T.

After collapsing tuples the b-weight is bounded by tau(b)^R for a
fixed R, times a fixed log power. Use (E3) with S=2J, NOT S=J.
Its proof therefore needs d_K to dominate tau^(4J). This second
doubling is included in the common K below. The resulting boundary
error is

 << x*log^((E+1-B)/2) x + x^(1-nu/2+o(1)),

which has arbitrary logarithmic saving for sufficiently large fixed B.
For small-divisor freezing, (E3) gives the same x*log^E x absolute
mass, so a sufficiently narrow fixed-logarithmic bin pays the smooth
variation. Exact b=T, the fundamental-lemma comparison and the mean
correction keep their previous separate estimates. The subsequent
two-smooth-variable Poisson/Weil estimate is unchanged.

4. Compatible order of fixed parameters

First retain the existing coarse cap policy, density tolerances, positive
licence reserves, a_star,k_star,zeta,sigma, beta precision/rough cutoff,
finite rough rank J and finite modulus atoms. Their construction may use
the old preliminary choices kappa_old=sigma/200 and kappa0_old. Once
those objects and all actual fixed divisor orders are fixed, form the
FINITE list of mixed moments needed in (i)--(iii). Include the squared
orders after the outer boundary-strip Cauchy. Put

 K_mom=max_over_list 2^(2*max(R_i,S_i,1)).

It is a fixed integer; it need not be small or effective for the final
asymptotic assertion. No Ccut, requested log accuracy or target value
is used in selecting these divisor orders. The triples of one-bounded
modulus factors already give a fixed divisor order locally; an optional
larger finite-rank bound globally remains legitimate.

Now replace only the auxiliary exponents by positive rational choices

 kappa_new <= min(kappa_old,1/(2*K_mom)),
 kappa0_new < min(kappa0_old,kappa_new/[4*(1+kappa_new)]).     (E5)

Do NOT shrink zeta after choosing the small-prime precision. There is
no need to change sigma,zeta,omega,J, the existing fine mesh, or any
outer density. The other bounds on kappa and kappa0 are upper bounds:
smaller values reduce local-coordinate and large-residue costs. In
particular the E2 retained-phase charge5*kappa decreases, all four
actual medium inequalities retain their margins, and the complete
support below3/5 and the below-half low-rank gaps improve.

Use the new complete-product trim at T^(1-kappa0_new). Its discarded
count is still T^(1-kappa0_new+o(1))=o(T/log^A T) for every fixed A.
Although this is a smaller power saving, kappa0_new is positive and
fixed. On retained complete scales c*T^(1-kappa0_new)<=x<=C*T,
one eventually has c0*x<=T<=x^(1+kappa_new), including fixed chart
constants. Thus (E3) applies to the ACTUAL shift T. No quotient scale
is substituted for the complete product scale.

Finally choose spectral/collision/divisor small epsilons below the new
positive minimum, choose the fixed logarithmic precision/truncations,
and take the uniform eventual target threshold. This modifies the
example hierarchy in the old common-parameter note, where a particular
ratio kappa=sigma/200 was frozen; it does not claim literal preservation
of that equality. Its downstream inequalities use only the upper bound.
The construction avoids a rough-rank circularity by reducing kappa and
kappa0 AFTER rank-derived divisor orders, while leaving zeta and the
already selected rough precision unchanged.

5. Dependency boundary after this substitution

The three named q=1 cleanup uses now require only the explicit elementary
divisor factorization count above, ordinary divisor/totient means, and
their already separate Fourier, fundamental-lemma, or Poisson inputs.
No Shiu theorem is needed for these particular shifted moments.

The preliminary progression estimate Z2(q), its Gram/dispersion uses,
and any source claim for shifts throughout an arbitrary polynomial
range are NOT replaced. Shiu therefore remains among the project's
overall external inputs. The spectral packet/continuous-spectrum
inputs, pointwise same-rho sieve construction, raw densities, finite
table inequalities and numerical certificates remain their separate
obligations. This local substitution is not an independent reproof
of the full b=.74821909 theorem and does not restore unrestricted L23.

All current constants, old numerical allowances and strict final
surpluses are retained; only a fixed eventual threshold may increase.
The open manuscript is unchanged. Formalization, GitHub and external
communications remain paused/not authorized for this continuation.
