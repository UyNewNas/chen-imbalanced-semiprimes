HB one-long and two-long branches at the moving target
2026-10-09. New analytic reconstruction; no change to frozen sources or constants.

Conclusion

For the actual complete-integer scales and fixed finite families, both long
branches can be justified without the disputed L23 Lemma4.6 phase deletion
or its medium-slot spectral estimate. The smoothed two-long core in Maynard
II Lemma7.1 is genuinely uniform in every primitive residue. Its passage
to rough, interval-restricted variables needs a separate moving-shift check:
the old smoothing proof uses divisors of a product minus a and cannot be
quoted literally at product=a. The nonzero shifted-moment estimate and an
isolated first-moment correction give that check below.

I use a slightly stronger, explicitly sufficient modulus inequality than
the printed two-long proposition, so that its proof and frequency losses
are fully covered. This costs no actual project density: the present level
is strictly below3/5, whereas this branch has a2/3 threshold. This note is
not a reproof of the medium-slot theorem, of the whole compiler, or of the
current global K,c,b claims.

2. Actual variables and the exhaustive HB alternative

Write T for the even target, x for the complete distributed integer scale,
and a=T. For an ordinary prime family the distributed integer is the whole
prime P; a marked prefix belongs to its modulus. For a switched family it
is the whole product of original prime factors. A quotient after removing
a marked factor is not the x used here. The retained power trim makes
|a|<=x^K for a fixed K; these long branches need no sharper a/x bound.

First fix an ORIGINAL prime chart p_i in[R_i,2R_i], put X=product R_i,
Xplus=2^k X and C_k=2^(6*k_star+2), and fix
Zchart=f(C_k*Xplus), f(u)=u^(1/(log log u)^3). This choice is made BEFORE
HB and is not changed separately in different HB terms.
Apply order-three HB separately in each original prime group. Retain only
dyadic rectangles meeting that group's original interval. A group's at most
six endpoint scales have product at least its original scale divided by64.
The raw HB scale x_HB=product U_j lies in[X/64^k,Xplus]. The later source
scale x=U*V, after a complementary dyadic convolution subdivision, can
differ by another fixed factor; it remains comparable to X and at most
C_k*Xplus. Thus the larger chart buffer covers these later source scales
too. For each fixed k_star and sufficiently large x,

                 f(x)<=Zchart<=x^(2/(log log x)^3).           (L0)

Every original prime is at least T^a_star for fixed a_star>0, so the original
roughness condition at Zchart is automatic and may be retained before
expansion. Every nonunit HB factor has this SAME roughness; units are
separate singleton factors. The Mobius factors are bounded by a fixed
multiple of X^(1/3), so they cannot be long in the alternative below.

Choose zeta>0 sufficiently small compared with a_star. Classify medium
slots on the original chart using the narrower window
[X^(3zeta),X^(1/3+zeta/4)]. On any constant-comparable local x this is inside
[x^(2zeta),x^(1/3+zeta/2)] eventually. If no original-chart medium slot
exists, every slot is short, below X^(3zeta), or long, above
X^(1/3+zeta/4). A group with only short slots has product at most
X^(18zeta+o(1)), incompatible with its retained original scale if
18zeta<a_star/2. Thus every group has a long slot. Three long slots would
have product>X^(1+3zeta/4), incompatible with the complete dyadic product
O(X). There are at most two groups. Every long scale is at least
x^(1/3+zeta/8) eventually, with a reserved constant-scale buffer.

With one long slot, the short product is at most X^(15zeta+o(1)) and hence
at most x^(16zeta). With two long slots there are at most ten short slots,
so the corresponding bound is x^(32zeta). Below I use the looser bounds
M1<=x^(20zeta), M2<=x^(40zeta). Equivalently the old one/two-long formulas
with parameter zeta_L apply with zeta_L=2zeta; the independent lower bound
on each long scale is the one just displayed. Long slots have coefficient1,
apart from the retained logarithm and separable smooth weights. They are
not arbitrary arithmetic sequences. This argument is independent of the
number of groups in other HB branches.

3. One long slot: a primitive progression sieve with the exact mean

Let the long scale be V, and assume V>=q*x^gamma for some fixed gamma>0.
For a fixed short product m, if (m,q)>1, both terms of the desired
discrepancy vanish because (a,q)=1. Otherwise replace the residue by
b=a*m^(-1) modulo q. Only its primitive residue class is used; the absolute
size of a or b does not enter the following sieve.

Here is a direct uniform justification of L23 Lemma3.4 in the needed range.
Sieve the progression n=b mod q, n<=t with t comparable to V, by primes
below z=Zchart not dividing q, using sieve-divisor level Y=x^(gamma/4). The local
density is1/p for p not dividing q and0 otherwise. The dimension-one
fundamental lemma is uniform for this deletion of primes. Its parameter
log Y/log z>=(gamma/8)(log log x)^3 tends to infinity sufficiently fast to
give every logarithmic saving. Since each sieving congruence has remainder
O(1), the remainder from one-bounded sieve weights supported below Y is
O(Y), smaller than (t/q)*x^(-gamma/2) for large x. Thus the progression has
main density

 (t/q) V(z)*product_{p|q,p<z}(1-1/p)^(-1),
 V(z)=product_{p<z}(1-1/p),

with O_A(t/(q log^A x)) error. This differs from t*V(z)/phi(q) only through
primes p|q with p>=z. The difference is bounded by
(t/q)*log^O(1)x/z and hence is smaller than any target logarithmic error.
The analogous unsifted-in-q rough count has main term t*V(z) with the same
quality of error. This proves the source's unrestricted rough mean formula
in the required range, uniformly in the primitive residue.

The desired mean is instead the exact coprime sum. Its difference from
the printed mean is at most

 (1/phi(q))*sum_{p|q,p>=z}(t/p+1)
   << (t/q)*log^O(1)x/z + log^O(1)x/q
   <<_A t/(q log^A x).

The second inequality uses t>=q*x^gamma, and q/phi(q) and the number of
prime divisors are logarithmically bounded. The convention p>=z or p>z
is kept consistent with the chosen rough cutoff; an equality prime belongs
in the displayed deletion set. Modulus1 has zero discrepancy separately.

Apply the estimate at both endpoints of any subinterval of[V,2V]. No
minimum length of that subinterval is assumed: the error is in terms of V,
not its possibly small interval length. Partial summation multiplies the
error by the bounded variation of the normalized logarithm or Mellin twist.
For frequency |t_mellin|<=log^C x this is a fixed logarithmic power.

Finally sum over the short products and moduli. Fixed divisor bounds give
sum_{m~M}|beta_m|<<M log^O(1)x and sum_{q<=Q}|rho_q|/q<<log^O(1)x.
Since MV is comparable to x, the total error remains x/log^A x after
increasing the initial exponent. This reasoning does not multiply a
logarithmic estimate by a pointwise x^o(1) coefficient bound.

4. Two smooth long slots: the exact place where residue uniformity holds

Let U<=V be their scales, and Z the complementary scale, so ZUV is
comparable to x. For each primitive residue a mod q and each m with(m,q)=1,
the first Poisson step gives phases e(a*h1*(m*n2)^(-1)/q). Complete the
n2 sum in residue classes and apply Poisson a second time. The complete
nonzero-frequency sum is

 S(a*h1*m^(-1), h2; q),

an ordinary Kloosterman sum. Its common divisor factor is exactly
gcd(h1,h2,q), because both a and m are units modulo q. The h2=0 term
is a Ramanujan sum bounded by gcd(h1,q). There is no approximation of
a real phase, no Bezout remainder discarded, and no factor depending on
|a|. Maynard II Lemma7.1 explicitly takes the supremum over all primitive
residues and moduli in the dyadic block; the above calculation explains
why its general fixed-a convention causes no problem in this lemma.

To retain every power loss explicitly, select a small fixed eta>0 and
truncate at H1=q*x^eta/U, H2=q*x^eta/V. The smooth derivative bounds allow
a negligible Poisson tail, by choosing sufficiently many derivatives.
If a cutoff is below1 the corresponding nonzero sum is empty. The estimates

 sum_{0<|h|<=H}gcd(h,q) <= O(H*tau(q)),
 sum_{0<|h1|<=H1,0<|h2|<=H2}gcd(h1,h2,q)
       <= O(H1*H2*tau(q))

follow by summing over divisors of q. The complete Weil bound is even
slightly better than the latter use. The exact coprime main terms are
expanded with their Fourier masses retained, and cancel. The resulting
bound, for a one-bounded complementary coefficient, is

 E(q) << x^(1+eta+o(1))/(q*U) + x^(2eta+o(1))*Z*q^(1/2).       (L1)

Consequently, if U>=x^nu and
q<=(x/Z)^(2/3)*x^(-omega) with fixed nu,omega>0, choosing
eta<min(nu/4,omega/4) gives a fixed power saving relative to x/q.
The o(1) includes ordinary divisor factors and fixed logarithmic smoothness
costs. This is a POWER-saving core, so later fixed divisor-bounded grouped
coefficients and a fixed divisor-bounded rho_q can be bounded by x^o(1)
here. After summing q, a positive power reserve remains.

There is a minor parameter discrepancy worth keeping explicit. The printed
Maynard II Proposition5.2 assumes Q<=(x/M)^(2/3-3epsilon). Its final proof
line instead uses Q<=x^(2/3-3epsilon)/M^(2/3), which is stronger when M>1.
Also the displayed final bound in Lemma7.1 suppresses the x^epsilon factors
from its chosen frequency cutoffs. Formula(L1) records these factors
explicitly. Neither issue is an obstacle for this application: below I use
the stronger sufficient modulus bound with an independent, smaller eta,
rather than rely on either suppressed factor.

5. Roughness and intervals: the missing moving-shift details

The proof of Maynard II Proposition5.2 invokes its Lemma6.11 twice, which
in turn cites the full smoothing proof in Maynard I. Its printed cutoff
condition is z<=f(x). The original-chart cutoff in(L0) can be larger, so I
use the following proof-level extension rather than invoke that condition
literally. The proof uses upper and lower one-bounded fundamental-lemma
weights lambda_d^+/- supported on d<=y=x^(1/log log x). Uniformly in(L0),
log y/log Zchart>=(1/2)(log log x)^2. Thus the fundamental-lemma density
error is still smaller than every fixed inverse power of log x. The same
weights and smoothing proof apply, uniformly in this fixed-chart window.
For each variable its difference-of-means error is

 O_A(x/log^A x) + (x/U)*x^o(1),

where U is that long variable's scale. This follows directly from its
fundamental-lemma density gap and the coprime progression remainder
O(tau(q)); sum the fixed divisor-bounded complementary coefficients by
ordinary divisor moments, and sum rho_q/phi(q) logarithmically. No estimate
depending on a is needed for this mean error. Complex test coefficients
are split into finitely many nonnegative real components when using the
pointwise upper/lower sieve inequalities. This does not assume an order
on complex coefficients.

The source next smooths an arbitrary interval and freezes d in narrow
multiplicative ranges. Here its proof bounds divisors of mn'd-a. At
mn'd=a its displayed divisor bound is not available. The following
independent bounds supply the required moving-a extension.

First remove each exact complete-product equality b=a. At every one of
these stages the tuple multiplicity and coefficient mass at b are bounded
by a fixed power of tau(b), even after both auxiliary divisors are inserted.
The complete modulus support has Q<=x^(1-delta) for fixed delta>0. The
counting contribution at b=a is therefore Q*x^o(1), while its mean is
x^o(1)*sum_{q<=Q}|rho_q|/phi(q). Both are negligible. This argument applies
to the intermediate unrestricted variables; it does NOT use the oddness
of the final rough product to discard these intermediate terms.

For b!=a, the total absolute modulus contribution is bounded by
tau(|b-a|)^J+log^O(1)x for fixed J. Boundary intervals of relative width
log^(-B)x have elementary tuple count

 #E << x*log^(1-B)x + (x/U)*y^O(1).

For example, with one auxiliary divisor this is
sum_{d<=y} M*(U/(d log^B x)+O(1)); two auxiliary divisors only change the
fixed logarithmic powers and the y^O(1) term. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 sum_{tuple in E, b!=a} |weight(tuple)|*tau(|b-a|)^J
 <= (#E)^(1/2)
    *(sum_all_tuples |weight(tuple)|^2*tau(|b-a|)^(2J))^(1/2).

Collapsing the second sum to the COMPLETE b inserts only a fixed divisor
power. The reviewed Z2(q=1) bounds it by x*log^C x, uniformly for polynomial
|a|. Thus the strip error is

 << x*log^((C+1-B)/2)x + x^(1-nu/2+o(1)),

where U>=x^nu. This gives every logarithmic saving by choosing B large.
The exact coprime mean obeys the same bound by the reciprocal-totient sum.
This proof does not incorrectly discard the boundary restriction and then
claim that its small measure alone bounds a signed discrepancy.

Freezing d inside a multiplicative interval of relative width log^(-B1)x
changes a smooth factor by O(log^(C1-B1)x). The full nonzero absolute
discrepancy is x*log^C2 x by Z2 and fixed tuple multiplicity. Choose
B1>C1+C2+A and the error is acceptable; treat b=a as above. There are
only fixed logarithmic powers of resulting pieces. Choosing those powers
before taking x to infinity introduces no circular parameter dependence.

After two roughness removals, write n_i=d_i*n_i', with d_i<=y. Group
m,d1,d2 into a new complementary variable with scale Z=M*D1*D2=M*x^o(1).
Its coefficient is fixed-divisor-bounded, not necessarily one-bounded.
Its pointwise x^o(1) bound is applied only to the POWER-saving estimate(L1).
It is never multiplied into a remainder known only to be logarithmically
small. The long scales are N_i'=N_i*x^(-o(1)), so their positive exponent
and the modulus gap survive. This finishes the actual rough interval
extension, including both signs and all intermediate exact products.

6. Parameters sufficient for the project and weight uniformity

Let the complete local modulus ceiling be x^theta with fixed theta<3/5,
after the already reserved local-scale/compiler losses. Choose zeta>0
as in section2, and then a fixed epsilon>0 so small that

  theta < (2/3)*(1-40zeta)-4epsilon,
  3epsilon < 1/3+zeta/8,
  1-20zeta > theta+epsilon.

These choices are possible with a strict margin. For sufficiently large x,
the dyadic constants and x^o(1) factors fit inside smaller reserved margins.
The one-long condition then holds with some fixed gamma>0. In the two-long
case the stronger proof-compatible bound

  Q <= x^(2/3-3epsilon)/M^(2/3)

holds, and after d1,d2 grouping a fixed modulus power gap remains. Choose
eta much smaller than both the remaining long-scale exponent and that gap
in(L1). Every fixed logarithmic piece count and divisor normalization is
absorbed by its surviving power saving.

The rough interval result is uniform at both endpoints independently in
each long slot. Two partial summations therefore permit separate weights
with fixed polylogarithmic total variations. These include the HB logarithms
and retained Mellin frequencies. A finite smooth multivariate weight can
be treated by the existing separated Mellin representation with its fixed
derivative bounds. Its discarded Mellin tail is controlled by the same
nonzero Z2 absolute bound, with b=a isolated; it does not require a new
uniform SW statement. The short coefficient can retain its own interval
restriction, since no SW assumption is made on that coefficient.

This does not license an arbitrary arithmetic coefficient on either long
slot, an arbitrary unbounded-frequency twist, or a sharp coupled inequality
whose approximation error has not been controlled. For the actual smooth
outer weights and their separate long-slot intervals, the construction
above supplies the needed uniformity. Long variables themselves require
no SW input. All choices are fixed before the target grows.

7. Final scoped assessment

The one-long branch is a primitive progression sieve and exact-mean
comparison, uniformly in a. The two-long smooth branch is complete
Poisson/Weil, uniformly in a. Their rough/interval extension is uniform for
the required polynomial moving target after the nonzero-moment and
first-moment exact-product corrections just proved. The actual short-product
thresholds permit the stronger modulus inequalities, and no questioned
L23 phase deletion occurs in these branches.

What this leaves unresolved is the medium-slot distribution branch and
the independent verification that all actual signed atoms meet their stated
factorization/SW inputs, together with other separately identified global
issues. No global status, coefficient, exponent, or manuscript is changed
by this report. The quoted existing project closure labels are not used
as evidence for the new uniformity conclusion.
