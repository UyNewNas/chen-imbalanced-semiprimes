Conclusion

The required bound survives with no lower dyadic restriction on k:
  ||b_tilde(nu)||_2^2 << x^o(1) (HS)^2 F/nu,
uniformly for every positive integer nu on the actual polynomially bounded
support. In particular it holds for all nu|a^infinity. There is no omitted
large-nu '+1' or a small-|a| assumption in this counting step. Its essential
features are: k is NONZERO; h is NONZERO; signed nonzero frequency differences
are counted from zero, not in an unjustified dyadic shell; and the actual
source has H/S<1. A zero difference multiplier belongs to the separate equal
case. The divisor of nu below is b=gcd(d,nu/c), not the difference multiplier g.

This report reconstructs that argument, including constants before x^o(1),
and supplies bounded exact integer controls. It does not audit the spectral

2. The first Cauchy step does not cost F twice

At fixed (z,y), the number of decompositions (s_1,s_2,f,k) is at most
tau(z) tau(|y|)<=D_*^2: choose s_1|z and f||y|, then s_2,k are determined.
Let R(s_1,s_2;k) count its allowed h pairs. Cauchy gives

 N_nu <= D_*^2 sum_{s_1,s_2} sum_{k!=0} R(s_1,s_2;k)^2
                         * #{f in [F,2F): nu|fk}.          (1)

Thus the h collisions share the same s_1,s_2,k and f on the right, but this
is obtained by Cauchy, not by pretending the original two f values agree.
This explains why the final estimate has F, not F^2.
The printed (5.10) also displays (s_1,s_2)=1 without deriving it from the
previous unrestricted positive multiplicity. I do not use that restriction:
all s pairs remain here and their ACTUAL gcd is factored in section 4.
This supplies the general counting argument used by the later source lines.

For each k let c=nu/gcd(nu,k). Then c|nu, nu/c divides k, and
  #{f in [F,2F): c|f} <= 2F/c.
If c>=2F there is no such f for the half-open convention. We may sum over
all divisors c<=2F and impose only nu/c|k, discarding the additional gcd
condition. This overcounts positively.

The general counting fact used repeatedly is
  #{j in Z: 0<|j|<=L, m|j} = 2 floor(L/m) <= 2L/m.        (2)
This has no '+1'. If L<m the left side is exactly zero. Likewise the
number of positive multiples <=2F is at most 2F/c. These facts avoid the
source's unnecessary k asymp HS claim (lines 2227, 2237, 2242 and later).

3. Equal h pairs: m_1=m_2=0

For a collision put m_i=h_i-h_i'. Equality of k gives
  m_1 s_1=m_2 s_2.
If one m_i=0 both vanish. For fixed nonzero k, there are at most 4HS
choices of (h_1,s_1), and then h_2 s_2=h_1 s_1-k is a NONZERO integer of
size at most 2HS on actual solutions. Its signed factorization has at most
D_* choices. The potential value zero is excluded by h_2!=0; tau(0) is
neither used nor assigned a bound.

There are at most 8HS*c/nu nonzero k with |k|<4HS and nu/c|k. Consequently
the equal collision sum on the right of (1), before the D_*^2 factor, is
at most
  sum_{c|nu,c<=2F} (2F/c)(8HS*c/nu)(4HS D_*)
  <=64 D_* tau(nu) H^2 S^2 F/nu.                         (3)
This bound also covers nu larger than HS: only sufficiently large c can
make the multiple range nonempty, and (2) already records that fact.

4. Unequal h pairs: reduce the actual gcd before counting

Write d=gcd(s_1,s_2), s_1=d u, s_2=d v, gcd(u,v)=1. Then
S/d<=u,v<2S/d and d<2S. For unequal pairs the equation m_1u=m_2v gives
  m_1=g v, m_2=g u, with a SIGNED NONZERO integer g.
Since |m_i|<=2H,
  0<|g|<=2Hd/S.
There are at most 4Hd/S choices for g, again by (2). If this range has no
integer it is empty, not O(1). The source's H/g asymp S/d can therefore
be replaced by the one-sided inequality actually justified. It is not
necessary to assume H/g is bounded below by a comparable scale.

Put k_0=k/d=h_1u-h_2v!=0. For a given divisor c of nu let
  b=gcd(d,nu/c).
Then, EXACTLY,
  nu/c | d k_0  iff  nu/(c b) | k_0.                      (4)
Here b divides nu; g need not divide nu and is never summed as its divisor.
Also b<=gcd(d,nu). Count k_0 by (2): at most
  8HS*c*b/(d*nu)
nonzero values of size below 4HS/d. For fixed (d,g,k_0), choose (h_1,u)
in at most 8HS/d ways. The nonzero integer h_1u-k_0=h_2v has at most D_*
signed factorizations. Once h_1,h_2,u,v,g are chosen the primed h values
are determined. Ignoring their size restrictions only enlarges this count.

Multiplying the four bounds (g, k_0, h/s, f) and summing c,d yields an
unequal collision sum, before the D_*^2 in (1), at most
  512 D_* tau(nu) H^3 S F/nu
                 *sum_{d<=2S} gcd(d,nu)/d.               (5)
The remaining sum obeys the elementary exact bound
  sum_{d<=2S} gcd(d,nu)/d
   <=sum_{e|nu} sum_{j<=2S/e} 1/j
   <=tau(nu) H_floor(2S),                                (6)
where H_m=sum_{j<=m}1/j. This uses gcd(d,nu)<=sum_{e|gcd(d,nu)}e.
The original coprimality (z,a)=1 would give gcd(d,nu)=1 when nu|a^infinity,
but that extra simplification is not needed; dropping it causes no gap.

Combining (1), (3), (5), (6), and tau(nu)<=D_* on nonempty support gives
the deliberately generous fully explicit bound

 N_nu <=576 D_*^5 H_floor(2S) (1+H/S) (HS)^2 F/nu.         (7)

This bound is valid without H<S. In the actual source put
D_0=N^2R^2S/x, NM comparable to x. The first size condition and Q<Nx^-sigma
give
  H/S <=x^o(1) QR^2S/M
       <<x^o(1)(Q/N)D_0 <=x^(-8sigma+o(1))<1.
Therefore (7) supplies precisely x^o(1)(HS)^2F/nu. No additive H-independent
term is generated when Hd/S<1; that part of the unequal count is empty.

5. Complex h weights and the subsequent a-smooth sum

For fixed separated parameters allow |omega_h|<=1 and the actual bounded
s coefficients. After expanding the square, a tuple has the weight
lambda'_(s_1) conj(lambda'_(s_2)) omega_(h_1) conj(omega_(h_2)), of modulus
at most one. Its aggregated coefficient B(z,y) satisfies |B(z,y)|<=b(z,y).
This remains true with endpoint restrictions and either h sign. Taking
absolute values at this point and aligning the INNER exponential sum by
one unit phase for each (z,y) preserves both the norm bound and the
restriction (z,a)=1. Reindexing n->nu*n and multiplying by further unit
phases or coprimality indicators also cannot increase this positive envelope.
One must not assume B is itself positive, or cancel its terms to establish
the positive counting bound.

This statement concerns the actual scalar h weights after separation:
they are fixed while the inner b,c exponential sum is taken. An arbitrary
weight still depending jointly on b,c,h could not be extracted by this
argument; no such extension is asserted. The counting estimate by itself
does not prove the preceding separation or a spectral theorem.

Finally for any fixed 0<theta<1 and |a|<=x^A,
  sum_{nu|a^infinity} nu^(theta-1)
   =prod_{p|a}(1-p^(-(1-theta)))^-1 = x^o(1).              (8)
For example split primes at log x. The small-prime part of the logarithm
is O_theta((log x)^theta), and the large-prime part is at most
O_{A,theta}((log x)^theta/log log x); both are o(log x). This is uniform
over the stated a range. Thus inserting (7) into the weighted spectral
nu sum does not lose another polynomial factor. There is no per-nu '+1'
whose sum would replace (8). If a truncated COUNT of a-smooth nu is used
elsewhere, for each requested delta>0 choose a fixed small Rankin exponent
epsilon first; Y<=x^C gives count<=x^(C epsilon+o(1)), hence x^o(1).
That separate count is not needed to replace the decaying series (8).

The k=0 piece is separate throughout: fixed nonzero h_1s_1 has only a
divisor-bounded number of equal-product partners, giving x^o(1)HS choices,
then the source's BFC factor. It is not reinserted into b(z,y) at y=0.
