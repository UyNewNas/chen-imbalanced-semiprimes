2. An exact nonnegative smooth dyadic partition

Choose once a nonincreasing C-infinity function chi on (0,infinity), equal to
1 on t<=1 and to 0 on t>=2, flat at both joining points. Such a function is
given, for 1<t<2, by
 chi(t)=b(2-t)/(b(2-t)+b(t-1)), b(u)=exp(-1/u) for u>0,
with the constant extensions just specified. Define
 psi(t)=chi(t)-chi(2t).
Then psi>=0 is smooth and supported in [1/2,2], with zero value and all
derivatives at the two support endpoints. For R_j=2^j and every n>0,

 sum_{j in Z} psi(n/R_j)=1.                              (S1)

Indeed the sum from j=-J to J is chi(n/2^J)-chi(n*2^(J+1)), which is exactly
1 once J is sufficiently large for this n. At most two chart weights are
nonzero for each n; at a dyadic boundary just one is nonzero. Hence there
is no convergence issue in the pointwise identity. If the outer support is
c*T^a<=p_i<=C*T, only O(log T) charts per group can occur. Use a finite range
slightly larger than [log2(c*T^a)-1,log2(C*T)+1] so that (S1) is exact on
the entire support, including endpoints.

For k original groups, insert prod_i sum_{j_i} psi(p_i/R_{j_i})=1 BEFORE
expanding any group. This is an exact equality of the original weighted
prime sum, its residue-class sum, and its exact coprime mean. It also holds
for signed W_T. The chart weights do not depend on the modulus or error
signs. For nonnegative W_T they are a nonnegative exact partition, not an
outward approximation; there is no extra density allowance or factor two.

3. HB cutoff and individual intervals

On the chart with center R_i, its nonzero weight implies
 R_i/2 < p_i < 2R_i.
The groupwise order-three identity is still used with the SAME cutoff
Z_i=(2R_i)^(1/3), mu truncated at Z_i. It holds for every integer n<=2R_i,
because Lambda*(delta-1*mu_{Z_i})^3 is supported above Z_i^3=2R_i. The new
chart extends downward, not upward. Multiplying that identity by psi(n/R_i)
is therefore exact on all n; the multiplier vanishes where the identity
has not been asserted.

There are at most six individual variables in each group. Keep their
ordinary dyadic intervals sharp: n_j in [U_j,2U_j), with a unit variable
retained separately if desired. These are individual interval restrictions
permitted by the actual coefficient hypotheses, not coupled group filters.
All group information after HB is now the smooth factor
 psi((prod_{j in group i} n_j)/R_i),
together with the already smooth global W_T. Do not also retain the old
indicator 1_{R_i<=prod n_j<=2R_i}; that would undo this repair.

Retain a rectangle only if it meets the support of all these chart factors
and W_T. With at most six variables, existence of a nonzero point gives

        prod_{j in group i} U_j >= R_i/128.               (S2)

This follows from prod n_j>R_i/2 and n_j<2U_j. The old R_i/64 must not be
quoted for these new original charts. Weak inequalities in the resulting
bounds suffice; zero-weight endpoints cause no exceptional arithmetic term.

4. Complete scales and the unchanged common projector

Put X=prod_i R_i, Xplus=2^k X, m<=6k, and x_HB=prod_{j=1}^m U_j. Every
retained HB rectangle contains a point with each original group between
R_i/2 and 2R_i. Consequently

       2^(-7k) X <= x_HB <= Xplus.                       (S3)

Select the medium slot with lower endpoint U. Write P_other for the product
of all other individual lower endpoints. When the complementary convolution
is split into nonempty dyadic intervals [V,2V), its support gives
 P_other/2 <= V <= 2^(m-1) P_other.
The formula also safely covers a complementary unit product. Thus the source
scale x=U*V satisfies the generous, uniform bounds

       2^(-7k-1) X <= x <= 2^(6k-1) Xplus.              (S4)

The original fixed constant C_k=2^(6*k_star+2) is STILL sufficient for the
roughness choice Xtop=C_k*Xplus and Zchart=f(Xtop), where
 f(v)=v^(1/(loglog v)^3).
It was an upper-scale reserve; smoothing changes only the necessary lower
comparison constant. More explicitly 1<=Xtop/x<=2^(14*k_star+3), a fixed
number. Since f is eventually increasing, the SAME single chart projector,
fixed before HB and used in every summand, obeys

       f(x)<=Zchart<=x^(2/(loglog x)^3)                  (S5)

for all sufficiently large x. The upper bound follows from the fixed ratio
Xtop/x: its logarithm and loglog differ from those of x by o(log x) and o(1).
No individual variable gets a newly selected roughness threshold afterward.

The complete product at an original nonzero point lies between 2^(-k)X and

5. Medium and no-medium alternatives, with all constants accounted for

Use the unchanged original-chart medium window
 X^(3*zeta)<=U<=X^(1/3+zeta/4).
By (S4), fixed scale ratios are eventually absorbed by the strict powers,
so this lies in [x^(2*zeta),x^(1/3+zeta/2)] on every eventual source block.
The fixed medium choice is not made again after a Fourier frequency or an
arithmetic tuple is observed.

If there is no medium slot, all individual slots are short (<X^(3*zeta))
or long (>X^(1/3+zeta/4)). A group of only short slots would have lower
product <X^(18*zeta), contrary to (S2) and R_i>>T^a, since X=O(T) and the
existing choice zeta<a/48 gives 18*zeta<a. The changed factor 128 is fixed.
Three long slots would exceed X^(1+3*zeta/4), contrary to x_HB<=2^k X for
large T. Therefore every group has a long slot, at most two long slots exist,
and k<=2 in this alternative. For large T all R_i>=1, so R_i<=X; a truncated
mu slot has endpoint at most (2R_i)^(1/3)=O(X^(1/3)) and cannot be long.

With one long slot there are at most five short slots; with two there are
at most ten. Their lower-endpoint products are at most X^(15*zeta) and
X^(30*zeta). The actual integer products have additional fixed factors at
most 2^5 and 2^10. Both are at most x^(16*zeta) and x^(32*zeta), respectively,
after absorbing these factors and the fixed scale ratios.
The previously proved one/two-long bounds using x^(20*zeta),x^(40*zeta)
are unchanged. This paragraph checks their input sizes, not their analytic
proof a second time.
