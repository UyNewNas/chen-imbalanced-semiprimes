The actual S=1 return from the spectral polynomial to Lemma 5.1
10 October 2026. A scoped interface reconstruction; frozen files unchanged.

0. Result, quantifiers, and independence

For the actual primitive-level, q0=1, fixed s=1 subclass, the spectral
polynomial (K*) in spectral-regular-polynomial-derivation-20261010.txt does
return to the reciprocal b,c kernel needed by Lemma 5.1. The Poisson
factor is D/C, the spectral outside factor is C sqrt(R), and the zero
Poisson frequency contributes D^2 N R times the squared coefficient norm.
The inverse on the first Kloosterman argument is essential:
  S(m inverse(r), a n; c), not S(m r, a n; c).
The actual specialization is (C,D,N,R,s,q0)=(C,B,Y,Z,1,1).

The conclusion is relative to the specified classical Kuznetsov, Bessel,
regular large-sieve, DI exceptional and normalized ABL packet/fiber inputs.
It is not a proof of their original theorems or of unrestricted L23
Theorem 10.2/1.8. In particular it gives no arbitrary level-dependent
m-interval theorem, no nonprimitive (a,R-level)>1 extension, and no
general s or q0 completion. Nor does this note restore a global prime
count or modify K,c,b. Preliminary dispersion and the earlier retained
phase reduction are separate inputs.

Independence: I authored the earlier packet-adversarial report and reviewed
the regular-spectrum polynomial. This is a new explicit reconstruction of
their smooth-separation/completion interface, not a second-person independent
reproof of those whole documents. Root authored the regular polynomial;
the separate multiplicity reconstruction is by another agent. Status labels
or numerical sieve certificates are not used as mathematical premises.

Quantifiers: fix a polynomial-scale bound A0 and a target loss epsilon>0.
Let a be a nonzero integer with |a|<=x^A0; all nonempty integer dyadic
scales and their reciprocals are bounded by fixed powers of x. Cutoff
functions have fixed compact supports and finitely many controlled smooth
seminorms. All constants below are uniform in a, the dyadic scales, the
common interval endpoints and common additive phases, and arbitrary complex
n-side coefficients. They may depend on A0, epsilon and those fixed smooth
seminorms. The finite differentiability order is chosen before the small
completion parameter delta and before x; its precise choice is described
in section 7. A fixed positive power x^delta is never called o(1).

1. The actual coefficient class, before spectral completion

Temporarily write the original Lemma 5.1 scales as N0,M0,Q,R0,S0, with
N0 M0 comparable to x. Keep its arithmetic d fixed. The relation
  f d q = n1-n2
gives |f|<=2N0/(dQ). Product endpoints are first separated by the source's
Fourier identity. The resulting scalar h weights omega_h are fixed during
the inner arithmetic b,c sum, with |omega_h|<=1 after paying their variation
norm. This is where the separate retained-phase reduction is needed.
The present argument does not license a still-joint rough weight
omega(b,c,h), or arbitrary new weights in the original q.

Apply the displayed positive Cauchy square in f,n1,n2,r1,r2,s2 BEFORE
discarding congruence and endpoint restrictions. Then set
  b=n2 r1, c=n1 r2 s2, B=N0 R0, C=N0 R0 S0.
Representations have multiplicity at most tau(b) tau_3(c)=x^epsilon.
This is a positive multiplicity in front of the squared inner sum; it can
be bounded before summation. It leaves a smooth positive b,c majorant
psi(b/B) psi(c/C), not arbitrary rough coefficients on the future Poisson
m side. Fixed smooth majorants of the full arithmetic boxes suffice.

Retain (s1,a)=1 in the inner sum even when other positive restrictions are
dropped. After expanding the square and relabeling its two s indices,
  z=s1 s2, (a,z)=1,
  y=f(h1 s1-h2 s2) !=0
up to an immaterial interchange of the h/s labels and sign. The zero
difference k=0 is a different diagonal term, not a y=0 coefficient here.
Both h signs, including h=+1 and -1, are retained. Their printed exclusion
in one source line must not be copied into the actual enlarged envelope.

At each (y,z), the scalar complex tuple coefficient is bounded in absolute
value by its positive counting coefficient b_count(y,z). By the triangle
inequality it is enough to bound
  sum_(y,z,(a,z)=1) b_count(y,z) |K(y,z)|,
where
  K(y,z)=sum_(b,c,(c,bz)=1) psi(b/B)psi(c/C)
                         e(a y inverse(zb)/c).
Choose one unit phase epsilon_(y,z) aligning each inner K. The resulting
beta_(y,z)=epsilon_(y,z)b_count(y,z)1_((a,z)=1) is arbitrary complex,
but independent of arithmetic b,c and of the future Poisson m. This does
not require b_count itself to be the original complex coefficient.

Split signs and dyadic ranges of y and z. Each resulting kernel has the
class stated in section 2. All restrictions can be dropped in the positive
counting norm when convenient; (a,z)=1 must still be kept in the kernel
to which the coprime packet theorem is applied.

2. Exact Poisson identity and the zero frequency

Use generic spectral scales C,D,N,R, reserving d for the Poisson variable.
Let beta_(n,r) be supported on n~N, r~R and (a,r)=1. For n>0 define
  V=sum_(n,r) beta_(n,r) sum_(c,(c,r)=1)
       psi(c/C) sum_(d,(d,c)=1) psi(d/D)e(an inverse(rd)/c).
Negative n is treated by changing the sign of a. There is no requirement
(a,c)=1. Arithmetic residue-class Poisson gives the exact identity
  sum_(d,(d,c)=1) psi(d/D)e(an inverse(r)inverse(d)/c)
   = (D/c) sum_(m in Z) psi_hat(mD/c) S(m,an inverse(r);c)
   = (D/c) sum_(m in Z) psi_hat(mD/c) S(m inverse(r),an;c).       (P0)
The last equality substitutes u=inverse(r)v in the Kloosterman sum and
uses (r,c)=1. Every bar means inversion modulo c.

Thus the outside factor is D/C, and the normalized smooth amplitude is
  A(c,m)=(C/c) psi(c/C) psi_hat(mD/c).                         (P1)
For m<0, S(-|m| inverse(r),an;c)=S(|m| inverse(r),-an;c) after
u->-u; use the opposite-sign Kuznetsov formula. Its regular estimates
have the same needed bounds and its same-sign holomorphic term is absent.
No positivity of beta is required at this stage.

At m=0 the Kloosterman sum is the Ramanujan sum c_c(an). It is not
discarded. Since an!=0,
  |c_c(an)|<=gcd(an,c),
  sum_(c~C) gcd(an,c) <= O(C tau(|an|)).
For the latter inequality expand gcd(an,c)<=sum_(j|an,j|c) j; only
j<=O(C) occur and their number of multiples is O(C/j). Consequently
  |V_0| << x^epsilon D sum_(n,r)|beta_(n,r)|
         << x^epsilon D sqrt(NR) ||beta||_2.                  (P2)
Its squared contribution is D^2 N R ||beta||_2^2. There is no tau(0)
and no use of a<=x^epsilon. Polynomially bounded a suffices for the
divisor estimate, with a separately chosen divisor exponent.

3. A genuinely common m cutoff and a quantitative tail

Choose delta>0 later and truncate at the GLOBAL value
  T=(C/D)x^delta.
It depends on the fixed dyadic C,D chart, not on c,n,r or level. For
each sign decompose the retained integers into common dyadic intervals
I contained in (M,2M], intersected with |m|<=T. All nonempty blocks can
be represented with M>=1/2. There is no level-dependent choice of I.

For any integer J>=2, rapid decay and the trivial bound |S|<=c give
  |V_tail| <<_J (DC+C^2) sqrt(NR)||beta||_2 x^(-(J-1)delta).    (P3)
Here one may use sum_(m>T)(1+mD/C)^(-J)
 << (1+C/D)x^(-(J-1)delta). This bound is also safe when T<1;
then all nonzero frequencies are in the tail. It does not use a false
no-+1 estimate for arbitrary translated integer intervals. Since all
scales are polynomial in x, J can be chosen to make (P3) an arbitrarily
negative power times ||beta||_2. The m=0 bound already supplies a
nonzero polynomial-scale reference term for comparison.

On a retained block M<=(C/D)x^delta, a fixed number j of normalized
derivatives of (P1) costs at most O_j(x^(j delta)), after increasing a
constant depending on the smooth cutoff. This follows from the chain
rule in mD/c. It contains no power of a.

4. Explicit four-variable separation preserves the spectral hypotheses

On a positive m block put
  Y0=4 pi sqrt(|a| M N)/(C sqrt(R)),
  v=m/M, w=n/N, zeta=r/R,
  t=[4 pi sqrt(|a|mn)/(c sqrt(r))]/Y0.
Then, exactly,
  u=c/C=sqrt(vw/zeta)/t.                                  (P4)
All four normalized coordinates lie in a fixed compact positive box
after smooth extensions of the dyadic supports. Crucially a cancels in
(P4); normalized coordinate changes and their inverses have uniformly
bounded derivatives of each fixed order, independently of |a|.

The canonical amplitude used by Kuznetsov is
  C sqrt(R)/(c sqrt(r)) f(4 pi sqrt(|a|mn)/(c sqrt(r))).
Therefore extend smoothly on the fixed box the function
  F(t,v,w,zeta)=u sqrt(zeta) A(Cu,Mv), u=sqrt(vw/zeta)/t,
with fixed auxiliary cutoffs equal to one on the actual ranges. Its
normalized derivatives through order j are bounded by a fixed seminorm
times x^(O_j(delta)). Fourier-expand F on a fixed period P:
  F=sum_(k in Z^4) a_k
            e((k_t t+k_m v+k_n w+k_r zeta)/P).
For every fixed L, integration by parts in each coordinate gives
  |a_k| <= C_L ||F||_(C^(4L)) product_i(1+|k_i|)^(-L).     (P5)
Insert a single smooth chi(t) equal to one on this support. Each term is
now of the canonical spectral form with
  f_k(u)=chi(u/Y0)e(k_t u/(P Y0)),
  a_m=1_I(m)e(k_m m/(PM)),
  beta^k_(n,r)=beta_(n,r)e(k_n n/(PN)+k_r r/(PR)).
The m interval I and the phase k_m/(PM) are common across ALL levels.
The n phase is allowed to depend on n and r; its absolute value is one.
In particular ||beta^k||_2=||beta||_2.

For a fixed Bessel-transform derivative order J0, f_k has normalized
seminorm O((1+|k_t|)^J0). Choose L>J0+2 in (P5). Then
  sum_k |a_k|(1+|k_t|)^J0 << ||F||_(C^(4L)).               (P6)
Apply the spectral bound per mode and the triangle inequality in norm
before summing these coefficients. The cost is the sum in (P6), squared
only when the final inequality is squared. There is no uncontrolled
number of modes or level-dependent coefficient on the m side.

This construction treats exactly the separable positive majorant left
by section 1 and its smooth transforms. It is not an assertion that an
arbitrary joint rough weight of m,r can be separated with a bounded norm.
Likewise arbitrary intervals I_r cannot be inserted: their DFT weights
depend on the Hilbert coordinate, which the DI interval-side argument
does not allow. Common Mellin weights, if used instead, have bounded
variation O(1+|t_Mellin|) on one dyadic m block; that extra norm must also
be paid by the transform decay, rather than called modulus one for J.

5. Level, packet, and the exact norm multipliers

Now the level is r (z in the actual application), and its two cusps are
infinity and 1. Their width parameter is mu=1/r. For (c,r)=1 the source
cusp identity reads
  S_(infinity,1)(m,an;c sqrt(r))
       =e(an/r) S(m inverse(r),an;c).
Thus put beta'_(n,r)=e(-an/r) beta^k_(n,r); its norm is unchanged.
Restoring 1/(c sqrt(r)) in Kuznetsov gives the outside C sqrt(R),
separately from the already extracted D/C in Poisson.

For h|a^infinity and (n,a)=1, the coprime ABL packet input has the common
oldform phase e(n(ah-1)/r). It yields the modified coefficient
  beta*_(n,r;h)=1_((n,a)=1) beta'_(hn,r)e(n(ah-1)/r)
               =1_((n,a)=1) beta^k_(hn,r)e(-n/r).
This phase need not be discarded or approximated. Define
  B_h^2=sum_(r,n)|beta*_(n,r;h)|^2
           <=sum_(r,n)|beta_(hn,r)|^2,
  B^2=sum_(r,n)|beta_(n,r)|^2.
The packet cost is (|a|h)^(theta/2), theta=7/32, with selectable divisor
losses. It acts on the n functional; the common m functional is unchanged.
No multiplicativity claim for a fixed orthonormal oldform is required.
For negative Fourier arguments use the signed packet identity consistently.

At length L=N/h, the fixed-level exceptional norm is
  sum_exc (1+X0)^tau |sum_n beta*_(n,r;h)rho_(j,1)(n)|^2
      << x^epsilon(1+L/r) sum_n|beta*_(n,r;h)|^2,
  X0=1+R/L, 0<tau<=theta.
Indeed 1+X0=2+R/L is at most a fixed multiple of max(1,r/L), so the
needed range is contained in Pascadi Theorem A. Arbitrary level-dependent
n coefficients are expressly permitted, and nonempty L>=1/2.

On the m side Pascadi Theorem J applies to e(m omega), uniformly in omega.
An exact finite DFT completes the common interval I with coefficient l1
norm O(log(2M)); Hilbert Minkowski therefore costs O(log^2(2M)) in the
SQUARED norm. This gives the required level-average bound
  S_m << x^epsilon M(M+R)
     [1+{M(1+X/sqrt(X0))^2/(M+R)^2}^theta],
  X=C sqrt(R)/(4 pi sqrt(|a|MN)).
Neither this DFT nor a packet basis change creates a level-dependent m
interval. The bounded positive cosh factor on the exceptional spectrum
changes only absolute constants. Pascadi's exponent theta_j is tau/2.

For the regular/holomorphic/continuous norms, the separately reconstructed
shell sum uses only |spectral t|>=max(2Y0,1) for its high-shell Bessel
decay. The r-Cauchy costs exactly sqrt(R), not R. Together with the two
large-sieve factors 1/sqrt(R) and the outside C sqrt(R), its squared
prefactor is bounded by
  P0=M C^4 R/(|a|MN+C^2 R).
The continuous part uses the normalized ABL character-pair oldform fiber
with its Plancherel measure. It is an explicit external input here;
Eisenstein series are not being made into L2 cusp-form packet vectors.

Consequently the inherited, explicitly derived spectral polynomial is
  |L_M|^2 << x^epsilon P0 {
     sum_h (|a|h)^theta (N/h+R+T0)(M+R+T0) B_h^2
    +(C^2 R)^theta (N+R)^(1-theta)(M+R)^(1-2theta) B^2 },   (K*)
  T0=|a|MN/C^2.
All factors 4pi and bounded dyadic ratios are absorbed in absolute
constants, not suppressed powers of any arithmetic scale.

6. Return polynomial, including short M blocks

First suppose M<=C/D and put L=N/h. The exact algebraic identity
  R(L+R+T0)(M+R+T0)/(R+T0)
   =R(R+T0+L+M)+RLM/(R+T0)
   <=(L+R)(M+R)+RT0                                      (P7)
shows that multiplying the first term of (K*) by (D/C)^2 is at most
  D^2 M [(L+R)(M+R)+RT0]
    <= C(L+R)(C+DR)+|a|NR.
This handles every short M block, not just its endpoint M=C/D.
For the second term use R/(R+T0)<=1 and 1-2theta>=0:
  D^2 M(C^2R)^theta(N+R)^(1-theta)(M+R)^(1-2theta)
    <=C(CD sqrt(R))^(2theta)
                  (N+R)^(1-theta)(C+DR)^(1-2theta).

For the ACTUAL cutoff put W=x^delta>=1. If M<=W C/D then
M+R<=W(C/D+R). The first bound above is at most W^2 times its displayed
target, and the exceptional bound at most W^(2-2theta) times its target,
hence also at most W^2. This explicitly pays 2delta in the squared
polynomial before the finite seminorm losses, rather than mislabeling a
fixed x^delta as o(1). Dyadic M summation adds a logarithmic norm cost.
This comparison is valid even when the real number M*=C/D is below1/2:
(K*) is invoked only at the ACTUAL nonempty M>=1/2; M* is merely a
positive real comparison point in a polynomial inequality. No spectral
theorem at an empty M* interval is being invoked. If T<1, section 3
already puts all nonzero integer m into the tail.

Adding (P2) and using the triangle inequality gives, for any prescribed
epsilon>0 after the choices in section 7,
  |V|^2 << x^epsilon J^2,
  J^2=sum_h (|a|h)^theta
        [C(N/h+R)(C+DR)+|a|NR] B_h^2
    +[C(CD sqrt(R))^(2theta)(N+R)^(1-theta)(C+DR)^(1-2theta)
                                  +D^2NR] B^2.           (P8)
This is the required S=1, q0=1 polynomial. The bars/inverses, B_h SQUARED
norms, and the D^2NR term must all survive the mapping. We have not used
the incorrect S^(-1/2) factor mentioned in earlier general completion
arguments. No unrestricted S-dependent assertion follows from (P8).

7. Parameter selection without circular smallness

Fix the desired final squared-bound exponent epsilon and polynomial
scale bound A0. First choose the finite Bessel derivative order J0 needed
by the stated transforms and the shell estimate. Then choose L>J0+2 for
the four-variable expansion, and a finite C0 so that its squared
seminorm cost is bounded by C x^(C0 delta). Such C0 is determined by
these derivative orders and is independent of delta, a and x.
Choose 0<delta<=epsilon/[4(C0+2)]. The W^2 bound and the seminorm cost
then consume at most epsilon/4. Choose the epsilons in the classical
spectral inputs, packet divisor estimates, a-smooth h count, and ordinary
divisor bounds separately small so their total is at most epsilon/4.
The finite number of logarithmic summations fits a further epsilon/4
for large x. Finally choose the Poisson tail derivative order J large
enough relative to delta,A0 and the requested tail power. This last
choice does not change the earlier fixed separation order 4L.

Hence the completion costs at most the prescribed x^epsilon, with spare
room for a harmless sum of finitely many pieces. Constants can worsen
as epsilon shrinks, as usual; they do not depend on the target a. If the
application reserves a spectral margin sigma, make the squared-bound
epsilon choice smaller than that budget in advance. The separate
retained five-variable phase loss x^(5kappa+o(1)) is NOT included in the
selectable completion epsilon and cannot be absorbed by this argument
unless kappa and sigma satisfy their separate ledger.

8. The actual counting input and large-residue scale after return

Now substitute (D,N,R)=(B,Y,Z), with the original scales
  B=N0 R0, C=N0 R0 S0, Z=S0^2,
  Y=N0 R0^2 S0^3/M0, F<=N0/Q,
  H<=x^epsilon Q R0^2 S0^2/M0.
Dyadic constants and the separately selected H truncation allowance are
handled by smaller epsilons. The actual original S0 is not the fixed
spectral arithmetic s=1.
These are UPPER scales. Before making this substitution, (P8) is applied
to the actual dyadic B',C',Y',Z' blocks. Its displayed factors are monotone
in these positive scales because 0<=theta<=1/2; extend the h sum with zero
coefficients as necessary and use the common counting envelope below.
Only then replace the scales by their upper values. Thus BZ>=C used below
is a relation between those upper values, not an assumption that every
original independent B',C' block has C'/B'>=1 or B'Z'>=C'.

The independently reconstructed positive counting norm supplies
  B_h^2 <= x^epsilon (HS0)^2 F/h.                          (P9)
It counts nonzero y=fk and 1<=|h_i|<=H, without assuming k comparable
to HS0 or gcd(s1,s2)=1. It is identically zero for h beyond the entire
nonzero y support. Section 5 of that separate reconstruction also checks
the scalar complex omega_h weights and the unit alignment phases used
in section 1 here. We rely on its proven general counting inequality,
not on bounded sample tests. The proof of the spectral return itself
only requires (P9) as a coefficient-norm hypothesis.

Let
  D0=N0^2 R0^2 S0/x=Y/Z,
  D1=|a|^theta N0 R0^2 S0^5 Q/x^2,
  D2=(N0 R0/S0)^(2theta) N0 R0^2 S0^5 Q/x^2.
The actual hypotheses give D0<1 and H/S0<=x^epsilon(Q/N0)D0<1.
Using BZ>=C, theta<1, and the convergent weighted a-smooth sum
sum_(h|a^infinity)h^(theta-1)=x^o(1), (P8)-(P9) imply
  J^2 << x^epsilon [|a|^theta(CBZ+|a|Y)Z
                 +C^(1+2theta) B Z^(2-2theta)+B^2YZ](HS0)^2F.
The relation is CBZ comparable to xY=N0^2R0^2S0^3, not S0^5.
Also B^2<x under the source size hypotheses. The |a|Y contribution
cannot be removed: it yields the factor 1+|a|/x.

The outside Cauchy factor is x^epsilon N0/(Q R0 S0^(3/2)). The separate
zero-difference diagonal is at most x^epsilon H S0 B F C. Combining
the diagonal with the returned off-diagonal bound through the two square
roots gives the needed natural estimate
  |W_original| << (N0^2/Q)x^epsilon
       {D0^(1/2)+[(1+|a|/x)D1+D2]^(1/4)}.                (P10)
Thus |a|<=x^(1+kappa) costs kappa/4 here, separate from the earlier
5kappa variation loss. This is not the printed unrestricted Lemma 5.1
under an implicit a<=x^epsilon convention.

9. Findings, genuine limitations, and dependency order

No new coefficient-class obstruction was found in this ACTUAL subclass.
The previously abbreviated completion can be made explicit without an
extra fixed power of a, z or C. The positive Cauchy step, global m cutoff,
four-variable smooth expansion, coprime packet, and one unit phase per
(y,z) are all necessary pieces of that statement.

The following stronger claims are not justified by this calculation:
* General q0 or noncoprime (a,z): the common packet phase argument uses
  (a,z)=1 and K=1 in the ABL specialization.
* Arbitrary level-dependent m intervals/phases: the required DI J norm
  is used only for a common interval and a common additive phase.
* General arithmetic s: fixed s=1 is a genuine restricted problem, not
  a literal substitution into a dyadic convention that excludes 1.
* Arbitrary b,c,h joint weights before Cauchy: their separation and
  norm must be proved, not inferred from |weight|<=1 after spectral sums.
* Removing the continuous-fiber hypothesis or the classical norm/trace
  formula inputs: these remain explicit external mathematical inputs.

Dependency order used:
  actual endpoint/retained-phase separation and positive Cauchy
    -> smooth b,c kernel with (a,z)=1 and the count envelope;
  exact Poisson (P0) + zero frequency (P2) + global cutoff (P3)
    -> common m blocks;
  smooth expansion (P4)-(P6) + normalized packet/fiber input
    + classical F/J and regular-shell inputs -> (K*);
  elementary polynomial inequalities (P7) + selectable losses -> (P8);
  actual positive count norm (P9) + original scale identities -> (P10).

The main-term dispersion cancellation, E1/E2 errors, full moving-target
parameter bridge, rough product compilation, lower/upper sieve density
identities, and numerical certificates are not recertified here. This
local derivation must not be substituted for a proof of any of them.
