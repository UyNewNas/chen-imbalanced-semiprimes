Fixed-d E2 to the well-factorable exponential sum: a unified interface

0. Result, scope, and reviewer role

This note reconstructs the actual fixed-d E3 array, rather than applying
the conclusion of the disputed phase-deletion lemma. Exact reciprocity,
five-variable summation by parts, a disjoint coprime partition, expansion
of BOTH small-factor choices, and three bounded-coefficient polarizations
reduce this array to the bare exponential sum in L23 Lemma 5.1.

Two endpoint details missing from the concise earlier bridge are supplied:
(i) a LOWER cutoff on either total rough product must be written as a
difference of upper cutoffs, not inserted into one individual factor;
(ii) the smooth q support can cross constant dyadic scales, so the source
margin is relaxed to sigma_S=3 sigma/4. The residue condition remains
kappa<=sigma/100; the natural-scale estimate verifies this directly,
without falsely invoking a corollary requiring kappa<=sigma_S/100.

The resulting bound is
  |E2(a)| << N^2/(DQ) x^(-sigma/2),
uniformly for the stated fixed family, conditional on the BARE coprime
spectral exponential-sum input S0 specified below. No independent extra
q weight, endpoint depending on a summation variable, or h weight coupled
to q,n,r is admitted. The retained phase is charged exactly once.

This is an audit of the E2 CONNECTION. It is not a reproof of the spectral
theorems, preliminary dispersion D, its main-term/zero corrections, E1,

1. Notation, actual hypotheses, and the analytic input being used

Set x=NM and L=log x. Fix 0<sigma<1/100, 0<=kappa<=sigma/100 and a!=0
with |a|<=x^(1+kappa). The restricted application has theta=7/32 and also
c0*x<=|a|, but the lower bound on |a| is needed by E1, not by this E2
connection. Assume the four actual scale inequalities
  Q < N x^(-sigma),
  N^2 Q2 Q3^2 < x^(1-8 sigma),
  |a|^theta N Q Q2^5 Q3^2 < x^(2-15 sigma),
  N^(1+2theta) Q Q2^(5-2theta) Q3^(2+2theta)
       < x^(2-15 sigma).                                      (1)
All lengths are at least one. Parameters and constants in coefficient
bounds are fixed before the asymptotic limit. All relevant scales are
bounded by a fixed power of x.

Split q2=u*s and q3=v*t into rough and smooth parts, respectively. On the
piece under consideration u~U, v~V, s~S0, t~T0, st=d~D, and uv~Rcal.
Here Rcal is comparable to UV, D*Rcal is comparable to Q2*Q3, and
D<=x^(2/log log x) up to fixed constants. The rough factors u,v and
alpha_n are supported on P^->=Z, with log Z bounded below by a positive
constant times log x/(log log x)^3. The chart-fixed larger Z permitted
by the HB reduction satisfies this requirement. Smooth s,t have no prime
factor above Z. Thus (d,uv)=1 automatically. Only d coprime to a occurs.

For fixed d define EXACTLY
  Lambda_d(w) = sum_{st=d} sum_{uv=w}
                   lambda_(us) nu_(vt) I(s,t,u,v),              (2)
where I contains the fixed individual intervals and smooth/rough
supports. A total-product interval w~Rcal may be left outside (2);
if it is included it is preserved explicitly below. Lambda_d is
independent of q. The spurious q1 summation printed in the source
definition at lines2479--2485 is not part of (2).
The original one-bounded gamma_q has already been eliminated by the
outer dispersion Cauchy step. It is absent from the actual E3 array.
This absence, visible in source lines2561--2569, is essential.

The analytic input S0 is the bare fixed-d, cross-coprime well-factorable
exponential-sum estimate, with diagonal one-bounded Gamma_r, Lambda_s,
alpha_n and exactly the kernel and restrictions at L23 lines1935--1949.
It has q0<=q<=q0'<=2q0 and product upper cutoffs r_i*s_i<=B_i, and permits
fixed (d,a)=1 with d=x^o(1). It does NOT assert arbitrary q weights.
The restricted coprime spectral derivation gives the natural form
  |W| << N^2/q0 * x^o(1)
          [D0^(1/2)+((1+|a|/x)*D1+D2)^(1/4)],                (S0)
  D0=N^2 R^2 S/x,
  D1=|a|^theta N q0 R^2 S^5/x^2,

Here R=V and S=U, not the total rough-product length Rcal. The bare W
has no extra smooth reciprocity phase. Its allowed H is

2. The exact fixed-d array and reciprocity

Put H=QD Rcal^2 L^5/M. For one fixed a, before taking a supremum, let
  E2(a) = sum_{d~D,(d,a)=1} E3(a;d)/d.                        (3)
E3(a;d) is the sum over q, w1,w2~Rcal, n1,n2~N and 1<=|h|<=H of
  Lambda_d(w1) conjugate(Lambda_d(w2))
  *alpha_(n1) conjugate(alpha_(n2))/(q*w1*w2)
  *psi0(q/Q) hatpsi0(hM/(qd*w1*w2))
  *e(ah inverse(n1*w2)/(qd*w1)
                  +ah inverse(n2*qd*w1)/w2).                 (4)
All of these original restrictions are retained:
  (q,a)=1, (w1,a*w2)=1, (w2,a*q*d*w1)=1;
  n1=n2 (mod qd), (n1,n2*q*d*w1)=1,
  (n2,n1*q*d*w2)=1, |n1-n2|>=N/L^Csep.                       (5)
The smooth support is q/Q in [1/2,5/2]. Supremums over a are bounded
uniformly AFTER (3), using the triangle inequality; the supremum of a
sum has not been written as an equality of independent supremums.

The two phases in (4) combine EXACTLY to
  e(ah*(n1-n2)*inverse(n2*qd*w1)/(n1*w2))
       *e(ah/(qd*w1*w2*n1)).                                 (6)
To verify this, apply reciprocity with the coprime integers n1*w2 and
qd*w1. Lift the inverse of n2*qd*w1 from modulus w2 to modulus n1*w2;
this is allowed by (5). Modulo n1*w2, inverse(qd*w1) is n2 times that
lifted inverse. Their difference gives (6). No inverse is used without
the corresponding coprimality in (5), and no Taylor deletion is made.

In particular E3 has denominator q*w1*w2, NOT q*d*w1*w2. Source line2593
inserts an extra d although line2564's E3 has none. Equations (3)--(4)
avoid that inconsistency. The q variable is never replaced by qd.

3. Five-variable variation produces scalar, frozen endpoints

If H<1 the sum is empty. Otherwise split h into its positive and negative
signs and split the q support
into disjoint intervals inside dyadic scales q0=Q/2,Q,2Q. Endpoint
rounding or half-open conventions can be implemented by differences
of anchored sums; only an absolute number of terms is needed.
If Q/2<1, use scale1 for the nonempty positive-integer part; all scales
remain within fixed factors of Q and the same argument applies.
Normalize the variables as
  (q/q0,w1/Rcal,w2/Rcal,n1/N,h/H)=(v1,v2,v3,v4,v5).
The first four coordinates are in fixed compact positive intervals;
v5 lies in [0,1] or [-1,0]. The smooth weight removed by summation by
parts is exactly
  W(v)=psi0(q0*v1/Q)
       *hatpsi0(Ld*v5/(v1*v2*v3))
       *e(thetad*v5/(v1*v2*v3*v4)),
  Ld=HM/(q0*d*Rcal^2)=(Q/q0)(D/d)L^5,
  thetad=aH/(q0*d*Rcal^2*N)=(a/x)(Q/q0)(D/d)L^5.             (7)
Because d~D, both ratios Q/q0 and D/d are bounded by absolute constants.
For every subset I of the five coordinates, each differentiated at
most once, the product/chain rules give
  ||partial_I W||_infinity
       <<_I (1+L^5+(|a|/x)L^5)^|I|.
The sum of all face-integrated variations is therefore
  V5(W) << (1+L^5+(|a|/x)L^5)^5 = x^(5kappa+o(1)).          (8)
There is no singular derivative at h=0; the original integer h=0 term
is simply absent from the coefficient array. Denominator 1/(q*w1*w2),
all congruences, gcd indicators, and the separation condition remain
in that array. No derivative of an arithmetic indicator is taken.

Repeated summation by parts bounds the weighted sum by V5(W) times
the supremum of the corresponding anchored rectangular sums. Every
integration endpoint is frozen before invoking a bound on a rectangle.
On each resulting rectangle:
  q lies in one interval contained in [q0,2q0];
  each wi has a scalar lower and upper endpoint;
  n1 has its independent endpoint n1<=N1 (n2 has its original support);
  omega_h is the indicator of a fixed signed interval, |omega_h|<=1.
One may take the h range in S0 to be the entire 1<=|h|<=H and encode the
chosen signed interval in omega. This also handles a negative anchored
interval whose absolute values are not anchored at zero.
Omega may depend on fixed a,d and all chosen endpoint parameters. It
may not depend on the summation values q,w1,w2,n1,n2. There is no weight
W(q,...,h) left in the rectangle: its derivatives have been integrated
as scalar total-variation measures. Reintroducing such a coupled omega
after (8) would invalidate the proposed application of S0.

4. Coprime classes and the full list of endpoint operations

From (5), the pair (n1*w2,n2) is coprime. Its entries are polynomially
bounded and rough, so each has O((log log x)^3) prime factors. Maynard I
Lemma13.2 gives a DISJOINT partition into exp(O((log log x)^4)) classes
N_j such that any two pairs (b,c),(b',c') in a class satisfy
(b,c')=(b',c)=1. Partition all possible pairs once; restricting endpoints
thereafter preserves the property. The construction is independent of
coefficient values and a, so no number of a values or endpoint choices
is paid. Fix a class before the next operations.

Expand BOTH Lambda factors in (4), independently choosing
  st=d and s't'=d.
There are at most tau(d)^2 choices. Write w1=v1'*u1' and w2=v2'*u2',
where v_i'~V and u_i'~U. The coefficient is then
  nu_(v1'*t) conjugate(nu_(v2'*t'))
       *lambda_(u1'*s) conjugate(lambda_(u2'*s')).             (9)
All restrictions on individual u_i',v_i' belong in their respective
one-variable sequences. Different s,s',t,t' generally produce different
sequences; setting the two choices equal is not an identity.

The total-product window on each wi is handled as follows, including
any original w~Rcal restriction and the endpoint from section3:
  1_(A_i<r_i*s_i<=B_i)
       =1_(r_i*s_i<=B_i)-1_(r_i*s_i<=A_i).                  (10)
Thus two total-product intervals produce at most four terms. These are
EXACTLY the independent upper cutoffs B1,B2 allowed in S0. This operation
does not place a coupled product indicator in Gamma_r or Lambda_s, and
does not drop any lower restriction. Upper endpoints below the possible
product range give empty sums, not an exceptional theorem case.

After expansion, the coprime class becomes (n1*r2*s2,n2) in N_j, precisely
as in S0. The other conditions become the full displayed restrictions
at source lines1937--1946. Source q0<=q<=q0'<=2q0 matches each anchored
q endpoint. A general subinterval is a difference of two such anchored
intervals; it is NOT an arbitrary bounded q sequence. A smooth function
supported on [Q/2,5Q/2] cannot in general be replaced by a single source
interval [Q,2Q]; the explicit constant-scale subdivision above is used.

5. Independent coefficients and why SW is not reapplied

For any fixed kernel B(u,v), linear in u and conjugate-linear in v,
let Q_B(w)=B(w,w). Even if the kernel is not Hermitian,
  B(u,v)=(1/4) sum_(j=0)^3 i^j Q_B(u+i^j v).
If u,v are one-bounded, (u+i^j v)/2 is one-bounded, so a uniform diagonal
bound Bcal gives |B(u,v)|<=4 Bcal. Apply this independently to the two
nu sequences, the two lambda sequences in (9), and finally
  u_n=alpha_n*1_(n<=N1), v_n=alpha_n.
The total polarization factor is at most 4^3=64. It is in addition to
the at most four product-interval terms in (10), not a replacement for
them. All coprime restrictions and the class N_j are held fixed in each
of these algebraic identities. Rough supports are preserved under the
linear combinations. S0 requires boundedness here, not SW. The earlier
dispersion application of SW precedes these operations; no claim of SW
for an arbitrary polarized sequence is made.

6. The bounded-h extension and fixed-d proof are compatible

The bare source statement sums symmetrically in h. Its proof supports
the fixed omega_h above: insert omega inside the inner (s1,h) sum before
Cauchy in f,n1,n2,r1,r2,s2. The ensuing square adds only
omega_(h1)*conjugate(omega_(h2)). It has absolute value at most one and
is independent of the completed variables b=n2*r1,c=n1*r2*s2. For
k=h1*s1-h2*s2=0 the same positive collision count dominates. For k!=0
the coefficient after grouping z=s1*s2, y=f*k is dominated by the same
positive multiplicity b_(z,y). Both its ordinary and restricted n''
L2 norms are therefore unchanged as upper bounds. Keep (s_i,a)=1
throughout; then (z,a)=1, the coprime spectral subclass actually used.

The printed 1<|h| in some W3/W4 lines must not delete h=+1 or -1. Keep
1<=|h| in those displays; the positive counts and the source's later
b_(z,y) definition already include them. Positive/negative y can be
estimated separately after taking the kernel absolute value; omega
need not be symmetric. This is a local notation issue, not phase loss.

The source's product-endpoint Fourier integral removes r1*s1<=B1 with
an L1 kernel O(log x); use periodic distance ||u|| in the Dirichlet-kernel
bound near both 0 and 1. The resulting e(s1*u) changes a one-variable
coefficient; omega_h remains independent of b,c. That logarithm is in
the bare S0 proof, not a second five-variable variation charge here.

Finally put f=(n1-n2)/(dq), a nonzero signed integer. Its natural range
is |f|<=2N/(d*q0). The exact inverse phase becomes
  e(ah*f*inverse(n2*r1*s1)/(n1*r2*s2)).
The source may enlarge the f range to 2N/q0 after its Cauchy square.
The condition n1=n2 (mod df) is dropped only from a positive sum after
Cauchy, not from E3 itself. For f<0 the two inequalities describing
q0<=q<=q0' reverse order; splitting the two signs is an absolute cost.
No sparsity condition d|q' is created, and no theorem with arbitrary
weights on q is used. These facts explain why treating d as fixed is
compatible with S0, whereas replacing q by qd and selecting multiples
of d without a new theorem would not be justified.

7. Uniform scales, one phase charge, and the final d normalization

Because R=V<<Q3 and S=U<<Q2, with fixed comparison constants, all three
natural ratios in S0 have positive exponents of U,V. In particular
5-2theta>0. The fixed-factor choices only shorten the original lengths.
Consequently (1) gives, uniformly in the endpoint blocks,
  D0 << x^(-8sigma),  D1,D2 << x^(-15sigma).                (11)
The q block q0 is between Q/2 and 2Q. Call the source estimate with
sigma_S=3sigma/4. For large x, q0<N*x^(-sigma_S), and (11) implies
its 7sigma_S/14sigma_S conditions with strict spare power. Also
  H/(q0*V^2*U^2/M) << D L^5 = x^o(1),
so the SAME full H used for a signed interval is allowed. Product
endpoints do not change this length condition.

There is a small but important quantifier detail. The simplified old
S audit stated kappa<=sigma_S/100; here kappa<=sigma/100 need not satisfy
that inequality after relaxing sigma_S. Use its natural formula instead:
before the retained phase, the saving is at least
  (7/2)sigma_S - kappa/4 - epsilon_internal.
With the retained phase charged ONCE it is at least
  (7/2)sigma_S - (21/4)kappa - epsilon_internal.
For epsilon_internal<=sigma_S/4 and kappa<=sigma/100 this exceeds
2 sigma. Thus the weaker bare estimate N^2/q0*x^(-sigma_S) needed here
has ample slack. This direct check supplies the slightly changed
parameter ratio; it does not silently reuse the narrower corollary.

Alternatively, use the stronger actual ratios (11) directly. After
one phase-variation charge they save
  15sigma/4 - 21kappa/4 - o(1),
which is also more than sufficient. These are alternative ledgers;
the phase multiplier from (8) must not be applied twice to a bound
that has already included it. Hereafter use the weak BARE S0 saving
sigma_S and charge (8) just once.

The partition count, tau(d)^2 pairs, fixed endpoint/polarization counts,
the fixed smooth derivatives, dyadic decompositions, and internal source
normalizations are all a single collection of x^o(1) factors. This
collection is independent of a,d and the frozen endpoints in their
stated ranges. The shorthand x^o(1) in the spectral estimate needs a
precise distinction: an external theorem's selectable exponent is
fixed BEFORE x tends to infinity; it is not a genuine vanishing o(1).
After fixing sigma and the finite polynomial scale bounds, choose all
such spectral/smoothing epsilons so that their accumulated fixed power
is at most sigma/20. Then take x large enough that the genuine vanishing
losses (divisors, D, coprime classes, logarithms, fixed counts) together
are at most sigma/20. The total allowance is sigma/10. If source-internal
losses were already absorbed when forming the weaker bare bound with
saving sigma_S, they are not multiplied in again; only the remaining
external losses are charged, bounded conservatively by the same total
allowance. This is an order of choices, not free epsilon absorption.
Every such loss multiplies a POWER-saving bound, never just a
logarithmic dispersion remainder. We obtain
  |E3(a;d)| << N^2/Q*x^(-sigma_S+5kappa+o(1)).              (12)
In (3), sum_(d~D)1/d=O(1); taking the uniform bound (12) therefore
introduces no D from the count of small factors. To express the result
in the D-normalized form used by outer dispersion, pay the one extra
factor D=x^o(1). Choose x large enough that ALL remaining subpower
losses, including this factor, are at most x^(sigma/10). Then
  sigma_S-5kappa-sigma/10 >= 3sigma/5 > sigma/2,
and hence
  |E2(a)| << N^2/(DQ)*x^(-sigma/2).                        (13)
The resulting contribution to dispersion is
  M sqrt(DQ) sqrt(|E2|) << x^(1-sigma/4),
before fixed external logarithms. This is only the E2 contribution;
no such exponent is asserted for preliminary D's zero-restoration
error or for the complete final discrepancy.

8. Boundaries of the conclusion

The connection is valid for the actual factorized, q-independent
Lambda_d of (2). It does not give S0 for arbitrary lambda_(q,d,w),
arithmetic restrictions on q, or rectangles whose endpoint changes
with a summation variable. It uses the real rough support of n,w to
partition coprime pairs; an arbitrary unprojected HB coefficient would
not automatically satisfy that partition hypothesis. Neither arbitrary
geometric inequalities nor signed compiler weights can simply be put
into the rectangle kernel: any earlier Mellin/finite-class separation
must already have supplied the stated one-variable coefficient form.

Within these limits, no extra E2 interface hypothesis is needed beyond
the bare coprime spectral input and the actual fixed-d array supplied
by dispersion. The two endpoint supplements and the weakened source
margin above close omissions in the concise earlier mapping. This
result alone cannot declare the full repaired dispersion theorem or
the project's global sieve constants proved.
