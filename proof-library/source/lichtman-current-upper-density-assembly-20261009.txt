0. Result and its logical scope

Write H_T=C_T T/log(T)^2, with the manuscript's singular-series
normalization, and let T tend to infinity through even integers. Relative
to the explicit classical/spectral inputs of the new restricted PP
derivation and the frozen outward numerical inequalities listed below,
the present construction supplies the following positive-count theorem
at K=21130429/3125000=6.76173728.

Fix a_star>0, k_star<infinity and a finite collection of labelled prime
product families. Each has at most k_star original prime slots, all
primes at least T^a_star, bounded nonnegative coefficients attached to
fixed labels, product at most T, and finitely many fixed monomial and
ordering constraints. Repeated factors may either be retained or
excluded. Their multiplicity is w_T(m), and suppose their actual
unsifted mass is X_T=(J+o(1))T/log T. Then

  sum_{m<T, T-m prime} w_T(m) <= (K J+o(1)) H_T.             (U)

An exact mass limit is not necessary: limsup (log T)X_T/T<=J
with finite J>=0 suffices for the corresponding limsup conclusion
in(U). This follows from nonnegative X_T and K>0, with the same
absolute error. In particular a nonnegative majorant with only an
upper mass estimate is admissible; no signed label coefficient may
be substituted for its nonnegative weight.

More generally the same conclusion applies directly to each fixed
bounded nonnegative smooth geometric weight with product O(T), replacing
J by its normalized mass, and to finite positive sums of these weights.
Sharp geometric domains are recovered by the positive radial smoothing
proved in Section 6 below. Constants depend on the fixed family,
cutoff, labels and finite policy; there is no uniform assertion as
a_star tends to zero, or for a policy that grows with T. No arbitrary
arithmetic filter on an outer prime or HB variable is allowed. A
restriction (m,T)=1 can be imposed by removing its power-small mass,
not by asserting a new SW property for filtered prime coefficients.

For the ordered bad-semiprime family r<=q, rq<T, r>q^b, with fixed
1/2<b<1, this implies limsup B_b(T)/H_T <= K log(1/b).
There is no extra factor two. The theorem (U), rather than only this
special case, is the positive input needed by finite switching actions.

6. From signed PP to a positive count and its radial mean

For a fixed smooth nonnegative W_T with p_i>=T^a_star/2, let X_W be
its unsifted prime tuple mass and X_W^(l) its exact coprime mass.
Squarefreeness, |rho|<=1 and support l<=T^theta_star give, for any primep,
  sum_{l:p|l}|rho_T(l)|/phi(l)
     <=(p-1)^(-1) sum_{e<=T^theta_star/p}1/phi(e)
     <<log T/(p-1).
Hence by a union bound over at most k_star slots,
  |sum_l rho_T(l)(X_W^(l)-X_W)/phi(l)|
       << X_W T^(-a_star)log T=o(H_T).
There is no extra T^theta_star factor in this mass removal. Repeated
prime slots cause only harmless duplicate terms in the union bound.

Apply (P) to n=T-product p_i BEFORE invoking distribution. For positive
prime n>=T^rho the rough indicator is one. Primes below T^rho cost at
most T^(rho+o(1)): each integer product has only a fixed-order divisor
multiplicity. This generalizes the unique ordered-factorization bound
used for bad P2. Divisor sums at n=0 or negative n remain nonnegative,
so outward smoothing creates no sign problem. The number of exact
product-T tuples is T^o(1); PP's proof separately accounts for this
case instead of ever using tau(0).

Products below 2T^(1-kappa0) need no near-linear-residue theorem.
For their absolute divisor-polynomial contribution, m!=T and
  sum_{m<2T^(1-kappa0)}tau_C(m)tau(|T-m|)
       <=T^(1-kappa0+o(1)).
The exact-coprime mean is bounded by their mass times O(log T).
A smooth product cutoff has bounded multiplicative derivatives on
dyadic charts. PP on the retained scales, with A larger than the
finite logarithmic partition losses, is o(H_T), uniformly in even T.
Combining PP, the preceding coprime removal and (R) bounds the positive
smooth count by (K J_W+o(1))H_T when X_W=(J_W+o(1))T/log T.
The mean is nonnegative because W is; positivity of arbitrary signed
atom remainders has never been asserted.

For sharp geometric families the appropriate coordinates are
 t_j=log(p_j)/log T (j<k), t_k*=1-sum_{j<k}t_j,
 y=product p_i/T.
Their limiting labelled mass is
  dmu_k=dt_1...dt_(k-1)dy/(t_1...t_(k-1)t_k*),
with dmu_1=dy, on a compact positive exponent simplex. Fix y>=epsilon
first. PNT for the last prime followed by reciprocal-prime partial
summation proves this measure uniformly; the region y<epsilon has
mass O(epsilon T/log T). A monomial wall with nonidentical exponents
is a proper affine wall of this simplex. A critical pure-product wall
is instead a radial wall y^lambda=c and must be smoothed in y.
Constant identities are retained. This handles the total-product
boundary, ordering, equalities and finite unions without declaring a
radial boundary null in the wrong exponent space.

For each fixed smoothing tolerance choose one global nonnegative
majorant whose excess normalized mass is at most that tolerance,
with all original primes >=T^a_star/2. Its normalized derivatives
