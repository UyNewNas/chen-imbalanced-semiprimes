1. Same coefficient and positive product mass

Let H_T=C_T*T/log(T)^2, with T even. For a fixed finite upper strategy
write R_T(n)=sum_{d|n}rho_T(d). Require all of the following for this SAME
rho_T: |rho_T(d)|<=1; squarefree support with (d,T)=1 and d<=T^theta,
theta<3/5; R_T(n)>=1_{(n,P_T(T^r0))=1} for every integer n including 0;
and sum_d rho_T(d)/phi(d)<=(K+o(1))*C_T/log T. Here r0<1 is fixed.
These are coefficient and density hypotheses, not consequences of PP.

Fix k_*, a_*>0 and finitely many nonnegative labelled geometric product
families, each with at most k_* prime slots, every prime >=c*T^a_*, total
product O(T), and fixed bounded label coefficients. The family may include
the canonical prime factorizations of a rough quotient, including its
unit quotient as a separate lower-dimensional family. It may not insert
an arbitrary arithmetic filter on an outer prime or an HB slot.

For a nonnegative smooth tuple weight W_T of such a family, let X_W be
its total unsifted mass, and X_W^(d) the mass with product coprime to d.
For every prime p,

  sum_{d:p|d} |rho_T(d)|/phi(d)
      <= (p-1)^(-1) sum_{e<=T^theta/p} 1/phi(e)
      << log T/(p-1).

Thus replacing the exact mean in PP by X_W costs
O_{a_*,k_*}(X_W*T^(-a_*)*log T), by the union bound over the actual slots.
It costs no factor T^theta. If X_W=O(T/log T), this is o(H_T).
Apply the pointwise nonnegative majorant first, then PP and this mean
replacement. If X_W=(J_W+o(1))*T/log T, the resulting upper count is
(K*J_W+o(1))*H_T. A limsup mass bound J_W<=J is sufficient as well.

For k>2 the small remaining prime 0<T-product<T^r0 contributes at most
T^(r0+o(1)), since a fixed number of labelled prime factorizations of one
integer is bounded by a fixed divisor function. This replaces the
special two-prime O(T^r0) observation in actual-k-positive-assembly.tex.
Likewise, products <2*T^(1-kappa0) cost T^(1-kappa0+o(1)) after the full
modulus sum, since the shifted integer is then nonzero. Exact products
equal to T in smoothed weights cause no sign reversal because R_T(0)>=0;
their treatment in the HB discrepancy is a distinct already stated step.

Sharp geometric families are approached by nonnegative smooth majorants
in simplex variables and product/T. Product equalities are treated in the
radial variable, and a unit quotient is explicitly retained, not declared
to be a zero-volume part of a higher-dimensional integral. After applying
the positive bound, let T grow, then let the fixed smoothing mass tend to
zero. Small boundary mass on its own is not a signed-discrepancy estimate.
This proves the general positive-product implication, conditional on the
same-rho upper hypotheses and the licensed PP statement. It is stronger
than just the bad-semiprime removal bound and is what the H/h bank needs.

2. Target-uniform normalization

Use precisely C_T=C_2*product_{p|T,p>2}(p-1)/(p-2). Direct multiplication
gives the identity in own-route-uniform-singular-series-20261009.tex:

 V_T(z)/(2*M(z)*C_T)
  = product_{p>=z}(1-1/(p-1)^2)^(-1)
    * product_{p|T,p>=z}(1-1/(p-1)),

where M(z)=product_{p<z}(1-1/p). Its logarithm is bounded in absolute value
by (1+log T/log z)/(z-2). Therefore for every fixed u>0, Mertens' theorem
implies V_T(T^u)=2*exp(-gamma)*C_T/(u*log T)*(1+o_u(1)), uniformly along
all even T. No exceptional target set or bounded number of prime factors
of T is assumed. Also C_T>=C_2>0.

For a marked squarefree prefix B with a fixed number of primes, each
>=T^a_*, phi(B)/B=1+o(1) and C_(BT)/C_T=1+o(1) uniformly on fixed boxes.
The primes dividing T can be removed from that tuple family with
O(T^(1-a_*+o(1))) mass; this is a deletion estimate, not a claim that an
arbitrarily filtered prime coefficient has the SW property.

At total exponent nu and marked exponent S, L=nu-S>0, take a nonnegative
marked family sigma(B)>=0 and a nonzero normalization Theta_Q>0. Empty
families give no term. The table mass is

 Theta_Q=4*li(T)*sum_B sigma(B)*C_(BT)/(phi(B)*log(Q/B)).

Its ratio to H_T tends to 4*integral dmu/L, where
dmu=product_i dt_i/t_i. Appending exponent L*u changes the normalized
child mass by du/[u*(1-u)], and appending L*u,L*v by
du*dv/[u*v*(1-u-v)]. These are the same factors in the actual H/h
recurrence; the global Chen factor 1/4 cancels the 4 once in U3/U4.
It would be incorrect either to divide their table difference by 4
again or to rescale K by the child's residual level.

For a W1 switched mass, let I be a fixed upper bound on its normalized
integral throughout the entire nonnegative marked family. Its actual
unsifted mass is then at most (I+o(1))*T*sum_B sigma(B)/(phi(B)*log(Q/B)).
The positive-product bound of part 1 divided by Theta_Q is at most
K*I/4+o(1), using C_T<=C_(BT). If the integral varies with B and no uniform
bound has been taken, retain its nonnegative weighted average instead;
it cannot simply be pulled out of the prefix sum. Actual finite boxes
use their uniform kernel bounds or this integrated version. The pointwise
W1 inequality has leading factor 2, producing the cost K*I/8. For W2
the corresponding factor 5 is applied once after assembling its ordinary
and switched terms. The unit surfaces in terms 20 and 21 have their own
prime-product masses and remain included.

3. Exact residual density is a necessary distinct input

The normalization in part 2 supplies no equality for a residual sieve
weight. The ordinary third/fourth/fifth replacements need the actual
raw Rosser densities V_T^+/V_T=F(s)+o(1) and V_T^-/V_T=f(s)+o(1).
Subtracting two one-sided sieve estimates cannot prove these formulas.

I read own-lower-compiler-chain-audit-20261009.tex, in particular its
positive first-exit comparison and exact subtree identities, and the
primary-interface note rosser-density-primary-interface-check-20261009.txt.
I also read the saved Suzuki primary text, pp8-9, 66-68 and 97-98:
the cubic raw support, exact positive-exit identity, convergence step,
and Lemma14.7's one-sided inequalities. The literal raw-density match
uses Suzuki, together with finite-depth prime harmonic limits and the
convergent positive series. Motohashi's presieved K_nu alone would not
identify the unpresieved raw sum and is not used for that purpose.

The moving-T reduction is particularly useful here. A surviving exit
term, normalized by V_T(y), is its fixed-g0 counterpart multiplied by
product_{q_last<=p<y,p|T}(1-g0(p)) in [0,1]. Other exits disappear.
Every fixed-rank last prime has a fixed positive exponent, so both
the deletion and this multiplier tend uniformly to their desired
limits. Sandwich first at fixed depth, then let the depth grow using
the classical convergent series. This yields the target-uniform
equalities, without interchanging an uncontrolled infinite sum with T.

For the upper residual in 1<s<2, use exact support invariance under
replacing y by R^(1/3), because the first upper cube forces p1^3<R.
The equality at s=3 and V_T(R^(1/3))/V_T(y)=3/s+o(1) then give
F(s)=2*exp(gamma)/s. No lower equality below s=2 is required.
Thus the ordinary telescope uses differences of actual multiplicative
density sums with their recorded parity, not subtraction of inequalities.

4. Common finite choices and absence of a table/K cycle

First fix the selected finite upper strategy domains and the lower
policy, the finite 106-action bank, 430 generations, and all occurrences
of the retained 248 or 430 arrays. Fix enough compact trims and coarse
boxes to meet the positive reserved approximation budgets. Every
admitted branch keeps its physical ceiling and has a positive exponent.
A fixed finite recursion can make the minimum exponent extremely small;
it cannot make it zero. It also has a finite maximum number of slots.

Apply part 1 at that fixed outer cutoff. Choose an upper realization
within the selected strict K allowance using its independent upper
tree; its density argument uses no H/h table. Then this K may be used
or weakened to an older larger coefficient in all switched terms.
The table induction now uses only the preceding generation and these
positive-product bounds. The current lower ordinary start is a
separate lower compiler, not a deduction from an upper K theorem.
This order has no directed cycle through the table.

There is no need that distinct ordinary table states use one common
modulus coefficient. Each application needs its own identical counting
and density coefficient. After fixing finitely many such applications,
choose the finite parameter precisions within their margins; the
maximum of their sufficiently-large-T thresholds is finite. Nor must
the same selected table replace every occurrence. The current policy
uses 430 only in U3/U4 and retains the earlier ordinary occurrences.

The new PP theorem is invoked at complete-product scale, with residue
T and its explicit restricted large-residue range, after its low tail
removal. An old citation to the original all-a Proposition5.2 is not
a substitute for this invocation. No new marked branch is admitted by
its support ceiling alone, and no finite maximum certifies an infinite
adaptive policy or a cutoff that tends to zero with T.
