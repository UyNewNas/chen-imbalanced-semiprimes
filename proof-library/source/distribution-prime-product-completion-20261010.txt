From the restricted medium estimate to the actual smooth prime-product estimate
10 October 2026. New proof; all earlier files and the manuscript remain unchanged.

Conclusion

This note proves the prime-product INSERTION, with the restricted medium
convolution estimate and the recorded finite whole-box licences as explicit
premises. It does not prove that medium estimate by naming it. The new
substantive step is a direct elementary Brun reduction for BOTH one-long
and two-long branches. It keeps the exact coprime mean, permits the actual
complex smooth profiles, and handles sharp individual intervals without a
moving-shift divisor moment, a product=T exception, or freezing a small
sieve divisor. Its smooth cores are uniform in every primitive residue.
The resulting long-branch argument needs no fundamental lemma of the sieve.

The physical upper/lower compiler still has its own fundamental-lemma and
density obligations. The elementary q=1 shifted moments are still used for
the medium beta truncation and the geometric Fourier tail. No global
K,c,b, density allowance, numerical table, or selected domain changes here.
The older common kappa cap, including the now unnecessary long-boundary
orders, may be retained unchanged; this note does not enlarge that range.

6. Explicit elementary Brun weights, uniform in deleted modulus primes

The next construction applies to either long alternative. Write L2=loglog x,
h=2 floor(L2^2), z=Zchart and P(z)=product_(p<=z)p, using the same endpoint
convention as the rough projector. Put
 lambda^+(d)=mu(d)*1_(d|P(z),omega(d)<=h),
 lambda^-(d)=mu(d)*1_(d|P(z),omega(d)<=h+1),
 y=z^(h+1)=x^o(1),
 U_z(n)=sum_(d|n)lambda^+(d), L_z(n)=sum_(d|n)lambda^-(d),
 G_z(n)=U_z(n)-L_z(n), r_z(n)=1_((n,P(z))=1).
The coefficients are one-bounded and d<=y. For s=omega(gcd(n,P(z))),
the finite alternating binomial identity gives
 L_z(n)<=r_z(n)<=U_z(n), U_z(n)>=0,
 G_z(n)=binom(s,h+1)>=0.                                  (B1)
This includes s=0, when both polynomials equal one. L_z may be negative;
its positivity is NEVER assumed.

For every q define
 A_q=sum_((d,q)=1)lambda^+(d)/d,
 g_q=sum_((d,q)=1)(lambda^+(d)-lambda^-(d))/d,
 V_q=product_(p<=z,p not|q)(1-1/p).
Finite inclusion-exclusion (equivalently Bonferroni for independent
Bernoulli variables with probabilities 1/p) gives
       0<=A_q-V_q<=g_q<=H(z)^(h+1)/(h+1)!=:epsilon_x,       (B2)
where H(z)=sum_(p<=z)1/p. This is UNIFORM in q, regardless of its factors.
One elementary bound is H(z)<=e*log(1+log z), by comparison with
log zeta(1+1/log z). Thus epsilon_x<=exp(-c*L2^2*log L2) eventually,
while log y/log x<=2*(h+1)/L2^3=O(1/L2). In particular A_q<=2 eventually.
No fundamental lemma, distribution theorem or growing divisor-moment
order is required for (B1)--(B2). The growing h is paid only through
y=x^o(1) in POWER-saving remainders; not through a log-saving term.

7. Two smooth cores with the EXACT coprime mean

Let alpha_m be a fixed complex short sequence with
 sum|alpha_m|<<M*log^B x; write the complete scale as x~M*U (one long)
or x~M*U*V (two long). Let f,g be compactly supported C^2 profiles on
[1/2,5/2] whose relevant norms are fixed logarithmic powers. For any
(a,q)=1 and fixed m with (m,q)>1 both discrepancy terms vanish.

For one long, ordinary progression counting and Mobius inversion give
 sum_(n=b mod q) f(n/U)=(U/q)*integral f+O(N1(f)),
 sum_((n,q)=1) f(n/U)=U*phi(q)/q*integral f+O(tau(q)*N1(f)),
where N1(f)=||f||_1+||f'||_1. The exact discrepancy for each fixed m is
therefore O(N1(f)*(1+tau(q)/phi(q))), uniformly in U and primitive b.
This applies at U/d, with m replaced by m*d; nonunits again vanish.

For two long, write fhat(t)=integral f(s)e(-ts)ds. Double periodization
gives the EXACT fixed-m discrepancy
 U*V/q^2 sum_(j,k in Z) fhat(jU/q)ghat(kV/q)
 [S(a*m^(-1)*j,k;q)-c_q(j)c_q(k)/phi(q)].                  (P1)
Both coordinate axes vanish identically, including their nonzero Fourier
masses: S(0,k)=c_q(k), S(bj,0)=c_q(j) for primitive b, c_q(0)=phi(q).
For nonzero frequencies use the composite Weil bound
 |S(bj,k;q)|<=tau(q)*sqrt(q)*gcd(j,k,q)^(1/2)
and |fhat(t)|<<N2(f)*(1+|t|)^(-2), N2(f)=||f||_1+||f''||_1.
The elementary sum
 sum_(j!=0,d|j)(1+|j|/H)^(-2)<=2H/d
holds for all H>0. Expanding the gcd through divisors gives the uniform
fixed-m bound
 N2(f)N2(g)*[sqrt(q)*tau(q)+tau(q)^2/phi(q)].              (P2)
The first term uses sum_(d|q)d^(-3/2)<=zeta(3/2); the second uses
|c_q(j)|<=gcd(j,q) and sum_(d|q)phi(d)/d<=tau(q).
For q=1 the discrepancy is exactly zero. There is NO size bound on a.

If |rho(q)|<=tau(q)^J*log^B1 x and q<=D, summing fixed divisor means
turns these smooth core bounds into
 one long: O(M*D*log^C x),
 two long: O(M*D^(3/2)*log^C x).                           (P3)
For fixed d_1,d_2<=y the profiles are exactly f(n_1/(U/d_1)),
g(n_2/(V/d_2)); their norms in (P2) are unchanged. The short product
becomes m*d_1*d_2 only inside the primitive residue. The core was proved
pointwise in that product, so its L1 mass is still sum|alpha_m|. Sum the
d pairs by their number, at most y^2. No dyadic freezing, new common
interval, or regrouped high-order divisor sequence is required. Thus
the expanded two-variable Brun errors cost M*y^2*D^(3/2)*log^C x.
The product-divisor support is d_1*d_2<=y^2=x^o(1), explicitly retained.

8. Passing from these smooth cores to rough smooth variables

First, for any nonnegative smooth F and any q, exact Mobius inversion gives
 sum_((n,q)=1) F(n/U)*U_z(n)
 =U*phi(q)/q*(integral F)*A_q+O(y*tau(q)*N1(F)),
 sum_((n,q)=1) F(n/U)*G_z(n)
 =U*phi(q)/q*(integral F)*g_q+O(y*tau(q)*N1(F)).            (E1)
The left sides are nonnegative by (B1); the second upper bound uses
g_q<=epsilon_x. This is a comparison of EXACT coprime sums, not a
replacement by an unrestricted Euler mean. The factor phi(q)/q cancels
against the exact mean's 1/phi(q) where appropriate.

For two variables abbreviate U_i=U_z(n_i), L_i=L_z(n_i), r_i=r_z(n_i),
G_i=U_i-L_i. The valid product sandwich is
 L_1*U_2+U_1*L_2-U_1*U_2 <= r_1*r_2 <= U_1*U_2,
 0<=U_1*U_2-r_1*r_2<=B:=G_1*U_2+U_1*G_2.                (E2)
Indeed U_i>=r_i>=0 and U_i-r_i<=G_i. This proves (E2) even if either
L_i is negative; using L_1*L_2 as a lower bound would be invalid.

For complex f,g choose fixed nonnegative smooth majorants F>=|f|,
G>=|g| with fixed polylog norms, and replace alpha by |alpha| only in
the following absolute error bound. Write C_q and M_q for the counting
and exact coprime-mean functionals, respectively, and Delta_q=C_q-M_q.
Then
 |Delta_q(alpha*f*g*r_1*r_2)-Delta_q(alpha*f*g*U_1*U_2)|
 <= C_q(|alpha|*F*G*B)+M_q(|alpha|*F*G*B)
 <= |Delta_q(|alpha|*F*G*B)|+2*M_q(|alpha|*F*G*B).         (E3)
Every count on the right is nonnegative; no order on a complex sequence
has been asserted. Expanding U_1*U_2 and B into their d pairs uses (P3)
with at most y^2 terms and coefficients of bounded absolute value.

Use (E1) for each factor of the mean in (E3) and then
sum_(q<=D)|rho(q)|*tau(q)^j/phi(q)<<log^C x for each FIXED j. The complete
rough smooth two-variable discrepancy is bounded by
 O(log^C x)*[M*U*V*epsilon_x
             +M*(U+V)*y+M*y^2+M*y^2*D^(3/2)].             (E4)
For one variable the same argument with 0<=U_z-r_z<=G_z gives
 O(log^C x)*[M*U*epsilon_x+M*y+M*y*D].                     (E5)
Here C depends on fixed profile/divisor norms, not on h. The epsilon_x
term is multiplied only by fixed logarithmic factors. All y factors
occur in errors with a fixed power reserve. In the actual alternatives
M<=x^(20*zeta) or x^(40*zeta), min(U,V)>=x^nu for fixed nu>0, and (iv)
therefore makes (E4)--(E5) O_A(x/log^A x) for every fixed A.
