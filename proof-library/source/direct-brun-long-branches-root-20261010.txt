Direct Brun treatment of one/two long variables: independent root derivation
10 October 2026. New local proof; numerical and physical sieve policies unchanged.

Purpose and scope

The prime-product insertion agent proposed replacing the two-long roughness
removal by explicit Brun polynomials. I independently derive the replacement
below, including negative lower weights, exact coprime means, interval edges,
and the one-long extension. The Poisson identity is also given, so the remaining
non-elementary input at this step is the classical complete Kloosterman Weil
bound (plus the elementary prime reciprocal bound). No shifted divisor moment,
fundamental-lemma error estimate, or small-divisor freezing is used here.
This is only the no-medium branch of PP, not its medium spectral theorem or
the separate physical small-prime compiler.

1. Explicit polynomials and uniform density gaps

Put L=log log x and fix the common chart threshold z with
 log z <= 2 log x/L^3, z->infinity.
Let h=2 floor(L^2), r=h+1, y=z^r=x^o(1), and P=prod_(p<=z)p.
For squarefree divisors d of P define
 beta+(d)=mu(d)1_(omega(d)<=h), beta-(d)=mu(d)1_(omega(d)<=r),
and zero otherwise. Both supports lie in [1,y], with |beta|<=1.
For a positive integer n define U(n)=sum_(d|n)beta+(d),
Lw(n)=sum_(d|n)beta-(d), I(n)=1_((n,P)=1), G(n)=U(n)-Lw(n).

If k=omega(gcd(n,P)), the alternating binomial partial sums give
 Lw(n)<=I(n)<=U(n), U(n)>=0, G(n)=binom(k,r)>=0.            (1)
At k=0 both polynomials equal1; for k>0 the partial sum through j is
(-1)^j binom(k-1,j), interpreted as zero when j>=k. This proves (1),
including the fact that Lw is allowed to be negative.

For any q, put V_q=prod_(p<=z,p does not divide q)(1-1/p) and
B_q^+=sum_((d,q)=1)beta+(d)/d, B_q^-=sum_((d,q)=1)beta-(d)/d.
Apply (1) to independent Bernoulli variables of means 1/p. It yields
 0<=B_q^+-V_q<=B_q^+-B_q^- = e_r({1/p:p<=z,p not dividing q})
                    <=H(z)^r/r! =:epsilon_x,              (2)
where H(z)=sum_(p<=z)1/p. In particular B_q^+<=1+epsilon_x and the gap
is nonnegative, uniformly in every deletion set of q-primes. No theorem
with constants depending on the number or sizes of those primes is used.
The prime harmonic estimate gives H(z)<=2L eventually. Since r~2L^2,
r!>=(r/e)^r shows epsilon_x <=exp(-c L^2 log L) for a fixed c>0,
eventually. It is smaller than log(x)^(-A) for every fixed A.

2. Smooth progression and exact coprime means

Let f be a compactly supported smooth profile on a fixed positive interval,
with N1(f)=||f||_1+||f'||_1 and N2(f)=||f||_1+||f''||_1.
One-dimensional summation in a residue class and Mobius inversion give
 sum_(n=b mod q)f(n/U)=(U/q) int f+O(N1(f)),
 sum_((n,q)=1)f(n/U)=U*(phi(q)/q) int f+O(tau(q)N1(f)).      (3)
The constants are independent of the primitive class b and all positive U.
The first estimate needs no coprimality; that condition is used in solving
the actual congruence after a sieve divisor is inserted.

For B=U or G, expand its at most y divisor coefficients. Terms with
(d,q)>1 vanish from the coprime sum. Equation (3) then gives
 S_B(f):=sum_((n,q)=1)f(n/U)B(n)
 =U*(phi(q)/q)*b_q int f+O(y*tau(q)*N1(f)),                 (4)
where b_q=B_q^+ for U, and b_q=B_q^+-B_q^- for G. For nonnegative f,
these sums are nonnegative. Thus (2) bounds their main terms by respectively
(1+epsilon_x)U phi(q)/q int f and epsilon_x U phi(q)/q int f.
Equation (4) is about the exact coprime sum, not an assumed density for a
different coefficient. Every finite expansion is legal although h grows.

3. One long variable

Let a be any integer, (q,a)=1. A short coefficient alpha_m has
sum_m |alpha_m|<=Z log^C x, on a fixed-multiple interval at scale Z;
put x=ZU up to fixed constants. For smooth f consider the discrepancy
 E_q(I)=sum_(m,n)alpha_m f(n/U)I(n)
             [1_(mn=a mod q)-1_((mn,q)=1)/phi(q)].
If (m,q)>1 both indicators vanish. Otherwise every inserted (d,q)=1
leaves one primitive class for n/d. Equation (3) cancels the two main
terms exactly, with error O((1+tau(q)/phi(q))*N1(f)) per d and m.
Consequently expansion of U or G costs
 O(Z*y*log^C x*(1+tau(q)/phi(q))*N1(f)).                    (5)

For a complex f choose a nonnegative smooth majorant F>=|f| of fixed support
and polylogarithmic N1 norm. The difference between I and U has magnitude
at most G. Its congruence sum is at most its exact coprime mean plus the
absolute discrepancy of G with |alpha|,F. Formula (4) bounds that mean;
use (5) for the discrepancy. Thus, for |rho(q)|<=tau(q)^J log^C x,
 sum_(q<=D,(q,a)=1)|rho(q)E_q(I)|
 << epsilon_x*x*log^C1 x + Z*y*D*log^C1 x.                (6)
Ordinary divisor/totient MEANS absorb all fixed powers; epsilon_x is not
multiplied by a pointwise x^o(1). Here and below C1 can increase by a fixed
amount for derivative norms or short coefficient bounds.
For Z<=x^(20*zeta), D<=x^Theta, Theta<3/5, (6) has arbitrary logarithmic
saving, since 20*zeta+Theta<1 with the actual zeta<=10^-6.

4. Two long variables: the smooth core

Put x=ZUV up to fixed constants. For any fixed m coprime to q let b=a/m mod q.
Double periodization gives the exact smooth discrepancy
 UV/q^2 sum_(j,k in Z) fhat(jU/q)ghat(kV/q)
       [S(bj,k;q)-c_q(j)c_q(k)/phi(q)].                    (7)
This includes the Fourier expansion of the exact coprime mean. All terms
with j=0 OR k=0 vanish because b is a unit. For q=1 the discrepancy is zero.
For q>=2 use complete Weil,
 |S(bj,k;q)|<=tau(q)*sqrt(q)*gcd(j,k,q)^(1/2),
and |c_q(j)|<=gcd(j,q). Integration by parts twice gives
 |fhat(t)|<<N2(f)(1+|t|)^(-2).
For every H>0 and d>=1,
 sum_(j!=0,d|j)(1+|j|/H)^(-2)<=2H/d.
The common-divisor sums are bounded by sum_(d|q)d^(-3/2)<=zeta(3/2)
and sum_(d|q)phi(d)/d<=tau(q), respectively. Therefore (7) is
 <<N2(f)N2(g)[sqrt(q)tau(q)+tau(q)^2/phi(q)].              (8)
This is uniform in ALL positive scales U,V and primitive residues a.
There is no frequency cutoff, discarded real phase or exact-product exception.

On inserting d1|u,d2|v, use scales U/d1,V/d2 in (8) and replace m by md1d2.
If that replacement is not coprime to q both indicators vanish; otherwise
(8) applies unchanged. No freezing of d1,d2 is required. Sum the at most y^2
pairs absolutely and then use divisor means in q. Each expanded pair of
Brun polynomials has total discrepancy
 << Z*y^2*D^(3/2)*log^C2 x.                               (9)
The x^o(1) factor y^2 multiplies only this fixed-power-saving term.

5. Two rough indicators, including a possibly negative lower polynomial

By (1), for I_i=I(n_i), U_i=U(n_i), G_i=G(n_i),
 P^+=U_1 U_2 >= I_1 I_2 >= P^-=U_1 U_2-G_1 U_2-U_1 G_2. (10)
For example U_1U_2-I_1I_2=(U_1-I_1)U_2+I_1(U_2-I_2), which lies between
zero and G_1U_2+U_1G_2. This proves (10) without assuming Lw_i>=0.
For complex f,g and alpha, use nonnegative smooth majorants F>=|f|,Gf>=|g|
and |alpha| to control the gap. Its exact coprime mean is bounded by
 [S_G(F)S_U(Gf)+S_U(F)S_G(Gf)]/phi(q),
times the short absolute mass. From (2),(4), summing |rho(q)| gives
 O(epsilon_x*x*log^C3 x
   +Z*(U+V)*y*log^C3 x+Z*y^2*log^C3 x).                  (11)
In deriving this, the main product has phi(q)/q^2<=1/q; cross terms use
tau(q)/q and the final error product uses tau(q)^2/phi(q). Their sums cost
only fixed logarithms. The congruence gap is its exact mean plus the
expanded discrepancy, bounded by (9). Triangle inequalities now yield
 sum_(q<=D,(q,a)=1)|rho(q)E_q(I_1 I_2)|
 << epsilon_x*x*log^C4 x
    +[Z*y^2*D^(3/2)+Z*(U+V)*y+Z*y^2]*log^C4 x.            (12)
There is no unlicensed estimate for a signed rough function. Positivity is
used only for the explicit gap majorants, before their finite expansion.

For actual no-medium HB blocks, Z<=x^(40*zeta), min(U,V)>=x^nu for a fixed
nu>0, and D<=x^Theta, Theta<3/5. The leading power exponent is at most
40*zeta+3Theta/2+o(1)<0.90004+o(1); the two cross terms are
x/min(U,V)*x^o(1) and Z*x^o(1). All are fixed power savings. Thus (12)
has arbitrary logarithmic saving. This holds even if a equals an intermediate
product: no divisor of zero was introduced. No assumption on the size of a
is needed in this branch beyond primitive coprimality for the summed modulus.

6. Sharp individual intervals and what is actually replaced

For an interval within a fixed positive scale, replace its indicator by a
smooth cutoff differing only on two normalized strips of width
delta=log(x)^(-B). Choose positive smooth outer strip envelopes with
integral O(delta), N1=O(1), N2=O(delta^(-1)), and fixed higher derivatives
bounded by powers of delta^(-1). Twists/log factors multiply these by fixed
logarithmic norms. Positive U or U_1U_2 bounds the rough strip count.
Equations (3)--(4) and (8)--(9) give its exact mean/main mass
O(delta*x*log^C x), with the same fixed-power errors times logarithms.
The exponent C in this boundary MAIN term is independent of B: it uses
envelope amplitudes/integrals, short mass and divisor means. B-dependent
derivative norms occur only in the fixed-power errors. Choose B after the
requested error exponent and those fixed main-term orders.
This proves the interval extension by positive boundary estimates. It does
not approximate an arbitrary sharp COUPLED domain by separated smooth weights.

Consequently the actual one/two-long branch needs neither the old fundamental-
lemma roughness removal nor small-divisor freezing, shifted-moment boundary
Cauchy, or separate removal of intermediate product=a. Its internal rank h
grows slowly only in auxiliary Brun weights; this is not a growing physical
modulus tree or a new numerical density policy. All its coefficients are
handled explicitly by support size y=x^o(1) and uniform gap (2).
The q=1 elementary moments remain needed for other steps, notably medium
beta truncation and global Mellin tails. Keep the previously certified common
kappa/kappa0 cap, including its former boundary moment order; a redundant
stricter bound is harmless. No relaxation of K, c, b or a physical cap follows.
