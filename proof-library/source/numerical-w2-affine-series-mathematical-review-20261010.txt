1. Product-tail inequality, including signs and degrees

On u=m+h*z, write the true signed log sum L(z), its true degree32
polynomial P(z), and the geometric reciprocal polynomial Q(z). The code's
directed intervals enclose their coefficients. If r_i=B_i*h/(A_i+B_i*m),
endpoint positivity implies |r_i|<1, including negative B_i. Absolute
series summation gives
 E_L=sum |c_i|*|r_i|^33/[33(1-|r_i|)].
Also ||P||<=sum |c_i|*(|log(v_i)|+|r_i|/(1-|r_i|))=:M_L.
The saved interval upper absolute value of log(v_i) safely replaces the
unknown exact absolute value. There is no need to bound the norm of an
arbitrarily rounded representative polynomial: the interval evaluation
encloses the true polynomial integral itself.

For s=D*h/(C+D*m),
 L/(1+s*z)-P*Q=(L-P)/(1+s*z)+P*(1/(1+s*z)-Q).
Taking absolute values gives exactly
 E_L/(1-|s|)+M_L*|s|^33/(1-|s|).
Multiplication by2h/(C+D*m) bounds the full panel integral error. It is
added outwards to BOTH endpoints. Using signed c_i without absolute values
in E_L would be invalid, but the implementation correctly uses abs(c_i).
The independent uniform remainder does not rely on alternating signs.

The nested loops retain every i,j between0 and32 with i+j even and multiply
by2/(i+j+1). Thus they integrate the FULL degree64 product, rather than
silently dropping terms above degree32. Odd moments are exactly zero. The
two denominator terms 1/u and1/(1-u) are integrated and bounded separately.
Their addition is exact partial fractions, not an estimated decomposition.

2. Outward arithmetic and logarithm

I checked every arithmetic method in V. Four-endpoint multiplication with
floor(min/P),ceil(max/P) is valid across zero and for negative products.
Division by a positive rational preserves those directions; division by
a negative rational negates the result. Reciprocal interval construction
is used only when its entire denominator is positive. Powers are repeated
interval products and may be broad, but are enclosures. radius_add uses
the upward integer rounding of the analytic error. It never removes a
negative error or rounds to nearest.

unit_log sums z^k/k, k=1..256, with z=1-1/x in[0,1/2]. After the loop
the variable power encloses z^257. Dividing its upper endpoint by
257*(1-z_upper) is the correct remaining tail. Lower powers and summands
are rounded down. Dyadic scaling and negative-log inversion swap the
correct endpoints. No precision assumption is inferred from floats.
