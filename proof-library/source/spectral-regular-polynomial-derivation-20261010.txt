An explicit regular-spectrum summation and the actual S=1 polynomial
10 October 2026. New local derivation; existing proofs and constants unchanged.

Purpose and exact boundary

The prior packet review displayed polynomial (K), but treated the regular,
holomorphic and continuous pieces through a brief reference to spectral
large sieve and Bessel decay. Here that summation is written explicitly,
then combined with the already stated exceptional F/J norms to derive (K).
The result is for the actual coprime q0=1, S=1 class only. It is not a new
proof of the classical trace formula, oldform or continuous-fiber input,
the full moving-residue dispersion argument, or a global prime theorem.

Primary sources read: Pascadi arXiv:2404.04239v3, Proposition E, Lemmas D
and H, Theorems A and J; and the retained primary L23 text, Theorem8.2,
Lemma8.3, equations(10.7)--(10.14). The newer Pascadi statements provide
the classical regular input independently of any disputed moving-a step.
https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04239v3
No claim is made to have independently proved DI's original spectral theory.

1. Data and precise already available packet input

Let a be a nonzero integer, r~R with (a,r)=1, and use the cusps infinity
and 1 of level r. Thus mu=1/r at both cusps. The m-side is a common
interval I contained in (M,2M], optionally with a common additive phase;
its squared coefficient norm is <=O(M). The n-side b(n,r) is arbitrary
complex and supported on n~N. All nonempty dyadic scales are >=1/2.

For h|a^infinity, h<=2N, put L=N/h, and let b_h be the restricted,
phase-modified coefficient on n~L from the coprime packet argument.
Write B_h^2=sum_(r,n)|b_h(n,r)|^2 and B^2=sum_(r,n)|b(n,r)|^2.
In particular B_h<=B. These coefficients do not depend on the individual
eigenvector inside a packet. The already proved packet operation costs
(|a|h)^(Theta/2), with Theta=7/32, up to independently selectable
subpower/divisor factors, and leaves the m functional unchanged.

The finite oldform norm identity is the one in
lichtman-spectral-packet-adversarial-20261009.txt. For the continuous
spectrum its corresponding character-pair fiber identity with Plancherel
measure is an explicit external ABL input. Eisenstein functions are not
being treated as L2 cusp-form vectors. Holomorphic normalization includes
the standard weight factors, as in Pascadi Lemma H. This note does not
remove or independently prove those packet/fiber inputs.

It is enough to treat an elementary separated smooth Bessel amplitude.
Put
  X=C sqrt(R)/(4*pi*sqrt(|a|MN)), Y=1/X,
and let its test function phi have support y~Y and normalized derivative
bounds |phi^(j)(y)|<=C_j Y^(-j). Overall fixed amplitude seminorms multiply
the resulting bound. The full smooth-amplitude separation and its frequency
seminorm summability are a separate interface, not inferred here.

2. An explicit abstract spectral-shell estimate

For a regular spectrum, including its appropriate positive measure, let
A and B be two coefficient functionals with cumulative squared norms
bounded at height K>=1 by
  C(K^2+u) A0^2,  C(K^2+v) B0^2.
The standard logarithmic/subpower factors can be kept outside this lemma.
Suppose the transform has the following two absolute bounds:
  |H(t)| << (1+|log Y|)/(1+Y),
  |H(t)| << |t|^(-5/2)+Y|t|^(-3), for |t|>=max(2Y,1).
These weaker forms suffice; they are covered by the cited Bessel bounds.
At t=0 use continuity of the integral transform, not a division by t.

Then
  integral |H(t) A(t) B(t)| dmu(t)
  << (1+|log Y|)/(1+Y)
       sqrt((1+Y^2+u)(1+Y^2+v)) A0 B0.                 (R1)

Proof. Set K0=max(1,2Y). On |t|<=K0 use the uniform transform bound
and Cauchy--Schwarz with the two cumulative norms. On the j-th higher
shell, whose upper height is comparable to 2^j K0, the square root of
the two norm factors is at most
  O(2^(2j)) sqrt((K0^2+u)(K0^2+v)).
The transform supremum therefore produces the summable geometric series
  sum_(j>=0) [K0^(-5/2) 2^(-j/2)+Y K0^(-3) 2^(-j)]
  << K0^(-5/2)+Y K0^(-3) << 1/(1+Y).
Since K0^2 is comparable to 1+Y^2, this proves (R1). The argument uses
positive cumulative norms, not a pointwise eigenfunction bound, and works
for discrete sums, the continuous Plancherel integral, and the holomorphic
weight sum with its stated normalization. For holomorphic weight k use
height k-1; k and 1+|k-1| are comparable, affecting only absolute constants.

This calculation displays the complete high-frequency summation. It does
not multiply a divergent absolute sum of spectral coefficients by a
uniform transform bound, nor introduce a hidden power of |a| or X.

3. Regular contribution at the actual arithmetic scales

At fixed r~R, apply Pascadi Lemma H at both cusps. For the h term take
u=M/R and v=L/R. Its two coefficient norms are O(sqrt(M)) and
(sum_n |b_h(n,r)|^2)^(1/2). Formula(R1), then Cauchy in the r sum,
gives, after restoring the outside Kuznetsov factor C sqrt(R),
  |L_reg,h|
  << x^epsilon (|a|h)^(Theta/2)
       C sqrt(M)/(1+Y)
       sqrt((R+M+RY^2)(R+L+RY^2)) B_h.                (R2)
The log in(R1) is absorbed only because all scales, including |a| and
their nonzero inverses, are bounded by fixed powers of the ambient x.

The r sum is responsible for exactly sqrt(R), not R: sum_r B_h(r)
<=O(sqrt(R)) B_h. Combined with the two factors 1/sqrt(R) in the
large-sieve expressions and the outside C sqrt(R), this yields (R2).
In particular the finite number of r=1 or other bounded levels causes
no singularity; the same positive-norm estimates cover them.

Up to absolute constants RY^2=|a|MN/C^2. Put T0=|a|MN/C^2. Also
  C^2 M/(1+Y)^2 << M C^4 R/(|a|MN+C^2 R)=:P0.
Squaring the h sum costs at most x^epsilon, because the number of
h<=2N with h|a^infinity is x^epsilon for every prescribed epsilon,
uniformly when |a|,N<=x^B0 with fixed B0. This is the previously explicit
Rankin bound, with its epsilon chosen before x. Consequently
  |L_reg|^2
  << x^epsilon P0 sum_h (|a|h)^Theta
       (L+R+T0)(M+R+T0) B_h^2.                         (R3)
The same expression bounds the normalized holomorphic and continuous
contributions under the explicit packet/fiber inputs in section1.
No assertion about their individual basis vectors is used.

4. Exceptional contribution, keeping the packet power visible

Use tau_j=2*sqrt(1/4-lambda_j), so 0<tau_j<=Theta. This is twice the
Ramanujan exponent used in some primary-source conventions. The positive
cosh factor stays bounded and may be absorbed in constants.
Set X0=1+R/L. The valid elementary split is
  1+X^tau <= 2(1+X/sqrt(X0))^tau (1+X0)^(tau/2).
It retains an absolute constant omitted in a literal source line.

The already derived exceptional norms F1/J2 are
  S_n << x^epsilon (1+L/R) B_h^2,
  S_m << x^epsilon M(M+R)
       [1+{M(1+X/sqrt(X0))^2/(M+R)^2}^Theta].
F1 is applied separately to arbitrary level-dependent n coefficients;
J2 is applied to the unchanged common interval/phase on m. Its use would
fail for arbitrary level-dependent m intervals. Here they are excluded.

Since all nonempty scales are at least1/2, the bracket in S_m is bounded
by a constant times
  1+[C^2 R/(|a|h (L+R)(M+R)^2)]^Theta.
Multiply by the squared packet cost (|a|h)^Theta, by S_n and by the
squared outside factor C^2 R/(1+Y)^2. The resulting two terms are
  P0 (|a|h)^Theta (L+R)(M+R) B_h^2,
  P0 (C^2 R)^Theta (L+R)^(1-Theta)(M+R)^(1-2Theta) B_h^2.
Thus the cancellation of |a|h in the improved exceptional term happens
only after both positive norm bounds and the packet cost are present.
It is not a small-a assumption or a deletion of a cusp phase.

Sum h with a separately selected subpower loss, and use L<=N, B_h<=B,
1-Theta>0. The first term is absorbed in(R3). The result is
  |L_total|^2 << x^epsilon P0 * {
     sum_h (|a|h)^Theta (N/h+R+T0)(M+R+T0) B_h^2
     +(C^2 R)^Theta (N+R)^(1-Theta)(M+R)^(1-2Theta) B^2 }.
                                                               (K*)
This is the actual S=1 polynomial(K), now with its regular spectral-shell
summation and exceptional multiplication exposed separately. Harmless
absolute constants from 4*pi and dyadic supports are included in <<.

5. Consequences and boundaries

No step above sets |a|/x to zero or assumes |a|<=x^epsilon. Subsequent
Poisson completion and the actual coefficient norm create the retained
(1+|a|/x)D1 contribution in Lemma5.1; this note neither removes that term
nor reruns the later dispersion algebra. The original arbitrary-q0,
noncoprime-residue or arbitrary-level-interval versions are not proved.

This is a local expansion of a previously abbreviated argument, not a
new numerical improvement or independent proof of the oldform, continuous
fiber, classical Kuznetsov, Bessel-transform and spectral-sieve theorems.
It makes the exact regular input and its summation usable without invoking
the entire printed L23 Theorem10.2 as a black box. Scope outside(K*) remains
explicit: smooth separation, elementary completion, arithmetic coefficient
norms, retained-phase variation, preliminary dispersion, finite sieve
weights and all numerical certificates.

No main manuscript, frozen old file, selected constant, formalization,
GitHub state or external message is changed by this note.
