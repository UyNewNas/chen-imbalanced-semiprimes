Actual prime-product reduction to the repaired convolution estimate
2026-10-09. New proof supplement; existing bound artifacts are unchanged.

Scope

This note reconstructs the elementary HB decomposition and error-removal
parts of the actual project insertion. It is a CONDITIONAL reduction to
the explicitly named distribution inputs below, not a restoration of K,c,b.
In particular, a statement that a fixed modulus weight has the necessary
three-factor representation must be proved for that weight separately.

Write T for the even target, x for the complete local integer-product
scale, theta=7/32, sigma for the repaired convolution saving parameter,
and z(x)=x^(1/(loglog x)^3). Fix a>0 and k<=k_star, with k_star fixed.
Every original prime group has scale R_i>>T^a, the complete product is
O(T), and the original weight is smooth with fixed polylogarithmic
bounds on normalized derivatives. Its support can impose fixed
geometric conditions on the original groups, but not an extra arithmetic
residue restriction on one of the primes. A finite family is handled by
using the minimum positive cutoff and common fixed parameter choices.

For each target there is one fixed signed modulus coefficient rho_T(l),
coprime to T and supported on l<=T^theta_star, theta_star<3/5. Its
absolute value is bounded by a fixed divisor power; the actual compiled
coefficients are more restrictive. At high rough rank, after removal of
rough square collisions, assume the exact finite atom representation
and a fixed slot partition on each atom described in the modulus
insertion review. Its three factors are one-bounded after fixed
factorial normalization and meet all four repaired monomial conditions
for each selected medium scale. At low rough rank, assume instead the
complete support has a fixed gap below x^(1/2), and use the actual
compiled coefficient's uniform bound |rho_T|<=1 (a fixed constant also
suffices). An arbitrary divisor-bounded modulus weight would require
a separately supplied weighted BV extension; it is not inferred by
multiplying an unweighted BV remainder by x^o(1). No factorability is
inferred from a support bound alone, and no modulus partition may be
chosen in response to the observed progression-error signs.

The analytic inputs still to be supplied at their stated scope are:
 (SW) full auxiliary, interval/twist-uniform SW for individual rough
      1 and rough mu on scales U>=x^(2*zeta), uniformly for thresholds
      between z(x) and x^(2/(loglog x)^3);
 (P) the restricted convolution repair, including its reviewed divisor-
     bounded alpha,beta extension and the original modulus hypotheses;
 (L) the one- and two-long estimates with their actual roughness,
     interval/twist conditions and primitive coprime means;
 (BV) classical/generalized BV in the strictly-below-half range.
The separate HB reviews address SW, L and BV. This note does not certify
them merely by naming them. Its elementary reductions are proved below.

1. Exact order-three identity, at each ORIGINAL group scale

For one group n in [R,2R], put Z=(2R)^(1/3), and let mu_Z(n) be mu(n)
for n<=Z and zero otherwise. All operations in this paragraph are
Dirichlet convolution, with identity delta. Since 1*Lambda=log, set
E=delta-1*mu_Z=1*(mu-mu_Z). The support of E is contained in n>Z.
Thus Lambda*E*E*E vanishes for n<=Z^3. Expanding the cube gives the
exact identity, on that range,

 Lambda = sum_{j=1}^3 (-1)^(j-1) binom(3,j)
              mu_Z^{*j} * 1^{*(j-1)} * log.                 (HB)

This proves the identity used here without importing a different
global-scale truncation. Every term has 2j<=6 individual variables:
j truncated mu variables, j-1 unrestricted 1 variables and one log
variable. Unit variables are retained; a unit log variable contributes
zero. No SW claim is needed for a unit variable.

Apply (HB) to each original group separately. Retain only dyadic HB
rectangles meeting EACH original group interval and the FULL original
geometric support, BEFORE Mellin separation. Removing an identically
zero rectangle at this stage changes no sum. If U_1,...,U_s are the
lower endpoints in one retained group, s<=6, then

          product U_j >= R/2^6.

Write x_HB=product_all U_j. It is comparable, within a constant depending
only on k_star, to a complete product in the original support. When a
slot is selected and the complementary convolution is split into dyadic
intervals, the source scale is x=U*V. This x can differ from x_HB by
another fixed factor at most 2^(6*k_star+1). Every one of these scales
is comparable to the original X=product R_i. Consequently each
R_i<=C(k_star)*x. This remains true on the entire separated rectangle,
even where the original weight is zero. It justifies the individual
mu-slot bound U_j<<x^(1/3), but does not justify identifying all the
scale endpoints exactly.

The roughness threshold must be fixed BEFORE the HB expansion, not
changed independently for its different summands. On the original
group chart put X=product R_i, Xplus=2^k*X, C_k=2^(6*k_star+2), and
choose Zchart=(C_k*Xplus)^(1/(loglog(C_k*Xplus))^3). Every source
scale after complementary dyadic subdivision is <=C_k*Xplus and
comparable to X. Since this function is eventually increasing,
z(x)<=Zchart<=x^(2/(loglog x)^3) for all sufficiently large x.
Thus the same roughness indicator used throughout (HB) is strong
enough for P's support hypothesis, and belongs to the uniform SW
window specified above. Fixed multiplicative chart constants cause
no loss in a strict exponent margin.

Insert 1_{Pminus(n_1 ... n_k)>Zchart} into the ORIGINAL group sequence.
It is automatic for the original primes, but not for every prime power:
p^r~R_i>=T^a need not imply p>Zchart. Instead replace the original
primes by the ROUGH von Mangoldt sequence and remove its proper prime
powers as in paragraph 5. On that sequence the insertion is exact.
Under (HB) it is the product of the SAME Zchart-roughness indicators
on all individual variables. Units use Pminus(1)=infinity.

2. The medium/long dichotomy and why the residual exponent is fixed

Choose 0<zeta<min(a/48,1/1000), with zeta additionally below all fixed
compiler margins. First classify slots on the ORIGINAL group chart.
A medium slot has lower scale

             X^(3*zeta)<=U<=X^(1/3+zeta/4).

The deliberately narrower chart interval absorbs all fixed scale
constants. For every eventual source x=U*V comparable to X it implies
x^(2*zeta)<=U<=x^(1/3+zeta/2), the precise interval required by the
modulus factorization with A0=U*x^(-zeta). No decision is made using
an as-yet unspecified complementary dyadic scale.

If it exists, select ONE such individual slot, not a grouped
convolution. After separating smooth factors, its coefficient is
rough 1 or rough mu, on a subinterval, possibly with a normalized
logarithm and a Mellin twist. These are exactly the coefficients of
input SW. Grouping the remaining at most 6*k_star-1 variables gives
a complementary coefficient of fixed divisor order, after normalizing
the finitely many logarithms. Input P and its divisor truncation apply
when the actual atom partition supplies the four scale inequalities.

If there is no medium slot, every slot is short (<X^(3*zeta)) or long
(>X^(1/3+zeta/4)). A group containing only short slots would have
lower-endpoint product <X^(18*zeta), contradicting R_i/64>>T^a
for large T. Hence every original group contains a long slot.
Three long slots would have product >X^(1+3*zeta/4), impossible since
the product of all dyadic lower endpoints is x_HB=O_k(X), with each
individual endpoint >=1.
There are at most two long slots and at most two original groups.
A truncated mu slot cannot be long, because its endpoint is
O(x^(1/3)); a long coefficient is therefore rough 1, with only the
allowed log, interval and smooth factors before partial summation.

With one long slot there is one original group, at most five remaining
short variables, and their product is O_k(X^(15*zeta))<=x^(16*zeta)
eventually. With two long slots there are at most twelve total variables
and at most ten short ones; their product is O_k(X^(30*zeta))
<=x^(32*zeta). The independent long-branch proof may use its own
precision zeta_L=2*zeta, giving the conservative bounds x^(10*zeta_L)
and x^(20*zeta_L). Zeta is chosen small enough for its stronger
proof-level modulus condition theta_star<(2/3)*(1-40*zeta)-4*epsilon_L.
Thus no residual bound of the
form x^(O(k_star*zeta)) is silently used in these exceptional cases.
This only reduces the problem to L; in particular it does not prove
that a fixed-residue or interval-restricted version of L is adequate.

3. The actual large-residue range

Remove the low complete-product tail below O(T^(1-kappa0)) using
the separately reviewed absolute tail estimate. Every retained HB
rectangle then has

             c1*T^(1-kappa0)<=x<=C1*T

with positive constants depending only on the fixed family. Thus a_res=T
satisfies T>=x/C1. If kappa0/(1-kappa0)<kappa, it also satisfies
T<=x^(1+kappa) for sufficiently large T, absorbing the fixed c1 factor.
For example choose sigma after zeta and all compiler margins, then

     kappa=sigma/200,  0<kappa0<sigma/(400+sigma),

and also impose the finite local-level and gate-continuity constraints.
Then kappa0/(1-kappa0)<sigma/400<kappa<=sigma/100. This verifies BOTH
residue endpoints of P with c0=1/C1. It does not revive the source's
larger all-a range, and does not replace x by T divided by a marked
prefix. The required x is the complete product being distributed.

4. Smooth separation and the logarithmic error budget

On a retained dyadic rectangle, use logarithmic coordinates
log(n_j/U_j), with a fixed smooth cutoff equal to one on that rectangle.
Fourier inversion separates its smooth geometric weight into products
of n_j^(it_j). Normalized derivative bounds imply, by integration by
parts, arbitrarily rapid frequency decay with fixed logarithmic
constants. Truncating all |t_j| at a sufficiently large fixed power
of log T leaves a pointwise O(log^(-B)T) error for any requested B.
The retained frequency domain and transform L1 norm cost at most a
fixed logarithmic power. The input bounds must be uniform on that
domain; this is precisely why SW and L include interval/twist scope.

The truncation error is estimated AFTER summing the moduli. For a
complete integer v!=T the sum of fixed-divisor-order modulus weights
over l|(v-T) is at most tau(|v-T|)^C. The coefficient at v from finitely
many HB variables is also bounded by a fixed divisor power, times a
fixed logarithmic power. The reviewed nonzero shifted-divisor moment
Z2 therefore gives an error O(x*log^(C'-B)x) on the complete local
scale. The logarithmic exponent C' is fixed before B is chosen.
For v=T use the first-moment bound T^(theta_star+o(1)), with the
extra log^(-B) harmless; the fixed gap theta_star<1 provides a power
saving. In the actual even-target rough problem that equality is
already excluded by parity, but the argument does not require this.
The coprime mean is controlled by the fixed logarithmic sum
sum_l |rho_T(l)|/phi(l), without a modulus-level factor.

All cutoff and frequency choices are fixed logarithmic powers. Start
with a larger arbitrary-log exponent in SW, P, L and BV to absorb
their total costs and the polylogarithmic number of HB/dyadic pieces.
Subpower factors are used only against the fixed power-saving terms,
never multiplied into a remainder that has only logarithmic saving.

5. Prime powers and rough-square collisions

Proper prime powers in an original group are removed by an absolute
estimate, independently of distribution. For group size >=c*T^a,
the sum of 1/p^r over prime powers p^r>=c*T^a, r>=2, is
T^(-a/2+o(1)), by dyadic summation of the elementary prime-power
count O(y^(1/2)*log y). The number of remaining product tuples of
size O(T) has at most fixed divisor order. Hence their total mass is
T^(1-a/2+o(1)). Summing congruences first costs only a divisor power
of |v-T| for v!=T, which is T^o(1); the exact v=T term contributes
at most T^(theta_star+o(1)). The mean has only a logarithmic additional
factor. This proves that replacing original primes by rough Lambda,
using HB, and then removing prime powers introduces a power-saving
error. Partial summation removes the prime logarithms, since every
original group is >=c*T^a. This covers both rough and excluded
nonrough proper prime powers; no assumption on their base primes
was necessary.

For removing a rough-square modulus collision, let w=T^omega with
fixed omega>0, and let C be the moduli <=D=T^theta_star divisible
by p^2 for some prime p>w. Elementary divisor counting gives
sum_{l in C}1/l << log T/w and #C<<D/w. Including divisor weights
in both the sequence and modulus costs T^o(1). Both the progression
count and its primitive mean therefore contribute at most

             (T+D)*T^o(1)/w = O(T^(1-omega/2))

eventually. The +1 in the progression count is retained, so v=T
is covered here as well. After this error is paid, the exact atom
representation may replace its squarefree rough convolution by
the unrestricted convolution whose factors can be normalized.
Small-prime beta support is disjoint from rough prime support;
factorial normalization and repeated-label factors must still be
tracked as in the separate modulus review.

6. Exact implication, not a global certificate

Given SW, P, L, BV and the actual finite compiler assumptions stated
above, the argument yields for each permitted fixed smooth family W_T

 sum_l rho_T(l) [ sum_{p1...pk=T (mod l)} W_T(p1,...,pk)
           - phi(l)^(-1) sum_{(p1...pk,l)=1} W_T(p1,...,pk) ]
                   = O_A(T/log(T)^A)

for every fixed A, uniformly in the even target. Positive outer
weights and signed lower-sieve weights use the SAME discrepancy;
the absolute error does not assume a positive error sign or a
positive lower bound on an individual cell's mass. Finite exact
linear combinations preserve the conclusion. This is an analytic
reduction, not yet a proof that every current compiler supplies
its assumptions or that the separate L and BV sources apply.
