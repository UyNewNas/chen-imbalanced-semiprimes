2. Mathematical functions and independently derived bounds

Write f0=f/(2 exp(gamma)), F0=F/(2 exp(gamma)), L(s)=s f0(s), H(s)=s F0(s).
The standard ordinary linear-sieve definition and its positivity/monotonicity
are explicit mathematical inputs: f0=0 up to2, H=1 up to3, f0 increases,
F0 decreases, F0>=f0>=0, and
 L'(s)=F0(s-1) for s>2, H'(s)=f0(s-1) for s>3.
In particular 0<=f0'(s)<=1/s and |F0'(s)|<=1/s in their nonconstant ranges,
since F0<=1 on[1,infinity). The initial1/s pieces obey the same bounds.
Consequently L is concave on[2,infinity), H is convex on[3,infinity), and
 |L''(u)|<=1/(u-1), 0<=H''(u)<=1/(u-1)
on those respective ranges. These first-derivative bounds, not the
subject's trapezoid/F0'' bounds, drive the new calculation.

All integer delays are grid endpoints. L has its initial derivative jump
at2, but no cell straddles it; H is constant through3. Later integer breaks
are also endpoints. Piecewise absolutely continuous derivatives and the
one-sided endpoint values suffice for the integral chord inequalities.
No assumption of a globally C4 solution or a Simpson remainder across
delay breaks is made.

3. Different integration method: numerator chord divided by u

Let the delayed cell be[a,b]=[j/n,(j+1)/n], h=1/n. The target increment is
an integral of H(u)/u or L(u)/u. If c_V is the endpoint chord of V then
 integral_a^b c_V(u)/u du = w0 V(a)+w1 V(b),
 w0=(j+1)log(1+1/j)-1, w1=1-j log(1+1/j).
Both weights are positive. This is exact logarithmic integration of a
linear numerator; it is NOT trapezoidal integration of V/u.

With |V''|<=M, the chord error is at most
 (M/2)(u-a)(b-u).
Hence its weighted integral is at most M h^3/(12a). Taking
M=1/(a-1) gives the rational error 1/[12 n j(j-n)]. Convex H lies below
its chord, so this error is subtracted only from the lower L increment.
Concave L lies above its chord, so it is added only to the upper H increment.
For target s<=4, H(u)=1 exactly; the L increment is simply log(1+1/j).
Known L monotonicity permits taking max(previous lower,new lower).

The weights have independent alternating expansions
 w0=sum_(k>=1)(-1)^(k+1)/(k(k+1)j^k),
 w1=sum_(k>=1)(-1)^(k+1)/((k+1)j^k).
Their positive term magnitudes decrease for j>=1. The fourth partial sum
is a lower bound and the fifth an upper bound; the same rule applies to
log(1+1/j). In common denominator60j^5 the fourth numerators are
 30j^4-10j^3+5j^2-3j,
 30j^4-20j^3+15j^2-12j,
 60j^4-30j^3+20j^2-15j,
and the fifth numerators add2,10,12 respectively. The code computes these
integers directly, avoiding cancellation of two nearly equal logarithms.

Every lower weight is floored at2^-112; every upper weight and curvature
error is rounded up. Numerator endpoint bounds are nonnegative, so positive
weighted sums have the stated interval direction. Their products are then
rounded outwards once more. Delayed indices j,j+1 are strictly earlier than
the target index; induction therefore gives certified enclosures at every
node. The proof covers every update, not only the tested sample points.

4. Original interpolation, cutoffs, and integer tail bounds

The subject's knots have now been covered by a separately certified
method. On a noninteger mesh cell for L above2, its endpoint chord bounds
L below, and chord+h^2/8 bounds it above because |L''|<=1. H above3 is
convex with H''<=1, reversing the two sides. The subject's numerator()
floors the lower interpolation and ceilings the upper, then applies the
appropriate one-sided error. Division by a positive argument is outward.
The exact-rational q,r=divmod(a*n,b) handles a grid node with r=0 directly;
an argument at maximum has no attempted out-of-range next-node access.

The rule point(s)=0 at s<=2 agrees with the defined lower function.
At s>maximum, monotonicity and f0<=F0 give
 f0(maximum)<=f0(s)<=F0(maximum),
so the fallback interval is valid without extrapolating a numerical DDE.
The scalar numerator(maximum)/maximum floors and ceilings have the right
direction. Only the lower-function query has this fallback: no unsupported
large-s assertion about H interpolation is inferred.

For D=F0-f0, the exact delay relation for s>=3 is
 (sD(s))'=-D(s-1).
As D is nonnegative and decreasing,
 D'(s)=-(D(s)+D(s-1))/s<=-2D(s)/s.
It follows that D(s)<=D(k)(k/s)^2 for every3<=k<=s. Thus taking the
minimum of all eligible upward-rounded integer-anchor bounds is valid;
the floor(s) restriction in d_upper never selects a future anchor.
The subject stores ceil((H_upper(k)-L_lower(k))/k), exactly an upper
bound. We independently compare all ten saved anchors k=3,...,12.
Also f0'(s)<=(F0(s-1)-f0(s-1))/s=D(s-1)/s, justifying the additional
derivative bound where the caller checks s>=4. This statement does not
independently recheck every caller's geometry.

5. Entire noncompact tail, not a sampled small-s test

For the actual ordered E0 domain, cap>=v(x) and y<=x. Below mu=25/128,
v(x)-2x>=1/2-3mu/2=53/256>rho; above mu, v(x)-2x is minimized at
rho=19/96, where57/64-7rho/2=rho. Also Theta>=153/256 and
Theta-2rho>rho, so taking the minimum with Theta preserves the bound.
Thus cap-x-y>=rho. Put z=t3, h=1/100, k=10. The condition
h<=rho/(k+1) gives sold>=(rho-z)/z>=k. Since both f0 values are bounded
by their common limit, their positive difference is at most D(sold).
Use the preceding anchor bound, enlarge the domain by positivity, and
integrate ordered x,y to get exactly
 tail <= 2 D(k) k^2 h/(rho-h)^2
          *[log(rho/h)^2+2log(rho/h)+2].
The factor2 is the original4 times the ordered xy factor1/2. The z^-2
density cancels the z^2 supplied by D(sold). No t3-tail is set to zero.

The new checker evaluates this whole tail with its new integer D(10)
upper bound and an independent160-term positive -log(1-w) series after
power-of-two range reduction. The remainder is bounded by

6. Direction-critical source audit and coverage limits

The original table subtracts h^3/6 only on its L lower endpoint and adds
h^3/12 only on its H upper endpoint. These directions follow from convex
F0 and concave f0. The original comments' loose bounds F0''<=2 and

denominators. The independent method verifies the resulting knot values,
