\paragraph{The actual inherited tree and the sign.}
Let $\lambda_5(t)$ be the frozen cap of the actual preceding
fourth/fifth tree. Its allowed endpoints belong to the raw first-five
selected caps and the inherited $W_3$ branch. Thus
\[
 \lambda_5\le R_5:=\max\{\Lambda-3t_3/8,
               v(s_I):\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,5\}\}.
\]
In each numerical box, every raw cap is bounded above by one fixed
affine plane, either defining plane of $v$ or the constant peak
$\Lambda$. Their maximum, together with $W_3$, is a convex function
$U_5\ge R_5$. Choosing these planes by their midpoint values does not
assert any midpoint-based inequality: every choice is a global upper
bound on its own.

The original third cap $L_3$ is checked throughout the box against
both $S_2+3t_3$ and $S_4+3t_5$. Consequently the original first,
third and fifth tests have passed, independently of which allowed
fourth/fifth refinements were activated. In particular the actual
rank-six node exists and
\[
 \lambda_5-S_6\ge L_3-S_6>2t_5-t_6>t_6.
\]
Select a new sixth endpoint $M$ passing every full and selected gate.
Change the frozen cap only on the region $d=M-U_5>0$, and only for
subsequent odd tests. There is no new exclusion at the even sixth
rank. The exact even-subtree difference is
\[
 \sum_{P_6}g_N(P_6)
 \{V_N^+(N^{\lambda_5}/P_6,p_6)
       -V_N^+(N^M/P_6,p_6)\}.
\]
It is the actual previous tree that appears here; this is not an
increment computed against the old fourth-only tree.

\paragraph{Positive-part averaging and denominator direction.}
On the active region, $\lambda_5\le U_5<M$. The retained fifth
cube gives old argument greater than one, and the independent
upper-argument check gives $(M-S_6)/t_6\le3$. Both upper sieve
functions therefore have the rational formula
$\overline F(s)=1/s$. Their limiting saving is
\[
 \frac{4(M-\lambda_5)}{(\lambda_5-S_6)(M-S_6)}\,d\mu_6.
\]
Let $R_n$ bound $M-S_6$ above and let $R_u$ bound $U_5-S_6$
above throughout the numerical box. On the active region the old
residual is at most both $R_u$ and $R_n$, so it is legitimate to put
$R_o=\min(R_u,R_n)$. The density saving, extended by zero off the
active region, is at least
\[
 \frac{4d_+}{R_oR_n}\,d\mu_6.
\]
The function $M$ is concave and $U_5$ is convex; hence $d$ is
concave. If $B$ is a rectangle with $64$ vertices, repeated concavity
and integration give
\[
 \frac1{|B|}\int_Bd_+\,dt
 \ge\frac1{|B|}\int_Bd\,dt
 \ge\frac1{64}\sum_{v\text{ vertex}}d(v).
\]
The positive part must be introduced before pulling out a lower
bound for the positive prime-density measure. Applying that lower
weight directly to a signed $d$ would be invalid. The author's
formula has the correct order and permits negative vertex gaps.

For integer grid coordinates $\ell_i$, upper coordinates
$u_i=\ell_i+1$, grid denominator $n$ and affine denominator
$D=256n$, write $d_\Sigma$ for the sum of the $64$ scaled gaps and
$r_o,r_n$ for the two scaled residual bounds. Since
$|B|=n^{-6}$ and $\prod t_i^{-1}\ge n^6/\prod u_i$, the resulting
box gain is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sixth-expanded-independent-term}
 \frac{D\,d_\Sigma}{16r_or_n\prod_i u_i}.
\end{equation}
Every saved contribution is this expression rounded down to
$2^{-80}$. The factor $16$ is $64/4$; no factor of $t_6$ remains
after taking the difference of $1/s$.

\paragraph{Repeated root bins and finite coverage.}
The root grid has weakly ordered indices $19\ge k_1\ge\cdots\ge k_6\ge2$.
The initial rectangles have disjoint interiors even when entries of an index
vector repeat.  Their dyadic children have integer indices $2k+b$;
this map is injective in the pair $(k,b)$, with $b\in\{0,1\}^6$.
Children are filtered by weak order, never sorted or identified by a
permutation. A strictly ordered interior point can lie only in a weakly
ordered cell, so this geometric filtering is sound.  Final acceptance
still requires $\ell_j\ge\ell_{j+1}+1$ for every adjacent pair.
Thus there is no factorial normalization and no overlap between final
boxes.  Only the last grid is summed. At grid $400$ there are just four
subintervals per original interval; root runs of multiplicity five or six
cannot yet produce a wholly separated box. This is a strictly partial
finite witness and not a full-domain coverage assertion.
