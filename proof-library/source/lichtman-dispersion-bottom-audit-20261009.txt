2. Moving and nonprimitive shifted moments

Let I lie in [-x^K,x^K], have length at most L<=x^K, and let
q<=L*x^(-delta). For k=r mod q, k!=0, put g=(r,q), q'=q/g,
k=g*t. The resulting residue of t modulo q' is primitive, also when
r=0 (then q'=1). Since tau(gt)^C<=tau(g)^C*tau(|t|)^C and
L'=L/g satisfies L'/q'=L/q>=x^delta, it suffices to bound that
primitive progression in an interval of length L'. The gcd factor
tau(g)^C<=tau(q)^C must be kept.

Values 0<|t|<=q'*x^(delta/2) contribute at most
O(x^(delta/2)+1)*x^(delta/4)=O(x^(3delta/4)) per gcd factor.
Here the pointwise divisor bound uses the fixed exponent delta/(4K)
and polynomial bound on |t|. This is smaller than L'/q', so this
part does not leave a pointwise x^epsilon loss in the final mean.

For larger |t| split signs and positive dyadic ranges. Short dyadic
ranges can be included whole; for ranges longer than L' extend each
intersection to a positive interval of length comparable to L'.
The total covering length is O(L') (an extra log would suffice), and
each interval [X,X+H) may be chosen with

 H<=X, H>=c*q'*x^(delta/2), H>=c*x^(delta/2), X<=C*x^K.

Taking alpha=beta=min(1/4,delta/(8K)) gives, eventually,
X^beta<=H and q'/H^(1-alpha)<=x^(-3delta/8+o(1))<1.
Fixed constant endpoint enlargements can alternatively be absorbed by
slightly reducing these positive parameters. All interval and modulus
conditions are satisfied in the Shiu variables X,H; the ambient x
is not silently substituted for a much smaller interval endpoint.

For f(t)=tau(t)^C, f(p^j)=(j+1)^C<=2^(Cj) and the required
subpower bound holds. The prime exponential and q'/phi(q') cost only
fixed logarithmic powers. Therefore uniformly in I,r,q,

 sum_(k in I,k=r mod q,k!=0) tau(|k|)^C
   << (L/q)*tau(q)^C*log(x)^O(C,K,delta).                 (M1)

Apply Cauchy to one and the same b progression in [1,C0*x], with
the two factors tau(b)^(2B) and tau(|b-a|)^(2C), omitting b=a.
Each is covered by(M1), the second on a translated interval and
residue r-a. Both possible gcd factors divide q. Hence

 sum_(b=r mod q,b!=a) tau(b)^B*tau(|b-a|)^C
   << (x/q)*tau(q)^O(B,C)*log(x)^O(B,C,K,delta).          (M2)

This uses q<=x^(1-delta), allows either sign of b-a and arbitrary
polynomial-sized a, and contains NO tau(a) factor. A tiny actual
interval may be enlarged to the stated length; no short-interval
estimate below the Shiu range is being asserted. The integer zero
stays excluded throughout(M1)--(M2).

3. Every use of the shifted moment in the dispersion proof

In Lemma14.2 keep mn1!=a while removing (m,r2)>1. A common factor
f>1 is >z0 because r2 is rough. The ordinary divisor sum over its
multiples r2 contributes (R/f)*log^O times a fixed divisor power of
f. Collapsing b=mn1 with f|m implies f|b; the multiplicity and
all m,n1 divisor weights are bounded by tau(b)^O(1). Since
f<=O(M)<=O(x^(1-delta)), (M2) applies with a smaller fixed delta.
Together with the outer1/f, this gives the sum

 (N/(QD))*x*log^O *sum_(f>z0) tau(f)^O(1)/f^2
   << (MN^2/(QD))*log^O/z0.                              (M3)

The divisor tail is bounded by ordinary divisor means and partial
summation. It is logarithmic, not x^o(1): z0=x^(1/(loglog x)^3)
would not absorb an arbitrary subpower loss. In E=1, the source's
separate removal of (e,r1r2)>1 is vacuous.

In Lemma14.3 both products mn_i-a are nonzero. After absolute values,
symmetry and uv<=(u^2+v^2)/2 bound the two shifted-divisor factors
by a single higher moment in (m,n1). The number of possible n2 in
its progression is O(N/(QD)+1). Collapsing b=mn1 and applying(M2)
with q=1 gives x*log^O, so the large-coefficient/divisor cutoff costs

 (xN/(QD)+x)*log(x)^(C0-C1).

Here C0 depends on the fixed divisor exponent, not on the moving a.
Choose C1 sufficiently large for the desired logarithmic error.

Under the remaining logarithmic cutoffs the source's later elementary
counts are uniform in a. For (r1,r2)>1 a common f divides both shifts;
since mn1=a mod r1 and (a,r1)=1, (m,f)=1 and f divides n1-n2.
For (n1,n2)>1, roughness supplies f>z0 and the progression step is
at least qd*f, with the n1-multiple count supplying the needed mean.
For (qd,r2)>1, f divides qd and (m,qd)=1; the stronger congruence
modulo qd*f again has only one n2 residue. These are algebraic
coprimality facts, not assumptions that the integer representative a
is small. Their errors are bounded by

 (xN/(QD))*log^C2/z0 + x*log^C2.

The close-pair exclusion similarly costs
(xN/(QD))*log^(C3-Cclose)+x*log^C3. The power bound QD/N<=x^-delta
makes the x terms negligible; z0 dominates every fixed log power.
Choose Cclose after C1. Restore the large logarithmic cutoffs only
on the still-nonzero pairs. The printed line4743 has tau(mn2 a)
in the reinsertion sentence; the preceding definition of the starred
sum unambiguously has tau(mn2-a). This audit uses that definition,
not the isolated missing-minus typography.

4. Exact product: modify before Cauchy, restore only single zeros

For a>0 define E(m,n)=1_(mn=a) and replace each congruence indicator
I_(qdr) by I'_(qdr)=I_(qdr)-E. Because (qdr,a)=1, E implies all
the needed congruences and coprimalities, so I' is precisely the
indicator with the zero shift deleted. Leave the coprime mean intact.
The first-moment change is at most QDR*x^o(1): there are tau(a)
factorizations and all coefficients are divisor bounded on polynomial
scales. If a<0 there is no positive exact product.

Apply Cauchy to the MODIFIED expression. In E=1, for each (q,d,m)
there is one allowed n residue modulo qd. Ordinary divisor means
bound the eliminated beta_m and gamma_(q,d) by
MQD*log(x)^O(B0), with no moving-residue factor. Extending with the
same nonnegative smooth majorants is valid before expanding the square.
The exact expansion has unchanged S1, S2 with E1 removed, and S3
with both zero shifts removed. In particular

 (I1-E1)(I2-E2)=I1*I2*(1-E1)*(1-E2),

where in this identity Ei=E(m,ni), not the exponential remainder Ei(a).

The E1*E2 term has not been forgotten; this identity describes why
it is absent from the modified variance. No m-dependent SW sequence
is created, since only the counting indicator was changed.

In S2 restore mn1=a only after(M3), immediately before its Poisson
step. For each of the x^o(1) exact factorizations there are N choices
of n2, QD choices of q,d, R^2 choices of r1,r2 and a mean factor
1/phi(qdr2)<<x^o(1)/(QDR). The restoration is RN*x^o(1).

In S3 retain the exclusion through ALL divisor cutoffs and the close-
pair deletion. Its S4 now has n1!=n2. Fixing mn1=a leaves
O(N/(QD)+1) choices of n2 per(q,d); mn2-a is nonzero. Thus r2
has x^o(1) divisor choices, whereas r1 has O(R) choices. Both
single-zero cases together cost R*(N+QD)*x^o(1)=RN*x^o(1).
They may now be restored to the complete smooth m sum used by CRT
and Poisson. Both zeros cannot occur in this S4. Later restoration
of close pairs is in the mean term AFTER Poisson, where ordinary
progression/SW estimates apply and no shifted zero divisor appears.

The normalization is explicit. Set V=xN/(QD), the raw variance
scale. Its multiplier under outer Cauchy is MQD*log^O. The RN
restoration has relative size QDR/x, and its squared-first-moment
contribution is

 (MQD)*(RN)*x^o(1)=x*(QDR)*x^o(1)<=x^(2-delta+o(1)).

Taking a square root yields x^(1-delta/2+o(1)). The first-moment
QDR correction is smaller. These are the ONLY places an uncontrolled
fixed-order divisor x^o factor remains, apart from the small-t part
of(M1), which was already absorbed by its power reserve.

For comparison, reinserting both zeros too early would permit a term
QD*R^2*x^o(1), requiring (QDR)^2<xN. This does not follow from D's
hypotheses: exponents N=.4,M=.6,QD=.3,R=.55 satisfy the stated
conditions for delta=.05 but give 2(.3+.55)=1.7>1.4=1+.4.
That is an obstruction to the prematurely reinserted proof, not to
the reviewed late-restoration argument.

5. Remaining uniformity and error ledger

S1 involves only unshifted divisor sums; dropping target coprimality
conditions in absolute errors makes its constants independent of a.
CRT uses primitive residues, and Poisson's tail estimate is uniform
in those residues; a occurs only in a modulus-one exponential at this
stage. No approximation of its phase is made in D. After Poisson,
all restored main-term exclusions are controlled by ordinary divisor
counts with the same QD/N and z0 gaps. Lemma13.8 is applied to the
ORIGINAL uniformly SW alpha, with modulus range O(QD)<N/log(N)^C.
Since N>=x^delta, log N and log x are comparable. The target a only
restricts outer moduli and does not become a large nonprimitive
residue inside that mean-square estimate.

The losses can therefore be kept in three separate classes:
 - Logarithmic: divisor averages, Cauchy multiplier, coefficient
   truncations, SW mean-square. Select their target exponents larger
   than every fixed log loss; no x^o is multiplied into them.
 - Roughness: V*log^C/z0, still log-small for every fixed C.
   Its divisor tails use means, not pointwise x^o bounds.
 - Fixed-power: V*x^-delta*log^C, the small-t estimate in(M1),
   QDR*x^o and RN*x^o. Only here are fixed-order subpower factors
   absorbed by a genuine positive exponent margin.

ultrashort intervals or unrestricted nonprimitive residues without their
tau(q) factor. Uniform SW constants, fixed polynomial a-range, positive
power margins and E=1 remain indispensable hypotheses. The standard
Shiu and SW mean-square theorems remain external inputs. No statement
about the later spectral proof, full repaired convolution assembly or
global K,c,b follows from this record alone.
