1. The missing sharp-singleton lemma, now proved

Let the ACTUAL coefficient rho_T have |rho_T(q)|<=C, squarefree support
q<=T^theta, theta<1, (q,T)=1, with C fixed before T. Suppose the current
smooth-singleton PP holds, with arbitrary O_A(T/log^A T) error for its
allowed polylogarithmic Euler-derivative norms. For even T>2 it implies

 sum_q rho_T(q){pi(T;q,T)-pi(T)/phi(q)}=O_A(T/log^A T).      (S1)

Here pi(T) may count p<T or p<=T, which coincide for these T. This is
exactly(2.5), and it also applies to the bounded marked scalar classes.

Fix desired A. Put delta=log^(-B)T, with fixed B>A+3. Smooth the original
prime endpoint in [T-delta*T,T+delta*T], using a fixed smooth transition
rescaled by delta. Its derivative of order j in p/T is O_j(delta^(-j)),
a FIXED logarithmic power. Multiply by the already allowed smooth lower
trim eta(p/T^(1-kappa0)), which equals0 below T^(1-kappa0) and1 above
2T^(1-kappa0). The resulting W_T is supported on primes of a fixed positive
power, product O(T), and has the precise polylog derivative norms allowed
by distribution-prime-product-completion section1, lines25--32. Smooth
PP therefore applies to W_T with this same rho. Choose its local log/SW
accuracy after B and the fixed derivative orders, as in that proof's
sections3 and10. No parameter of the physical finite sieve changes.

The upper boundary error can be bounded AFTER summing all moduli, without
postulating a signed sharp-domain PP theorem:

 sum_(prime p,0<|p-T|<=delta*T) sum_(q|T-p)|rho_T(q)|
 <=C sum_(prime p,0<|p-T|<=delta*T)tau(|T-p|)
 <=2C sum_(1<=n<=ceil(delta*T))tau(n)
 <<delta*T*log T+log T.                                 (S2)

The elementary last estimate follows from sum_(n<=X)tau(n)=
sum_(d<=X)floor(X/d)<=X(1+log X). The excluded n=0 means p=T; it never
occurs because T>2 is even. Thus no tau(0), extra T^theta or signed
boundary-positivity argument is hidden in(S2).

The exact-coprime mean boundary is at most
 C*# {integers p in the strip}*sum_(q<=T^theta)1/phi(q)
 <<delta*T*log T+log T.                                 (S3)
One can ignore primality for this bound. The elementary reciprocal-phi
sum is O(log T), obtained by n/phi(n)=sum_(d|n)mu(d)^2/phi(d) and the
convergent sum of mu(d)^2/[d phi(d)]. With B>A+3, (S2),(S3) are
O_A(T/log^A T).

The small-prime trim costs at most
 C sum_(p<2T^(1-kappa0))tau(T-p)=T^(1-kappa0+o(1)),
and its exact mean at most O(T^(1-kappa0)log T). Here T-p is nonzero
and <=T, so the usual pointwise divisor bound suffices. These fixed
power savings are smaller than every prescribed logarithmic error.
Removing both smoothing changes thus gives the sharp discrepancy with
mean pi(T)-omega(q): all q<T, so every prime divisor of q lies below T.

Finally squarefreeness and |rho|<=C give

 |sum_q rho_T(q)*omega(q)/phi(q)|
 <=C sum_(prime l<=T^theta) 1/(l-1)
           *sum_(e<=T^theta/l)1/phi(e)
 <<log T*sum_(l<=T^theta)1/(l-1)<<log^2 T.                (S4)

For the last crude bound, summing over all integers rather than primes
already suffices. Equations(S2)--(S4) prove(S1). Thus the O(log^2 T)
statement in C is correct, but the passage to its sharp pi needed this
separate endpoint argument. A positive fixed-tolerance smoothing alone
would not have justified it for a signed lower weight.

The lower pointwise count sometimes restricts original p to (p,T)=1.
There are O(log T) omitted primes dividing T, and their total compiled
divisor contribution is T^o(1), since p<T and |rho|<=C. This harmless
power-small adjustment does not change (S1) or its normalization.
