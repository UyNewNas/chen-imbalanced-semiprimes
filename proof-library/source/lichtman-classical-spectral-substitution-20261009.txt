Classical spectral inputs sufficient for the restricted packet argument
9 October 2026. New substitution note; older files are unchanged.

Purpose and input boundary

The restricted Lemma5.1 argument currently names L23 Theorems9.1 and9.5
as external a-free inputs. Neither of those two full statements is needed
as a separate input: the actual fixed-level weight and the interval-side
average follow from the two classical Deshouillers--Iwaniec bounds below.
The oldform packet, Kuznetsov/completion, and regular-spectrum arguments
remain separate. This note does not reprove classical spectral theory or
claim a new exponent, an unrestricted large-residue result, or a prime count.

Primary source used to identify the precise classical statements:
Alexandru Pascadi, arXiv:2404.04239v3, Theorem A (1.4)--(1.5), Theorem J
(3.34)--(3.35), and their attribution to Deshouillers--Iwaniec.
https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04239v3
The paper's publication is Forum of Mathematics, Pi14 (2026), e8,
https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2026.10025 .
The saved v3 HTML and TeX-preserving text are the primary text actually read.
This is a direct application of the stated classical estimates as inputs;
it is not an independent proof of those estimates from DI's original paper.

Use DI-normalized Fourier coefficients throughout. Put
  tau_j=2 sqrt(1/4-lambda_j)=2i kappa_j,
  0<tau_j<=Theta=7/32
on the exceptional spectrum. Pascadi calls tau_j/2 theta_j, so his weight
X^(2 theta_j) is exactly X^tau_j here. The letter Theta below is unrelated
to the sieve carrier level. This factor-of-two convention is essential.
The Kuznetsov factor 1/cosh(pi*kappa_j)=1/cos(pi*tau_j/2) is positive
and bounded above and below by absolute constants for tau_j<=7/32.
It can therefore be inserted or removed in these positive exceptional
norms without changing any exponent or parameter dependence.

Classical inputs, stated only to the strength used

F. At a cusp a of level q with mu(a)=1/q, arbitrary complex coefficients
b_n on n~L satisfy
  sum_exc X^tau_j |sum_(n~L) b_n rho_(j,a)(n)|^2
    <<_epsilon (qL)^epsilon (1+L/q) sum |b_n|^2
whenever L>=1/2 and X << max(1,q/L).
This is a restricted consequence of Pascadi's Theorem A, whose admissible
range also contains a third term q^2/L^3. That extra term is unused here.

J. At the infinity cusps, using the identity scaling matrices, uniformly
in omega modulo1,
  sum_(q~Q) sum_exc X^tau_j |sum_(m~M) e(m omega)rho_(j,infinity)(m)|^2
    <<_epsilon (QM)^epsilon (Q+M)M
for M,Q>=1/2 and X << max(M,Q^2/M).
This is Pascadi's Theorem J. The uniform additive phase is part of the
stated input; we do not infer it by multiplying an arbitrary coefficient
into a theorem for the constant sequence. Level multiplicities arising
from q=rs are paid separately by the elementary divisor bound.

1. The fixed-level substitution at exactly the weight we use

In the packet calculation fix n'' dividing a^infinity and put L=N/n''.
Only nonempty positive-integer ranges occur, so L>=1/2. At level q=rs,
(r,s)=1, the cusp1/s has mu(1/s)=1/(rs). Since r~R and s~S, q is between
RS and4RS. The weight in L23 (10.9) is
  X_f=1+X0=2+RS/L,  where X0=1+RS/L.
It satisfies
  X_f <=3 max(1,q/L).
Thus input F gives, separately at each level,
  sum_exc (1+X0)^tau_j |sum_(n~L) b*_(n,r,s)rho_(j,1/s)(n)|^2
     <<_epsilon (qL)^epsilon (1+L/q) ||b*_(r,s)||_2^2.       (F1)
The constant3 is harmless in a <<-range; equivalently first apply F at
max(1,q/L), then use tau_j<=Theta to multiply by at most3^Theta.

The packet coefficient b* includes its retained unit phase and coprime
restriction. F permits arbitrary complex coefficients, so this changes
no applicability condition and does not increase its l2 norm. Summing
(F1) over the existing r,s family gives the n-side factor already used in
the packet proof, namely x^eta(1+N/(n''RS)) times its summed squared norm,
for every prescribed eta>0 when the scales are polynomially bounded in x.
This use is uniform for each fixed such parameter family. No general
large-X version of L23 Theorem9.1 is assumed in this substitution.

2. Completing a partial interval without changing the level dependence

Let I be any interval of integers contained in (M,2M]. It is fixed across
levels q. Set P=ceil(4M)+1. Distinct integers in (M,2M] remain distinct
modulo P. The exact finite Fourier identity is
  1_I(m)=sum_(k=0..P-1) c_k e(km/P),
  c_k=P^(-1)sum_(v in I) e(-kv/P),
for every integer m in (M,2M]. A geometric sum gives
  |c_0|<=1,
  |c_k|<=min(|I|/P, 1/(2P ||k/P||))  (k!=0).
Pairing k and P-k therefore yields
  sum |c_k| <= 2+log P.                                  (C1)
The bound is uniform in both endpoints of I, including empty/singleton
intervals and non-integral M. Changing the harmless absolute constant in
(C1) covers the bounded M range as well.

For fixed X define the Hilbert norm on the direct sum of the exceptional
spaces of all q~Q, with coordinate weights X^tau_j. Apply the triangle
inequality IN THAT NORM to the exact Fourier identity, then input J at
each omega=k/P. This proves, for every epsilon>0,
  sum_(q~Q)sum_exc X^tau_j |sum_(m in I)rho_(j,infinity)(m)|^2
    <<_epsilon (QM)^epsilon (Q+M)M                       (J1)
at X << X_*=max(M,Q^2/M). Use a smaller epsilon in J to absorb
(2+log P)^2. Neither the Fourier coefficients nor the interval depends
on q or on an eigenvector. This is why the norm argument is applicable;
an arbitrary level-dependent selection of endpoints has not been admitted.
A common pre-existing additive phase e(mt) is also allowed: apply input J
at omega=t+k/P. Its uniformity in omega is essential for this assertion.

For an additional scalar bounded-variation weight w(m) on I, partial
summation in the same Hilbert space multiplies the norm by at most
||w||_infinity+Var_I(w). In particular Mellin weights m^(it) cost
O(1+|t|) in norm on (M,2M]. Existing smooth Mellin decay can pay for this
cost. No pointwise supremum is interchanged with the spectral sum.

3. Recovering the interval-side exceptional factor for arbitrary X

The classical threshold is comparable to the symmetric expression:
  X_* <= (Q+M)^2/M <=4 X_* .                             (C2)
For any Y>0, positivity and 0<tau_j<=Theta give
  Y^tau_j <= max(1,(Y/X_*)^Theta) X_*^tau_j.
Insert (J1), use (C2), and absorb the fixed factor4^Theta. Thus
  sum_(q~Q)sum_exc Y^tau_j |sum_(m in I)rho_(j,infinity)(m)|^2
    <<_epsilon (QM)^epsilon
       [1+(MY/(Q+M)^2)^Theta](Q+M)M.                     (J2)

L23 Theorem9.5 writes Y=X^2. Formula(J2) consequently gives its required
interval-side shape without invoking that theorem's recursive proof.
In the actual packet pairing take Q comparable to RS and
Y=(1+X/sqrt(X0))^2. The number of representations of a level as rs is at
most tau(q), so its total extra cost is x^eta, not an extra power of Q.
All spectral spaces remain positive Hilbert sums; weights depend only on
the spectral parameter. The oldform packet operations preserve the
interval vector on this side, as required by the separate packet argument.

The selectable epsilon/divisor/log losses in(F1) and(J2) occupy the
existing selectable subpower allowance of the restricted Lemma5.1 proof.
There is no new fixed monomial power and no loss in K, c or the numerical b.
In particular D0, D1, D2 and the retained (1+|a|/x) term are unchanged.

4. Two literal problems in the printed fixed-level proof, and their scope

This substitution also avoids two lines in L23's proof of Theorem9.1 that
should not be copied literally. The saved primary text and current HTML
both contain them; these are observations about the displayed proof,
not counterexamples to the theorem or to any actual Maass spectrum.

(i) The text takes Y=4pi*mu(a)*N, with a test supported on [Y,2Y], then
declares the Kloosterman side empty. But m,n~N have sqrt(mn) up to2N;
the argument can reach2Y when 1/c=mu(a). For example at the infinity cusp,
c=q, m=n=3N/2 (N even), the argument is3Y/2, inside that support.
Moving the entire support to [16pi*mu(a)*N,32pi*mu(a)*N] restores a
strictly empty geometric side. This is a constant-scale issue.

(ii) For 0<Y<1 and tau_j<=Theta_q, the comparison of powers is
Y^(-tau_j)<=Y^(-Theta_q), whereas the final comparison in its(9.5)
uses the other direction as a lower bound. A bound for the actual
transform must retain tau_j. For example
  X^tau_j <= [1+(XY)^Theta_q]Y^(-tau_j)
is the correct elementary comparison, proved by distinguishing XY<=1
and XY>=1. We need not assert a uniform lower bound by Y^(-Theta_q).
Our use of F requires neither printed step.

This note does not diagnose every normalization or proof line of the
original paper. It does not rederive the classical Bessel transform,
Kuznetsov, or level-averaged DI estimates. In particular it does not
infer failure of their theorems from the two elementary slips above.

5. Resulting dependency substitution

For the actual coprime packet subclass only, replace the two a-free
L239.1/9.5 invocations by F1/J2. The remaining restricted-packet proof
still requires: the same packet norm identity with its correct oldform
normalization; classical regular spectral estimates and completion;
and the already explicit combinatorial multiplicity/scale analysis.
The large-residue retained phase and moving-residue dispersion issues
are separate and are not resolved merely by this substitution.

This reduces the set of newer spectral statements taken as inputs while
preserving exactly the estimates used downstream. It provides a local
mathematical derivation relative to the two stated classical inputs,
with separate scoped independent reviews recorded alongside this note.
No old source or result,
main manuscript, formalization project, or GitHub content is modified.
