The actual E1 sum: explicit completion, gcd counting and uniform residue bound
2026-10-10. New local derivation; no existing proof or numerical file changed.

1. Statement and the precise external inputs

Let Q,R,N,M>=1, NM comparable to x, with all four lengths bounded by a
fixed power of x. Let psi be a fixed smooth compactly supported function.
Put H=C QR/M (log x)^5 for a fixed C>0. For one-bounded alpha and lambda,
consider the E1 sum from Maynard I v2, Lemma17.2:

 Z = sum_(q~Q,(q,a)=1) sum_(r,s~R,(rs,a)=1)
       lambda(q,r) conjugate(lambda(q,s)) / [phi(qs) q r]
       sum_(n1,n2~N,(n1,qr)=(n2,qs)=1) alpha(n1) conjugate(alpha(n2))
       sum_(1<=|h|<=H) psihat(hM/(qr)) e(a h inverse(n1)/(qr)).       (E1)

All implied constants below are independent of the integer a. They depend
on the fixed support and smooth norms and on the fixed polynomial scale
bound. There is no assumption on the absolute size of a: only the displayed
primitive supports are needed. If H<1 the sum is empty. A fixed nonnegative
smooth q weight is permitted by absorbing its square root in lambda, after
constant normalization. There is NO inserted condition (r,s)=1.

This note proves the explicit inequality

 |Z|^2 <= x^o(1) (N^4/Q^2)
       [ NQ/x + N Q^(5/2) R^3/x^2 + N^2 Q R/x^2 ].               (E2)

Here x^o(1) means that every fixed positive power x^eta is an available
upper bound after enlarging the threshold, uniformly over the coefficient
family. It consists only of fixed logarithmic and fixed-order divisor
factors on polynomial-sized integers. No merely logarithmic saving is
multiplied by this notation.

The external analytic inputs used are Poisson summation for a fixed smooth
function and the classical complete Kloosterman Weil bound
 |S(u,v;m)| <= tau(m) sqrt(m) sqrt(gcd(u,v,m)).
The elementary divisor subpower bound and reciprocal-totient bound are also
used. In particular this proof does not take Maynard17.2, its Lemma16.1,
L23 Lemma4.7, a moving-residue distribution theorem or a spectral estimate
as an analytic black box. Maynard17.2 is the primary source for the sum
and the three final scale products, which are rederived here.

2. Smooth inverse-phase completion, retaining the zero Fourier frequency

For a fixed smooth compactly supported w on the positive real axis define
 T(A;m)=sum_(n,(n,m)=1) w(n/N) e(A inverse(n)/m).
Its exact Poisson expansion is

 T(A;m)=N/m sum_(k in Z) what(kN/m) S(k,A;m).                    (E3)

The k=0 term is a Ramanujan sum, not automatically zero. The classical
identity for that sum gives |c_m(A)|<=gcd(A,m), hence its absolute size is
at most ||w||_1 (N/m) gcd(A,m).

For nonzero k, use |what(t)|<=C_w(1+|t|)^(-2), and write g=gcd(A,m).
The divisor domination
 sqrt(gcd(k,A,m)) <= sum_(d|g,d|k) sqrt(d)
and the elementary inequality, valid for every Y>0 and positive d,

 sum_(k!=0,d|k) (1+|k|/Y)^(-2) <= 2Y/d                         (E4)

give a total at most
 2 C_w tau(m) sqrt(m) sum_(d|g) d^(-1/2)
 <= 2 C_w tau(m)^2 sqrt(m).
For (E4), compare the decreasing positive summand on each half-axis with
its integral from zero. No additional +1 appears, even if Y<1.
We have therefore proved, for every integer A,

 |T(A;m)| <= C_w [tau(m)^2 sqrt(m)+(N/m) gcd(A,m)].              (E5)

If (a,m)=1, then gcd(a ell,m)=gcd(ell,m). This is the complete reason that
the estimate is uniform in the original residue a. It requires keeping
(a,m)=1 until this step, rather than dropping it before completion.

3. Cauchy with the original, uncontracted r,s support

Absorb the bounded factor (R/r) psihat(hM/(qr)) into c(q,r,h), and use a
fixed smooth nonnegative majorant w(n1/N) for the original n1 interval.
The coefficient outside the r,h sum has absolute value
 <= x^o(1)/(Q^2 R^2),
since q~Q,s~R and 1/phi(qs)<=x^o(1)/(QR).
There are O(Q R N^2) choices of q,s,n1,n2. Cauchy, followed by removing
bounded coefficients and the n2 restriction in a positive upper bound,
gives

 |Z|^2 <= x^o(1) N^3/(Q^3 R^2) * T2,                          (E6)

where
 T2 = sum_(q~Q) sum_(r,r'~R,(a,qrr')=1)
         sum_(1<=|h|,|h'|<=H)
         |sum_(n,(n,qrr')=1) w(n/N)
             e(a ell inverse(n)/(qrr'))|,
 ell=h r'-h' r.

More explicitly, before the last bound the Cauchy sum still contains s
and n2, and its multiplier is x^o(1) N^2/(Q^3 R^3). Expanding the square
in r,h leaves coefficients independent of n; triangle inequality permits
s,n2 to contribute O(RN), which gives (E6). Dependence of bounded inner
coefficients on s is harmless for this particular bound. The identity
between the two inverse phases modulo qr,qr' and the single phase modulo
qrr' holds whenever (n,qrr')=1, without (r,r')=1.
At no point is (r,s)=1 added to the original sum. The corresponding extra
restriction in the printed first Cauchy display is not used here.

4. Exact zero-phase pairs

When ell=0, h and h' have the same sign. For fixed nonzero h and r', the
identity h'r=h r' permits at most 2 tau(|h r'|) choices of h',r, even before
their interval restrictions. Thus the number of (r,r',h,h') is at most
RH x^o(1). Bounding the n sum by O(N) gives

 T2_zero <= x^o(1) Q R H N.                                  (E7)

This argument uses nonzero h; it never calls tau(0). The product |h r'|
is polynomial-sized, so its divisor factor is legitimately subpower.

5. Nonzero phases: both terms in (E5)

For ell!=0 use m=qrr', (a,m)=1, and (E5). The square-root contribution is

 T2_sqrt <= x^o(1) Q^(3/2) R^3 H^2.                          (E8)

The Ramanujan contribution requires a genuine gcd sum. Put d=gcd(r,r'),
r=d u, r'=d v, (u,v)=1. Since ell=d(hv-h'u),

 gcd(ell,rr') <= d^2 gcd(h,u) gcd(h',v)
                <= d^2 gcd(h,r) gcd(h',r').

Also gcd(ell,qrr')<=gcd(ell,q) gcd(ell,rr'). For a fixed nonzero ell,
the identity gcd(ell,q)=sum_(j|ell,j|q) phi(j) yields

 sum_(q~Q) gcd(ell,q) <= 3Q tau(|ell|)=Q x^o(1).               (E9)

Indeed a relevant j is <=2Q and the count of q multiples is at most
Q/j+1<=3Q/j. This is precisely where ell=0 must remain excluded.
For each positive r and every H>=1,

 sum_(1<=|h|<=H) gcd(h,r)
 <= 2H sum_(j|r) phi(j)/j <= 2H tau(r).

Finally,
 sum_(r,r'~R) gcd(r,r')^2
 <= sum_(d<=2R) d^2 * #{r~R:d|r}^2 << R^3.
The domination by all common divisors is in the correct upper-bound
direction; each multiple count is <=3R/d. Combining these estimates,

 sum_(q,r,r',h,h',ell!=0) gcd(ell,qrr') <= x^o(1) Q H^2 R^3.

Multiplying by N/(QR^2), the zero Fourier frequency from (E5) therefore
contributes

 T2_Ramanujan <= x^o(1) N H^2 R.                            (E10)

For (E9), the a-coprimality conditions may now be dropped in an absolute
upper bound, because a has already disappeared from the gcd. The h-sum
can similarly be enlarged after (E9). This order avoids an illegitimate
bound for gcd(a ell,m) when a is not a unit.

6. Quantitative conclusion and actual fixed-d substitution

Equations(E6)--(E10), with H=O(QRN/x (log x)^5), give

 |Z|^2 <= x^o(1) [N^5/(Qx)+N^5 Q^(1/2)R^3/x^2+N^6 R/(Qx^2)],

which is exactly(E2). If each of its three dimensionless scale products
is at most x^(-4 epsilon), then |Z|<=N^2/Q*x^(-2epsilon+o(1)); the usual
weaker N^2/(Q x^epsilon) follows uniformly. Fixed multiples of H or of the
dyadic supports, and fixed smooth q weights, change only the allowed constants.

For the actual restricted P5.2 application fix the small factor d~D, with
D=x^o(1), and write q'=d q and L=d Q1. Arbitrary q'-dependent bounded
coefficients are permitted in(E1), so the indicator d|q' belongs in lambda;
one is not restricting an unweighted cancellation theorem after applying it.
The denominator phi(qdr2) qdr1 and phase are exactly those of(E1) with
Q replaced by L. The actual cutoff differs by D/d=O(1).

The already supplied exact size combinations, rechecked here, use n,q,u,v
for log_x(N,Q1,Q2,Q3), theta=7/32 and |a|>=c0*x:

 (35/16)(q-n)+(23/16)(2n+u+2v)
 +(5/16)(n+q+5u+2v)-v/2 = n+(5/2)q+3u+3v,

whose upper bound is 1021/512-(147/8)sigma+O(1/log x).
The other products have exponents n+q<1-9sigma and
2n+q+u+v<17/10-(29/2)sigma. These imply all three required scale gaps
with epsilon=sigma, after the fixed constants and subpower D costs.
Although(E2) itself has no absolute-a range, this substitution DOES use
|a|>=c0*x to derive the second size gap from B3.

With Lambda divisor bounded, normalize it at an x^o(1) cost only here,
where a power saving is available. The fixed-d bound is
 |E1(d)| << N^2/(d Q1) x^(-sigma+o(1)).
The original E1 is sum_d E1(d); the factor 1/d occurs in this bound through
L=d Q1, not as an extra prefactor to E1(d). Hence sum_(d~D)1/d=O(1).
Absorbing the remaining D=x^o(1) restores the preliminary-dispersion normalization:

 |E1| << N^2/(D Q1) x^(-sigma/2).

The actual bridge's zero-product restoration still supplies its separate,
weaker fixed power loss. This note does not promote x^(1-sigma/5) to a bound
for the whole dispersion proof, and claims only this E1 input.
