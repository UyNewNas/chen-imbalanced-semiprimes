Independent mathematical/source review of the new compact-third enclosure
10 October 2026. This review does not run the adaptive integral or establish
that a pilot has reached the frozen numerical allowance.

Subject: numerical-third-compact-independent-20261010.py. All line references
below refer to the frozen file bound by this review. The new implementation
reuses the separately proved112-bit numerator-chord table and160-bit rational
interval/log arithmetic; it does not import the original E0 enclosure. It
reconstructs the same analytic inequalities, rather than claiming a new
analytic formula. This review is a direct examination of those inequalities
and their implementation, not another execution of the author's integral.

1. Explicit inputs and normalization

The target is the compact part z>=h=1/100 of

 4 integral_[0<z<=y<=x<=rho, x+y+3z<=Theta]
 [f0((Theta-x-y-z)/z)-f0((L-x-y-z)/z)] dx dy dz/(xyz^2),

where Theta=299/500, rho=19/96, mu=25/128, f0=f/(2 exp(gamma)) and
L=min(Theta,Phi) below mu, or v(x) above mu. The reduced envelope identity
for Phi, and the ordinary sieve definition/monotonicity, remain explicit
mathematical inputs; this computation does not prove prime distribution.
On the symmetric extension below mu the reduced Phi is the maximum of
v(x),v(y),v(x+y),v(x+z),v(y+z),v(x+y+z),W(z), with
W(z)=153/256-3z/8. Above mu the symmetric extension is v(max(x,y)).

The integer denominator48000*2^24 represents all four fixed cutoffs exactly.
Every argument passed to the table is dimensionless. The mass formula at
lines161--164 is exactly
 4 log(x1/x0) log(y1/y0) (1/z0-1/z1),
not an extra factor4 beyond the target normalization. Multiplying a point
bound in unitsP by the interval mass in unitsP and dividing byP gives the
correct final unitsP; lower floor and upper ceiling have the right direction.

2. Coverage, pruning, and repeated bins

The finite initial z-cuts include h,mu,rho and an increasing3/2 progression.
Each z-strip [z0,z1] receives a disjoint rectangular partition of
[z0,rho]^2 in x,y, additionally cut at mu. This covers every ordered point
z<=y<=x in that strip. All later integer-midpoint subdivisions partition the
parent rectangle exactly; largest-relative-span selection affects efficiency,
not coverage. The fixed cut at mu is preserved by every subdivision.

The three outside tests in line142 use y0>=x1, z0>=y1, or
x0+y0+3z0>=Theta. An equality case contains at most a lower-dimensional
ordered/raw-boundary subset and therefore has zero3D measure. The density
is bounded on the compact z>=h set, so these equality cases may be discarded.
A boundary rectangle contributes zero lower bound and a nonnegative upper
bound for its whole rectangular mass; this only enlarges the integral.

The halving rule is used only when the x and y intervals are identical and
z1<=y0. Below mu the reduced envelope and the raw cube condition are symmetric
in x,y. Above mu the code explicitly uses the extension v(max(x,y)). Thus
the ordered half of the square has exactly half the full-square integral,
even if the raw cube boundary cuts it. Arbitrary partially overlapping x/y
intervals are NOT halved; they remain conservative boundary boxes. The test
`full` is stronger and also requires the entire raw cube inequality, as
needed for Jensen. No lower bound is assigned to an unverified ordered part.

3. Cap ranges and affine selection

For v(t)=min((1+t)/2,57/64-3t/2), a whole interval lower bound is the minimum
of its endpoint values; an upper bound is the maximum of the endpoints,
or153/256 if it crosses mu. max over branches preserves both directions.
W(z) is decreasing. Above mu, v is decreasing, giving precisely the separate
cap_bounds branch at lines74--75. Taking min with Theta preserves the range.
All endpoint evaluations are floored on the lower side and ceiled on the
upper side. Assertions ca>x1+y1 ensure the positive residual numerator used
for the subsequent argument extrema; failure would abort, not silently prune.

An affine plane is selected only when the exact sign of its difference from
the other tent plane is constant on the entire box. It then equals that
tent branch throughout the box, hence is a minorant of the max envelope.
The extra test max_box plane<=Theta licenses the final clipping. Choosing
the best among these available minorants by midpoint/range score cannot
invalidate the bound; an unavailable plane causes no claimed improvement.

There is a necessary special case: above mu on a symmetric square, v(x)
itself is NOT a pointwise minorant of v(max(x,y)). The code does not use its
mean there. Instead it uses the exact mean of v(max(x,y)) in Section5 below.
The diagonal endpoint extrema computed with the plane v(x) are still the
true extrema of the symmetric residual argument: max(x,y) and x+y increase
together, and57/64-3max(x,y)/2-x-y stays positive. This exception is essential.

4. Rectangular difference bounds and decay

For2<=s, f0 increases and is concave. Indeed the delay equation gives
 f0'(s)=[F0(s-1)-f0(s)]/s >=0,
 f0''(s)=[F0'(s-1)-2 f0'(s)]/s <=0
on each smooth piece, with compatible one-sided derivatives at the delay
breakpoints. The definition f0=0 below2 has a derivative jump at2, so global
concavity across2 must NOT be used. The new code respects this distinction.

The separate extrema in box() use positivity of cap-x-y and Theta-x-y:
the minimum argument is at largest x,y,z and smallest cap, the maximum at
smallest x,y,z and largest cap. Thus separate f0 endpoint intervals give
a valid difference enclosure. The integrand is nonnegative since L<=Theta.

For a fixed increment delta>=0, f0(s+delta)-f0(s) decreases in s>=2.
For s<=2 it is at most f0(2+delta)-f0(2). Hence lines152--153 use
s0=max(2,ao/z1) and delta=(Theta-ca)/z0 correctly, including when a boundary
box extends outside the raw cube. No concavity below2 is assumed.

For D0=F0-f0, D0(s)<=D0(k)(k/s)^2 when3<=k<=s. The implementation selects
only eligible integer anchors and takes their minimum, always rounding up.
Because f0(new)<=the common limit<=F0(old), the difference is at mostD0(old).
When old>=4, f0'(s)<=D0(s-1)/s yields the further upper bound
 D0(ao/z1-1)*(Theta-ca)*z1/(z0*ao).
This is exactly line155's expression, with all positive denominators.

5. Product-weighted Jensen and the old chord

Only a `full` rectangle enters mean_upper. There the new argument is>=2,
so concavity gives E f0(new)<=f0(E new). Under normalized density1/(xyz^2),

 E x=(x1-x0)/log(x1/x0), E y=(y1-y0)/log(y1/y0),
 E(1/z)=(z0+z1)/(2z0z1).

The code's integer-coordinate/D conversions give exactly these physical
quantities. Independence of x,y,z gives E new=(Theta-E x-E y)E(1/z)-1.
The upper interval endpoint for this mean may be passed to increasing f0.

For an affine minorant p=a+b x+d y+e z, b,d<=1 and the residual numerator
a+(b-1)x+(d-1)y>0, the old minorant argument lies between its diagonal
endpoints. If that range crosses2, no chord estimate is used. If entirely
below2, the old lower bound is zero. If entirely above2, the concave f0 lies
above the line joining its endpoint values. Replacing both values by rigorous
lower bounds still leaves a lower line, regardless of the line's slope.
The mean chord uses the exact affine mean and E(1/z). Clipping the interval
for its interpolation weight to[0,1] is legitimate because that exact mean
weight belongs to[0,1]. Every multiplication uses signed interval arithmetic.

For the symmetric square above mu, X,Y are independent log-uniform on[a,b].
Direct integration gives
 E max(X,Y)=2[b log(b/a)-b+a]/log(b/a)^2.
Line128 computes this exact quantity as an interval; line129 then uses
 E old=[57/64-E X-E Y-(3/2)E max(X,Y)] E(1/z)-1.
Its diagonal range is the one justified in Section3. Applying the chord to
this exact mean bounds the symmetric target itself; division by2 is then
valid. Below mu an arbitrary nonsymmetric affine minorant can likewise bound
the full square first, before halving the symmetric target integral.

6. Ordinary queries and finite aggregation

The new112-bit numerator chords have already been independently justified.
Their new wrapper interpolates concave L=s f0(s) below and adds
1/[8 n(i-n)] above; this is M h^2/8 with M<=1/(i/n-1).
Exact knots have no interpolation error. Division by the positive s and
conversion by the exact integer factor2^(160-112) preserve the bounds.
For s>12 the wrapper uses f0(12)<=f0(s)<=F0(12), not extrapolation.
The extension s<=2 is exactly zero. Anchor differences Hhi-Llo are upward
bounds. These statements cover arbitrary queried rational arguments, not
only previously sampled ones.

The heap sums all inserted rectangle lower/upper contributions; before a
split it removes that parent's contribution and adds its two disjoint
children. A zero-width contribution is not put in the heap but remains in
the total, so exact or zero cells are not lost. The loop cannot certify the
saved allowance merely by exhausting its split limit: status explicitly
depends on the final exact upper rational being<=the saved upper rational.
No claim in this review relies on a floating display or on the pilot value.

The output field cell_evaluations is the count of positive-width heap
insertions, not the number of all box calls. sum(counts.values()) is the
actual total number of box evaluations. This naming issue does not affect
integral sums, status or the stream digest; it must be reported accurately.

Conclusion: no incorrect analytic direction, boundary pruning, symmetric
halving, Jensen range or rounding step was found in this scoped direct
review. Attainment of the old compact upper allowance is a separate numerical
run result, and it is intentionally not asserted here. No original leaf
partition or2M stream has been independently replayed by this reviewer.
