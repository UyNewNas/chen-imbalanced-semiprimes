Moving-residue preliminary dispersion: nonzero shifts and the exact-product term
2026-10-09. New proof supplement; not a restoration of the global sieve constants.

1. Scope and additional power margins

Use x=MN, up to fixed constants, and fixed delta>0. Assume
  N,M >= x^delta, QD <= N*x^(-delta), QDR <= x^(1-delta),
and that every scale and |a| are bounded by x^K for fixed K.
Coefficients have a fixed divisor-power bound, with the roughness and
Siegel--Walfisz hypotheses of L23 Proposition4.5 unchanged. Only its E=1
application is needed here. The source reduction is Maynard I,
Proposition14.4 and Lemmas14.1--14.3. The discussion below supplies two
uniformity details not covered by simply saying that proof is uniform in a:
  (i) divisor moments of |mn-a| when the shift is comparable to or exceeds x;
  (ii) the exceptional equality mn=a, where tau(0) must never be used.
Shiu's theorem, the ordinary divisor estimates, Poisson summation and the
source's SW mean-square lemma remain explicitly cited external inputs.
This note does not prove the subsequent E1 or spectral E2 estimates.

2. A uniform shifted divisor-moment bound

Claim. For fixed C,K,delta>0, let I be an interval of length at most L,
contained in [-x^K,x^K], with 1<=q<=L*x^(-delta) and L<=x^K.
For any residue r, after omitting the integer zero,
  sum_{k in I, k=r mod q, k!=0} tau(|k|)^C
    << (L/q) tau(q)^C (log x)^O_{C,K,delta}(1).                 (Z1)
The same statement with an enlarged constant covers intervals intersecting
zero. The parameters K,delta are fixed before x tends to infinity.

Proof. Put g=(r,q), q'=q/g and k=g*t. The progression in t is primitive,
and tau(|g*t|)^C <= tau(g)^C tau(|t|)^C. Its interval length bound is
L'=L/g, with L'/q'=L/q>=x^delta. Split signs. Values with
  0<|t|<=q'*x^(delta/2)
contribute at most (x^(delta/2)+1)*x^(delta/4) times tau(g)^C, by the
fixed-power divisor bound on the polynomial-sized t range. This is
O((L/q)*tau(g)^C).

For the remaining values split into dyadic ranges in |t|. If the range
length is at most L', use the full dyadic interval; otherwise cover the
intersection by at most two positive intervals of length comparable to L'.
In every such interval J, its length H and left endpoint X satisfy
  H>=q'*x^(delta/2)/O(1), H>=x^(delta/2)/O(1), X<=x^K,
  q'<=H*x^(-delta/2)*O(1).
Thus Shiu's theorem applies with fixed positive parameters, for example
alpha=beta=min(1/4,delta/(8K)), after enlarging x. For f(t)=tau(t)^C,
f(p^j)=(j+1)^C<=2^(Cj) and f(t)<<_eta t^eta for every eta>0.
The resulting bound is H/phi(q') times a fixed power of log x.
The dyadic intervals of length below L' have total length O(L'); the
larger intersections have total length O(L') and only O(log x) pieces
even with a wasteful cover. Also q'/phi(q') is bounded by a fixed power
of log x. This proves (Z1), since tau(g)<=tau(q).

The standard primitive Shiu statement used here is recorded, with both
coprimality and interval/modulus hypotheses, in Thomas Wright,
arXiv:2508.17217v1, Theorem1.1, citing Shiu1980. This use is of the classical
fixed-function theorem, not Wright's stronger main theorem. In particular
we do not apply a primitive progression bound to a nonprimitive class.

By Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the same progression, (Z1) gives, for
I subset [1,C0*x], |a|<=x^K, q<=x^(1-delta), and fixed B,C,
  sum_{b in I, b=r mod q, b!=a} tau(b)^B tau(|b-a|)^C
    << (x/q) tau(q)^O_{B,C}(1) (log x)^O_{B,C,K,delta}(1).     (Z2)
Use L=C1*x in (Z1), increasing K slightly to cover fixed constants.
Neither a factor tau(a) nor a pointwise x^eta loss is inserted in this
mean bound. The exact zero b=a has been excluded explicitly.

3. Where these moments replace the fixed-shift source argument

For mn!=a and n~N,m~M, collapse b=mn; the number of factorizations and
all fixed divisor powers in m,n are bounded by a fixed power of tau(b).
Equation (Z2), with q=1, bounds each moment used in the large-coefficient
and large-tau(mn-a) truncation in Maynard Lemma14.3 by x*(log x)^O(1).
Choosing the logarithmic cutoff exponent sufficiently large therefore
gives arbitrary logarithmic savings, uniformly in polynomial-sized a.

The removal of (m,r2)>1 in Lemma14.2 requires an extra f|m. Collapsing
to b now gives f|b; here f<=O(M)<=x^(1-delta)/O(1) after shrinking delta.
Equation (Z2) with q=f gives x/f * tau(f)^O(1) * log^O(1). Its outer
factor 1/f leaves
  sum_{f>z0} tau(f)^O(1)/f^2 << (log z0)^O(1)/z0.
This is the required rough-factor saving. The same collapsed nonzero
moments cover the coefficient cutoffs. The subsequent common-factor and
close-pair exclusions in Lemma14.3 use the logarithmic divisor cutoff,
ordinary progression counts, and roughness. Their counting inequalities
do not require |a|<x-y once mn_i=a is excluded.

4. Delete the exact product before taking the variance

If a<=0 no positive product equals a. Otherwise replace each congruence
indicator in the original discrepancy by
  1_{mn=a mod qdr} - 1_{mn=a}.                               (Z3)
Keep the original coprime mean term unchanged. Since (qdr,a)=1,
the subtracted exact product was indeed counted by the congruence.
There are at most tau(a)=x^o(1) pairs (m,n) with mn=a, and at most
O(QDR) triples (q,d,r), with all divisor-bounded coefficients x^o(1).
Consequently the original first moment changes by
  O(QDR*x^o(1)) = O(x^(1-delta+o(1))),                       (Z4)
which is smaller than x/log^A x for every fixed A.

Apply the original Cauchy step to this modified inner expression.
No SW condition is imposed on an m-dependent sequence: alpha and the
mean term are unchanged, and (Z3) is part of the counting indicator.
After inserting the same nonnegative smooth majorants and expanding the
square, the pure-mean term S1 is unchanged. The cross term S2 has mn1=a
omitted; the two-indicator term S3 has both mn1=a and mn2=a omitted.
Thus all divisors of mn_i-a introduced in the ensuing proof are divisors
of a nonzero integer, and (Z2) applies to their absolute values.

5. Reinsert only after the dangerous double diagonal is excluded

In S2 the terms omitted by mn1=a are bounded as follows. Fix a factorization
(m,n1) of a; there are x^o(1) such choices. The remaining n2 has O(N)
choices, q,d have O(QD) choices, r1,r2 have O(R^2) choices, while the mean
coefficient 1/phi(qdr2) is <<x^o(1)/(QDR). Hence the total omitted S2 is
  O(R*N*x^o(1)).                                            (Z5)
This argument includes all original coprimality restrictions by dropping
them only in an absolute-value upper bound.
Keep the exclusion throughout the rough-factor removal in section3;
reinsert these S2 terms only just before the one-congruence Poisson step.
Thus the original rough-factor argument is never asked to evaluate tau(0).

For S3 perform all the original nonzero divisor cutoffs and rough
coprimality/close-pair exclusions first. In the resulting S4 we have
|n1-n2|>=N/log^C x>0. Therefore both mn1=a and mn2=a cannot occur.
If mn1=a, fix the x^o(1) possible (m,n1). For each q,d the congruence
n2=n1 mod qd leaves O(N/(QD)+1) choices of n2. The integer mn2-a is now
nonzero, so the possible r2 divide it and number x^o(1); r1 still has
O(R) choices. Thus the omitted S4 terms, including their conjugates, total
  O(R*(N+QD)*x^o(1)) = O(R*N*x^o(1)).                       (Z6)
At this point they may be reinserted, and the source's exact CRT/Poisson
calculation applies to the full smooth m sum. The mean-term restoration
and SW mean-square steps then proceed with the original alpha.

The scale of the raw dispersion remainder is MN^2/(QD)=xN/(QD).
Relative to this scale, (Z5)--(Z6) cost
  (RN)/(xN/(QD)) = QDR/x <= x^(-delta),                     (Z7)
up to x^o(1). After the outer Cauchy step their contribution is bounded
by x^(1-delta/2+o(1)); it is absorbed by every fixed logarithmic target.
Together with (Z4), this supplies the exact-product correction to the
E=1 preliminary dispersion reduction under the stated power margins.

It would be incorrect to reinsert the double exact diagonal before the
close-pair exclusion: that crude term is QD*R^2*x^o(1), whose comparison
would demand (QDR)^2<xN. This stronger condition does not follow from
the four monomial conditions of Proposition5.2. The order above avoids
requiring that unjustified additional inequality.

6. Scope of the resulting preliminary bridge

Subject to the explicitly cited Shiu, Poisson and SW inputs and the
unchanged elementary nonzero dispersion manipulations just located,
the E=1 Proposition4.5 bound extends to |a|<=x^K with the additional
fixed power margins in section1. The extra errors in sections4--5 are
absorbed by its x/log^A x error. This statement supplies no new bound
for E1 or E2 and is not a reproof of any spectral theorem. Combining it
with a retained-phase E2 estimate still requires all of that estimate's
hypotheses and an independent final parameter audit.
