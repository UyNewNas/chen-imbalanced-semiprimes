Preliminary dispersion: the common main term and its positive SW remainder
2026-10-10. New mathematical reconstruction, restricted to e=1.

0. Result and independence

The three dispersion terms have a common main term with their original
q,d,r coefficients and coprime means. Their remaining zero-frequency
difference is a positive Gram-weighted Siegel--Walfisz variance. This
statement is exact for the restored main terms, not an assertion that
the two-indicator term has no nonzero Fourier frequencies.

One detail needs more care than the printed transition to Maynard (14.6):
the restrictions (n_i,r_i)=1 cannot simply disappear from an identity.
I retain them throughout. Normalizing alpha_n 1_(n,r)=1 by tau(r)^B
preserves uniform auxiliary-coprimality SW. Its BDH cost tau(r)^(2B)
is retained and averaged over r, costing logarithms only. Thus no
unproved free uniformity in the auxiliary integer r is required.

I previously participated in the project's exact-product repair review
and long-variable audits. This is a fresh reconstruction of the principal
terms, not a wholly independent review of every earlier error estimate.
The preliminary roughness/close-pair error reductions are located below;
Shiu, ordinary divisor means, smooth Poisson and the stated BDH lemma are
explicit analytic inputs. No estimate of the later E1/E2 exponential sums,
no full distribution theorem and no global K,c,b are proved here.

1. Quantifiers and notation

Fix delta>0, a divisor exponent B>=1, and K>=1. All constants in the
coefficient and SW bounds are uniform if those coefficients depend on a.
Let MN be comparable to x, M,N>=x^delta, QD<=N*x^(-delta),
QDR<=x^(1-delta), and 0<|a|<=x^K. Every scale is polynomially bounded.
Set Y=QD and ell=qd. Each (q,d) pair remains separately weighted; we
collapse ell only in an absolute upper bound, with multiplicity <=tau(ell).
Always (ell,a)=1 and r~R, (r,a)=1. Write eta_r=lambda_(q,d,r).
Assume |eta_r|<=tau(ell*r)^B and |alpha_n|<=tau(n)^B.
The single alpha sequence is fixed before the q,d,r summations; a
modulus-dependent choice of alpha is not allowed by this argument.
The original rough support P^-(r),P^-(n)>=Z is kept, with
Z=x^(1/(log log x)^3), or the chart-fixed comparable threshold already
permitted by the preliminary error reduction. No roughness hypothesis is
needed for the finite Gram identity itself.

The full auxiliary SW condition is, for every integer d0>=1 and A>0,

 |sum_(n~N,n=b mod s,(n,d0)=1) alpha_n
   - 1/phi(s) sum_(n~N,(n,d0*s)=1) alpha_n|
 <= C_A N tau(d0)^B (log N)^(-A),                 (SW)

uniformly in (b,s)=1. B may be enlarged to include the source SW exponent.
Let psi be a fixed nonnegative smooth majorant and h0=integral psi>=0.
Write w_q=psi(q/Q), and psi_m=psi(m/M).

2. The exact square, including the deleted exact product

For fixed (q,d), define

 B_r=sum_(n~N,(n,ell*r)=1) alpha_n,
 A_r(b)=sum_(n~N,n=b mod ell,(n,r)=1) alpha_n,  (b,ell)=1,
 I_r(m,n)=1_(mn=a mod ell*r),
 E(m,n)=1_(mn=a),
 C_r(m)=1_(m,ell*r)=1 * B_r/phi(ell*r),
 X_r(m)=sum_(n~N,(n,ell*r)=1) alpha_n [I_r(m,n)-E(m,n)].

For negative a, E is identically zero. For positive a, (ell*r,a)=1
implies that E=1 already imposes all the displayed coprimalities.
The modified discrepancy at m is sum_r eta_r[X_r(m)-C_r(m)].
After Cauchy eliminates beta_m and gamma_(q,d), and the nonnegative
majorants extend the q,m sums, the resulting variance is exactly

 V'=S1-2 Re S2'+S3',
 S1=sum_(q,d) w_q sum_m psi_m |sum_r eta_r C_r(m)|^2,
 S2'=sum_(q,d) w_q sum_m psi_m
                         (sum_r eta_r X_r(m)) overline(sum_s eta_s C_s(m)),
 S3'=sum_(q,d) w_q sum_m psi_m |sum_r eta_r X_r(m)|^2.       (1)

The original first moment differs from the modified one by at most
QDR*x^o(1). The Cauchy multiplier is M QD (log x)^O_B(1), since there
is only one compatible residue b=a*m^(-1) modulo ell when e=1.
No 1/phi(ell) is lost or introduced at this stage.

Expanding S2' gives the coefficient eta_r*conj(eta_s)/phi(ell*s),
the n-conditions (n1,ell*r)=(n2,ell*s)=1, and the m-conditions
mn1=a mod ell*r, (m,s)=1, mn1!=a. Expanding S3' gives eta_r*conj(eta_s),
the two congruences, n1=n2 mod ell, and both mn_i!=a. The latter n
congruence follows since m is invertible modulo ell. In particular the
double exact-product diagonal has not been omitted from an expansion:
it is absent from the squared modified indicator from the outset.

3. Pure mean: the common coefficient is a Gram kernel

Put delta(u)=phi(u)/u and

 c_ell(r,s)=delta(ell*r*s)/(phi(ell*r) phi(ell*s)).           (2)

Then elementary smooth counting of coprime m gives

 MT=M h0 sum_(q,d) w_q sum_(r,s) eta_r conj(eta_s)
                                      c_ell(r,s) B_r conj(B_s),
 S1=MT+O(N^2 (log x)^C/Y).                                (3)

Indeed sum_(m,(m,ell*r*s)=1) psi_m=M h0 delta(ell*r*s)
+O(tau(ell*r*s)); summing this error uses ordinary divisor means.
The fixed power lower bound on M absorbs it into V(log x)^(-A),
where V=MN^2/Y=xN/Y, with any requested fixed A.

For a finite set of supported r, choose any common multiple L of
ell and all these r. The exact identity

 c_ell(r,s)=1/L sum_(v mod L)
 [1_(v,ell*r)=1/phi(ell*r)] [1_(v,ell*s)=1/phi(ell*s)]       (4)

shows that c_ell is a real positive semidefinite Gram matrix.
L is used only to prove a finite identity; it is never counted in an
error bound and is not assumed polynomially small. This observation
also proves MT is real and nonnegative for complex eta and alpha.
No assumption (q,d)=1, squarefreeness of the moduli, or (r,s)=1 is used.

4. The cross term and the exact place roughness is used

Keep mn1!=a while removing (m,s)>1. The nonzero shifted-divisor estimate
(Z2) in lichtman-zero-product-dispersion-repair-20261009.txt bounds that
removal by V(log x)^C/Z. Only then restore mn1=a, immediately before
one-congruence Poisson. Its absolute cost is RN*x^o(1), not QD R^2.
The zero Fourier frequency has coefficient

 M h0 eta_r conj(eta_s)/(ell*r*phi(ell*s)) * B_r conj(B_s).

Its ratio with (2) is

 c_ell(r,s) / [1/(ell*r*phi(ell*s))]
 =delta(ell*r*s)/delta(ell*r)
 =product_(p|s,p not dividing ell*r) (1-1/p).              (5)

Consequently (5)=1+O(log x/Z), uniformly over all supported r,s.
This is not an exact equality before making that roughness comparison.
The summed error is V(log x)^C/Z, by divisor means for B_r and eta.
With E1 defined by the remaining frequencies 0<|h|<=QDR log^5(x)/M,
using the full phase e(a*h*inverse(n1)/(ell*r)), we have

 S2'=MT+M E1+O(V(log x)^(-A)+RN*x^o(1)).                 (6)

Here and below logarithmic cutoff exponents are chosen for the final A.
There is no expansion or deletion of an a-dependent phase in (6).

5. The two-indicator term, CRT, and restoration of the main term

Maintain both exact-product exclusions during the shifted-divisor
cutoffs, rough common-factor exclusions and close-pair exclusion.
On the resulting clean set retain

 (r,s)=1, (s,ell)=1,
 (n1,ell*r)=(n2,ell*s)=1, (n1,n2)=1,
 n1=n2 mod ell, |n1-n2|>=N/(log x)^C.                      (7)

These are the source restrictions for E=1. Only after n1!=n2 is fixed
may a single exact product be restored. If mn1=a, fixing that
factorization leaves O(N/Y+1) choices of n2 per (q,d); s divides the
nonzero mn2-a while r has O(R) choices. The two single-zero cases total
R(N+Y)x^o(1)=RN*x^o(1). Both zeros cannot occur in (7).

For (7), the two congruences specify exactly one class m=b modulo ell*r*s:

 b=a*inverse(n1) mod ell*r, b=a*inverse(n2) mod s.

Compatibility with the missing ell part of the second congruence is
precisely n1=n2 mod ell. Poisson therefore gives h=0 equal to M h0/(ell*r*s),
and retains for h!=0 the CRT phase

 e(a*h*[inverse(n1*s)/(ell*r)+inverse(n2*ell*r)/s]).         (8)

Under (r,s)=1 and (s,ell)=1 there is the EXACT Euler-factor identity

 phi(ell)*c_ell(r,s)=1/(ell*r*s).                         (9)

In fact (9) already holds whenever no prime outside ell divides both
r and s. Thus the E=1 zero-frequency conversion on the clean set has
no missing totient factor and needs no approximate prime-product identity.

Restore the exclusions in (7) ONLY IN THIS h=0 main term, not in the
nonzero-frequency expression (8). Keep (n1,ell*r)=(n2,ell*s)=1.
The resulting complete zero term is

 J=M h0 sum_(q,d) w_q phi(ell) sum_(r,s) eta_r conj(eta_s)c_ell(r,s)
                           sum_(b mod ell,(b,ell)=1) A_r(b)conj(A_s(b)). (10)

The standard restoration bounds used at this step are
O(V(log x)^C/Z + MN(log x)^C + V(log x)^(C-Cclose)).
They concern ordinary n-congruences, not divisors of a zero shifted product.
For example a shared rough prime f in n1,n2 is coprime to ell, so the
two counts have respective sizes N/f and N/(ell*f)+1. A shared prime
in r,s gives two factors 1/f; a shared prime in s,ell gives one from
each modulus sum. The divisor-weighted sums are the source's ordinary
mean estimates, leaving sum_(f>Z) tau(f)^C/f^2, hence logarithms/Z.
For close n pairs replace N by N/(log x)^Cclose in one progression
count; its +1 term contributes MN(log x)^C. These are exactly the
restoration operations at Maynard v2 lines4839--4856, with fewer
conditions removed because the two own r-coprimalities are retained.
The rough-cutoff and restoration estimates, including their divisor
means, remain the preliminary error reductions already checked in the
bottom audit; the present note does not replace them by pointwise x^o/Z.

Let E2 consist of the remaining h!=0 terms on (7), with its coefficient
eta_r conj(eta_s)/(ell*r*s) and phase (8). Consequently

 S3'=J+M E2+O(V(log x)^(-A)+RN*x^o(1)).                   (11)

The signed error in (11) can be complex in notation; its magnitude has
the stated bound. In the final real variance use |E2|. Restoring the
close pairs after this Poisson step never recreates a tau(0).

6. Exact positive remainder, with all coprime means retained

For b coprime to ell put d_r(b)=A_r(b)-B_r/phi(ell). Since
sum_b A_r(b)=B_r, elementary expansion gives exactly

 sum_b A_r(b)conj(A_s(b))-B_r conj(B_s)/phi(ell)
       =sum_b d_r(b)conj(d_s(b)).                         (12)

Equations (3),(10),(12) give J=MT+P, with

 P=M h0 sum_(q,d) w_q phi(ell) sum_(r,s) eta_r conj(eta_s)c_ell(r,s)
                                                sum_b d_r(b)conj(d_s(b)).

Using (4), each (q,d) contribution equals

 M h0 w_q phi(ell)/L sum_(v mod L) sum_(b mod ell)^*
   |sum_r eta_r 1_(v,ell*r)=1 d_r(b)/phi(ell*r)|^2 >=0.    (13)

Thus the common main terms cancel as 1-2+1, while the positive SW
variance remains. There is neither a fictitious exact cancellation of
P nor a need to assume its sign from complex r-coefficients individually.

7. Bounding P without discarding the r-coprimalities

Use the following explicit BDH input, for every fixed A,D>0:
if a divisor-bounded sequence satisfies full auxiliary SW uniformly,
then for Q0<=N/(log N)^C(A,D,B),

 sum_(s<=Q0) tau(s)^D sum_b^* |sum_n alpha_n
 [1_(n=b mod s)-1_(n,s)=1/phi(s)]|^2 << N^2/(log N)^A.    (BDH)

This is the correct coprime-mean form of Maynard Lemma13.8, and can
also be supplied by a primitive-character large-sieve derivation. The
printed line4202 with an unqualified constant mean is not used here.
For the current insertion, the separate new proof
lichtman-auxiliary-sw-mean-square-20261010.txt, sections1--7, supplies
this precise input directly from primitive-character large sieve and
(SW). I read its fixed-conductor/inducing-modulus decomposition: the
coefficient retains (n,k)=1 before applying large sieve at fixed k,
and the small-conductor step retains the full auxiliary SW price.
That proof does not use the Gram identity of this note, so the two
components can be joined without a circular appeal to dispersion.

For each r, the normalized sequence

 alpha^(r)_n=alpha_n 1_(n,r)=1 / tau(r)^B                 (14)

has the SAME uniform SW constants: the auxiliary d0 condition for its
numerator is that for alpha with d0*r, and
tau(d0*r)^B/tau(r)^B<=tau(d0)^B. Its divisor bound does not increase.
Hence (BDH), uniformly in r with the same cutoff Q0, gives

 sum_(ell<=8Y) tau(ell)^D sum_b^* |d_r(b)|^2
                  << tau(r)^(2B) N^2/(log N)^A.          (15)

This tau(r) factor is kept, not absorbed into a constant. Bound (13)
by Cauchy over its O(R) r terms, use phi(ell*r)^(-1)<<log x/(ell*r),
|eta_r|<=tau(ell)^B tau(r)^B and phi(ell)<=ell. Since ell~Y,
and there are at most tau(ell) original (q,d) pairs,

 P << M(log x)^2/(Y R) sum_(r~R) tau(r)^(2B)
                 sum_(ell<=8Y) tau(ell)^(2B+1) sum_b^* |d_r(b)|^2.

Choose D>=2B+1 in (15). Ordinary divisor means give
sum_(r~R) tau(r)^(4B)<<R(log x)^C_B. Therefore, for any A0,

 0<=P<<MN^2/(Y(log x)^A0),                               (16)

by selecting the BDH exponent A>A0+C_B+2. The fixed power gap
Y<=N*x^(-delta), with log N comparable to log x, supplies the common
BDH cutoff for every r. No number of r-families is absorbed into SW
constants, and no pointwise subpower factor multiplies a log-saving.
In this application r is fixed first and (BDH) then sums over ALL ell.
The normalized alpha^(r) does not depend on ell or its representation
ell=qd. Any q,d dependence of lambda has already been removed by its
absolute divisor bound. The condition (a,ell)=1 can be dropped in this
nonnegative variance upper bound; it does not change the sequence.

8. Assembly and exact-product budget

Equations (1),(3),(6),(11),(13) yield

 V'=P+M(E2-2 Re E1)+O(V(log x)^(-A)+RN*x^o(1)),

with real parts understood for the harmless complex error notation.
In particular V'<=P+M(2|E1|+|E2|)+the absolute error.
Multiplying by M Y(log x)^C and taking square roots gives

 |S(a)| << x/(log x)^A0
       + M sqrt(Y)(log x)^C (sqrt(|E1|)+sqrt(|E2|)),       (17)

after increasing the preliminary logarithmic exponents. The restored
single-zero contribution has squared-first-moment size
(MY)(RN)x^o(1)=x*(QDR)x^o(1), hence square-root size
x^(1-delta/2+o(1)). The original exact-product correction QDR*x^o(1)
is smaller. Conversely premature reinsertion of both zeros could cost
QD R^2*x^o(1) in the variance and would require the extra unsupported
condition (QDR)^2<xN. No such reinsertion occurs above.

Uniformity in a enters here only through the explicitly uniform
nonzero shifted moments, the power-bounded exact-product correction,
and the original uniform SW/divisor bounds. CRT h=0 is independent
of a; CRT h!=0 retains its full phase. This note supplies no license
to bound or simplify that phase in a subsequent spectral argument.
