Assembling the repaired distribution input for the licensed finite families
2026-10-09. New analytic assembly; no change to the open manuscript or constants.

Claim and explicit boundary

The new restricted convolution estimate can now be inserted into the
finite prime-product distribution argument, for the licensed coefficient
classes stated below. The conclusion is a signed discrepancy estimate,
not yet a recertification of the numerical upper K, lower c, or b transfer.
This is a derivation relative to the named classical and spectral inputs.
The component reviews are internal cross-checks, not formal verification
or external peer review. None of the original full-a source statements
is restored by this assembly.

Fix an even target T, a positive cutoff a_star, a fixed maximum number
k_star of original primes, a finite smooth geometric family W_T with
each prime >=c*T^a_star and total product O(T), and one compiled signed
modulus coefficient rho_T. All policies, atom labels, compact trims,
rough-rank bounds and smoothing precisions are fixed before T grows.
Their constants may depend on these choices. No extra arithmetic filter
is inserted on an outer prime or individual HB slot.

The coefficient rho_T is supported on moduli coprime to T, with total
level T^theta_star for fixed theta_star<3/5. At high rough rank it is the
actual finite sum of labelled prime-convolution atoms and a small-prime
beta factor. The slot partition is fixed on the ENTIRE prime box for
each Type II scale. Its upper endpoint vector has full squares and
either the selected-subproduct complement squares or the repaired
strict W licence, with a positive buffered margin. The squarefree
restriction is removed only after paying its fixed-power error. Group
factorials and the one original repeated-label factorial are kept exactly.
At low rough rank the actual coefficient has a fixed constant bound,
and its COMPLETE support, including beta and endpoint losses, lies
strictly below x^(1/2) after the local-scale conversion. A mere support
level or pointwise factorization property is not an alternative input.

For these data, for every fixed A>0, the derived conclusion is

 sum_{l,(l,T)=1} rho_T(l) [
       sum_{p1...pk = T (mod l)} W_T(p1,...,pk)
       - phi(l)^(-1) sum_{(p1...pk,l)=1} W_T(p1,...,pk) ]
                         = O_A(T/log(T)^A).                 (PP)

This is uniform along all sufficiently large even T, with the fixed
choices above. It is not an almost-all-target assertion, and its error
is absolute, not proportional to a possibly tiny single-cell mass.
Finite signed or positive combinations have the same property.

3. The medium case

Use one individual medium variable. The explicit Brun/Mobius proof in

in all auxiliary integers, all moduli, arbitrary subintervals, normalized

It uses only classical unrough Mobius SW as a non-elementary distribution
input. It does not use the restricted large-modulus theorem to justify
that theorem's own coefficient condition. The root SW/L review checks
this argument independently, including its signed Brun gap.

The actual endpoint partition gives three one-bounded modulus factors
after the fixed factorial normalization and attachment of beta. Put
A0=U*x^(-zeta). The full/selected or W argument supplies the endpoint
monomials; replacing A0 by U leaves savings zeta, 2*zeta and 9*zeta/16.
The separate local and beta/fine reserves cost at most zeta/8, leaving
more than the required 8*sigma or15*sigma. The first factor also has
its fixed zeta-scale slack. Thus all FOUR restricted P5.2 inequalities
hold, including the factor T^(7/32). The complement is divisor-bounded;

The actual residue is T and the source scale is the COMPLETE integer,

The one-bounded repaired convolution, its coefficient extension, and

4. Exhaustive alternatives

If there is no medium slot in the buffered original chart, every
original group must have a long variable. Otherwise its at most six
short scales have product <X^(18*zeta), too small for that group.
There are at most two long variables, since three exceed the complete
chart product. They cannot be truncated mu variables. With one long
variable the short product is <=x^(16*zeta); with two it is
<=x^(32*zeta). The independent long reconstruction safely uses the
larger bounds x^(20*zeta) and x^(40*zeta).

The one-long proof is a primitive progression sieve plus its exact
coprime-mean correction. The two-long proof uses double Poisson and
the complete Kloosterman Weil bound, uniformly for every primitive
residue. The new reconstruction retains the frequency-cutoff losses
and a stronger sufficient modulus condition, rather than using the
source's mismatched Q expressions unchanged. Its chart-window roughness
removal, short interval endpoints and small-divisor freezing are treated
with the reviewed nonzero shifted moments, with every intermediate
complete product=T handled separately. The root review checks these
repairs against the original proof. No questioned real-phase deletion
or medium spectral theorem is used in the long alternatives.

At low rough modulus rank, use ordinary BV for one original prime and
generalized BV for a product, with one original prime interval as the
SW factor and the others as its divisor-bounded complement. Both
lengths have fixed positive power bounds in the product case. The
classical below-half theorem has a maximum over all primitive residue
classes, which contains T mod l for each (T,l)=1. Prime SW with the
required auxiliary coprimality follows by deleting the primes dividing

The bounded actual modulus coefficients are therefore permitted.

5. Recombination errors and the complete target

The root HB reduction and its separate review show that normalized
logarithms, the finite-dimensional Mellin separation and the number of
dyadic/HB blocks only cost fixed logarithmic powers. The Mellin tail
is paid AFTER summing moduli: for complete v!=T, the modulus sum is
bounded by a fixed divisor power of |v-T|, and the reviewed Z2 controls
the resulting fixed moments. For v=T the first-moment contribution is
T^(theta_star+o(1)). No tau(0) expression is used, and no subpower
multiplier is applied to a merely logarithmic remainder.

Proper prime powers cost T^(1-a_star/2+o(1)), plus the isolated
T^(theta_star+o(1)) term. Rough-square modulus collisions cost
(T+T^theta_star)*T^(-omega+o(1)). Each has a fixed power reserve.
For the low complete-product tail v<2*T^(1-kappa0), eventually v!=T,
so summing all fixed divisor-order coefficients gives at most
T^(1-kappa0+o(1)); the exact coprime mean adds only logarithmic factors.
This supplies the tail for every fixed positive kappa0, however small.

Choose the input arbitrary-log exponents after the fixed piece counts
and Mellin truncation requirements. Recombine the local bounds and
these power errors to obtain (PP). This sum includes all positive
product and ordinary-prime signed discrepancies in the stated finite
licensed classes. The same already fixed rho_T is used in counting

6. What has, and has not, now been supplied
