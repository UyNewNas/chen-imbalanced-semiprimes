2. A different logarithm primitive and its rounding

For y in [1,2], set z=1-1/y in [0,1/2]. Then
 log y = sum_(k=1)^m z^k/k + remainder,
 0 <= remainder <= z^(m+1)/[(m+1)(1-z)].
The work denominator is P=2^160 and m=192. The lower/upper z and each power
are rounded down/up, respectively; lower terms are floored and upper terms
ceiled. The displayed geometric remainder uses the upper (m+1)-st power and
the smaller denominator 1-z_upper. All signs are positive. For x>=1, repeated
division by 2 writes x=2^k*y; the two log2 endpoints are multiplied by k>=0.
For x<1 the implementation negates and swaps the reciprocal interval.
Thus no atanh expansion or author log routine is used.

For the 70 exact recalibration masses, a separate unrounded Fraction sum of
384 terms uses z=1-1/x directly, 1<=x<=25/12. The same exact tail bound applies.
This avoids a fixed precision larger than the truncation error of a saved
exact 48/64-term atanh lower polynomial. The program compares those saved
mass lower bounds to its own lower enclosure, not merely to a numerical
approximation or to an upper bound for the true mass.

3. JTable: analytic integral series instead of another mesh

The original JTable computes J(s)=int_2^(s-1) log(v-1)/v dv for 3<=s<=5.
Make the exact substitution z=(v-2)/(v-1). Its integrand becomes
 -log(1-z)/[(1-z)(2-z)]. Expanding two positive convergent series gives
 J(s)=sum_(n>=1) c_n*z_s^(n+1)/(n+1), z_s=(s-3)/(s-2),
 c_n=H_n-d_n, H_n=sum_(k=1)^n 1/k,
 d_0=0, d_n=d_(n-1)/2+1/(2n).
Equivalently c_n=sum_(k=1)^n (1-2^(-(n-k+1)))/k. In particular
0<=c_n<=H_n<=n. After n=m terms the integrated tail is at most
 z_s^(m+2)/(1-z_s). All ten actual s0 queries use m=256 and z_s<=2/3.
This is an exact analytic series with an exact positive remainder, not a
reproduction of either the original or the previous review's Darboux table.

The original JTable source was also read directly. Its q(v)=log(v-1)/v is
increasing for v in [2,4], since
 q'(v)=[v/(v-1)-log(v-1)]/v^2>0;
the new log enclosure verifies log3<4/3. Every original lower prefix adds
a downward product at the left endpoint, every upper prefix an upward product
at the right endpoint. Its floor lower-index/ceil upper-index query extends
the integration interval outwards. Clipping s to [3,5] is legitimate for the
actual ten queries (all s0 in [3.2,5]); this is NOT a certificate for calls
with a true s>5. At s0=5 only outward fixed-point overshoot is clipped.
No original prefix-array value or saved baseline is accepted on the strength
of this source inspection alone: the independently enclosed full baselines
below must fit inside both saved endpoints for all 70 actions.

4. Ordinary W1 integral: two-sided Simpson enclosure

For each action put l=1/s0, b=min(1/s,1-2/s0). The ordinary term is
 O=int_l^b L(u)/(2u(1-u)) du, L(u)=log(s0(1-u)-1).
All 70 intervals satisfy 0<l<b<1/2 and s0(1-b)-1>=1, so the maximum-with-1
in the author formula introduces no interior nonsmooth point. Its omitted
part, if b<1/s, is identically zero.

With g(u)=1/u+1/(1-u), the integrand is L*g/2. For 1<=k<=4,
 |L^(k)(u)| <= (k-1)! [s0/(s0(1-b)-1)]^k,
 0<=L(u)<=log(s0(1-l)-1),
 |g^(k)(u)| <= k! [l^(-k-1)+(1-b)^(-k-1)] for 0<=k<=4.
Hence B4=(1/2)*sum_(k=0)^4 binom(4,k)*L_k*G_(4-k) bounds the absolute fourth
derivative. Composite Simpson with n=64 even subintervals has absolute error
at most (b-l)*((b-l)/64)^4*B4/180. Its node coefficients are all positive,
so substituting lower/upper log enclosures gives a two-sided interval after
subtracting/adding this error. There is no unproved derivative sign,
convexity assumption, or cancellation of interval errors.

The other mass is
 T=C*((s0+s)*log(s0/s)-2*(s0-s))>0.
The independent action enclosure is
 [-J_upper+O_lower-K0*T_upper/8,
  -J_lower+O_upper-K0*T_lower/8].
The script requires the entire independent enclosure to be contained in
[saved_base_lower/Q,saved_base_upper/Q] for every action, and independently
requires T_upper<=saved_triple_upper/Q. It records all derivative constants,
errors, query intervals, and domination slacks in the witness.

5. Positive weights, actual endpoints, and finite-generation logic

Each Buchstab cell weight is log(b/a). Each W1 h-cell preimage is exactly
[u0,u1]=[1/s0,1/s] intersect [1-b/s0,1-a/s0], for source cells a>=2, and its
positive mass is (log(u1/u0)+log((1-u0)/(1-u1)))/2. The script reconstructs
all 1858 nonempty preimages, proves positivity and checks each saved lower
weight against a new independent lower enclosure; all 200 Buchstab weights
are checked likewise. It also checks all target lists and every point index:
s0 is the right endpoint of the selected closed cell. This uses its proven
uniform cell lower bound, not interpolation or a false continuity inference.
All targets have right endpoint<=s<=3, so the ordinary A-shift is zero.

There is no action supremum exchanged with an integral: these are exactly
70 fixed actions and a finite maximum of their proved lower bounds. Positive
weights permit replacement of each source value by a uniform lower bound.
At each finite round, all right sides use only the preceding generation.
Per-product floor, sum, division by the positive action denominator, and
maximum all preserve the lower-bound direction. The seed and the W2 typed
actions remain separately proved inputs; no infinite fixed-point claim is
required. Existing complete recurrence replays are not rerun here.

For calibration let b0<=O-J-K0*T/8 and 0<=Tlo<=T. Then, for K1<K0,
 b0+floor(Q*(K0-K1)*Tlo/8)/Q <= O-J-K1*T/8.
The same argument may be applied successively with the declared anchors.
The new checker validates all 70 saved 48-term masses, all 70 saved 64-term
masses/slopes, the 120 and 312 corrections, and the 70 baselines in each of
the 375/430 terminal arms. It also directly compares those terminal bases to
the independently enclosed analytic base at that arm's K. It does not claim
to re-prove the 36 W2 cost calibrations.
