Literal ordinary Rosser densities for the target-dependent local factors
2026-10-10. New local derivation; no frozen source, state or constant changed.

1. Precise statement and scope

Let T tend to infinity through even integers. Set
 g_0(p)=1_{p>2}/(p-1),  g_T(p)=g_0(p)1_{p does not divide T},
 V_T(z)=product_{p<z}(1-g_T(p)).
Extend g multiplicatively to squarefree products. All cutoffs below use
strict p<z; 1 is always included as a divisor and as an empty continuation.

For R=T^b, y=T^t define the literal raw upper coefficient to be mu(d)
on decreasing prime strings p1>...>pr<y satisfying
 p1...p_{j-1} p_j^3<R at every odd j<=r,
and zero otherwise. The literal raw lower coefficient uses the even j
tests and the initial test p1^2<R. Write their actual finite divisor sums
as W_T^+(R,y) and W_T^-(R,y), respectively. These are specific weights,
not weights whose existence is asserted by an upper/lower sieve theorem.

For each compact parameter set
 K+ subset {(b,t): b>t>0}, or K- subset {(b,t): b>=2t>0},
the conclusions are, uniformly for (b,t) in that set and all even T,
 W_T^+(T^b,T^t)/V_T(T^t)=F(b/t)+o_K(1),                 (R1+)
 W_T^-(T^b,T^t)/V_T(T^t)=f(b/t)+o_K(1).                 (R1-)
Here F=2 exp(gamma) Fbar and f=2 exp(gamma) fbar are the ordinary
linear-sieve functions. In particular this is the ordinary F, not F*.
The lower endpoint s=2 is included. The upper range 1<s<2 is established
by an exact support identity in section7, not by enlarging a citation's
range without proof.

The positive lower bound on t is necessary. Merely saying that s=b/t
is fixed is insufficient if y fails to grow: y=3,R=9 has s=2 but its
upper density ratio is exactly 1, not F(2)=exp(gamma). This note does
not assert uniformity as t tends to zero with T, nor as s tends to infinity.
Fixed positive cutoffs in a previously selected finite construction give
exactly the compact sets used in (R1). Removing such cutoffs afterwards
needs its separate density-tail argument.

2. Primary inputs actually used, with their hypotheses checked

Primary source S is Yuta Suzuki, The Rosser--Iwaniec Sieve, March14,2023,
102 pages, downloaded previously from the author's site:
 https://www.suzuyu1729.com/notes/NagoyaSeminar2022/RosserIwaniec.pdf
The bound local PDF is authoritative; its printed and one-based PDF
page numbers coincide. Its accompanying extraction has control-character
artifacts, so signs below are identified by the displayed formula and
the adjacent proof, not by an isolated malformed text character.

(S-a) S pp8--10, (6.1), Lemmas5.2,6.1,6.2: beta=2 means precisely the
cubic tests above, with first exits of odd/even rank for upper/lower.
Section6 works initially at s>=beta. Our additional lower p1^2<R test
is automatic when p1<y and y<=sqrt(R), and hence does not change this
literal support on K-. The exact positive-exit identity is (6.2) and
(14.29), not an asymptotic estimate.

(S-b) S p13, Definition8.2: a local function lies in Omega(kappa,K0)
when 0<=g(p)<1 and, for all z>=w>=2,
 product_{w<=p<z}(1-g(p))^{-1}
 <= (log z/log w)^kappa (1+K0/log w), with K0>=2 fixed.       (R2)
Classical Mertens product estimates give (R2) for g_0 with kappa=1
and an absolute K0. Indeed V_0(z)=c/log z*(1+O(1/log z)); the bounded
small-w range is absorbed by enlarging K0. Deleting p|T decreases the
left side, so the same K0 also works for every g_T. The proof below
needs the analytic one-sided density theorem only for the single fixed
function g_0; thus target uniformity is not hidden in that invocation.
Classical Mertens prime-harmonic and product estimates are external inputs.

(S-c) S pp18,22--23, (9.1),(9.2), Proposition9.4: define nonnegative
functions a_j by
 a_1(s)=(3-s)_+/s,
 a_j(s)=s^{-1} integral_{max(s,2+epsilon_j)}^{j+2}
                  a_{j-1}(u-1) du,
 epsilon_j=1 for odd j, 0 for even j; empty integrals are zero.       (R3)
Odd functions are used on s>1, even functions on s>=2. Proposition9.4
gives compact-uniform convergence CONDITIONAL on convergence. We do not
mistake it for the convergence proof.

The convergence hypothesis is supplied by Proposition11.8, pp64--69.
Its conditions are beta in J and beta>rho_tilde. At kappa=1,
J=(1,infinity), q(s)=r_{1,1}(s)=s-1 (Proposition10.8(ii), p33), and
rho=1. The positive adjoint solution is
 p(s)=integral_0^infinity exp(-s x-Ein(x)) dx >0,
as used explicitly in its proof on p67. The function on pp60--62 is
 D(s)=p(s-1)q(s)+q(s-1)p(s),
and D(s)>0 iff s>rho_tilde on J intersect (rho,infinity).
Consequently D(2)=p(1)>0 proves STRICT beta=2>rho_tilde. The weaker
rho_tilde<=rho+1 would not suffice. The proof on p68 dominates every
finite positive partial sum by the independently constructed auxiliary
DDE solution in (11.11), and obtains convergence before identifying the
limit. This is a genuine external convergence theorem with satisfied
hypotheses, not an invocation of the desired raw-density equality.

Thus, writing A_+(s)=sum_{j odd}a_j(s) and
A_-(s)=sum_{j even>=2}a_j(s), these sums converge compactly uniformly
on s>1 and s>=2, respectively. The source defines
 F^+=1+A_+, F^-=1-A_-.
The normalization can be checked within this same source, without
identifying Motohashi's presieved K_nu with an unpresieved weight.
Proposition11.8(ii) gives A=2q(2)/D(2)=2/p(1), B=0. To evaluate p(1),
put E_1(x)=integral_x^infinity e^{-u}du/u. The elementary identity
 Ein(x)=gamma+log x+E_1(x)
and (exp(-E_1(x)))'=exp(-E_1(x))e^{-x}/x give
 p(1)=e^{-gamma} integral_0^infinity (exp(-E_1(x)))' dx
     =e^{-gamma};
the endpoint values are 0 and 1. Hence A=2e^gamma and B=0. The source
delay relations and initial conditions are exactly sF=A on 1<s<=3,
f(2)=0, (sf)'=F(s-1) for s>2, and (sF)'=f(s-1) for s>3.
They identify F^+=F and F^-=f by interval-by-interval DDE uniqueness.
In particular
 A_+=F-1,       A_-=1-f.                                  (R4)

(S-d) S pp97--98, Lemma14.7 is a ONE-SIDED estimate for the same raw
weights. It assumes D>=z>=2, g in Omega(kappa,K0), 0<Delta<Delta_0,
d>7/(Delta_0-Delta). At kappa=1, Delta_0=1; choose Delta=1/2 and d=15.
For s in a fixed compact subset of [2,infinity), it supplies
 W_0^+/V_0 <= F(s)+epsilon_T,
 W_0^-/V_0 >= f(s)-epsilon_T,                              (R5)
where epsilon_T<=C_K (log R)^(-1/2)=o_K(1). The source error is
C exp(sqrt(K0)) E(s)(log R)^(-Delta), and E is bounded on that compact
set. The implied constant is independent of T,b,t there.
The fixed g_0, fixed dimension constant and fixed parameters satisfy
every hypothesis. We use no stronger conclusion than these inequalities.

The above two classical analytic inputs -- positive-exit series convergence
and the one-sided raw estimate -- are not re-proved in full here. Their
distinct roles and precise primary hypotheses are exposed. They are not
replaceable by finite-depth convergence, a numerical DDE table, a count
inequality for different weights, or Motohashi's presieved density alone.

3. Exact first exits and target-prime deletion, at every rank

For a fixed finite prime universe p<y, every excluded divisor has a
unique first excluded decreasing prefix P=p1...pj. Summing all later
smaller primes gives the factor V_T(pj). Therefore exactly
 W_T^+/V_T(y)=1+sum_{j odd} E_{j,T},
 W_T^-/V_T(y)=1-sum_{j even>=2} E_{j,T},                   (R6)
where
 E_{j,T}=sum_{P a first exit of rank j} g_T(P)V_T(pj)/V_T(y)>=0.
All sums here are finite at each T. The unit term is the 1 in (R6).
Finite lower densities are not assumed nonnegative.

Take the same geometric exit set for g_0. If (P,T)=1, its normalized
summand satisfies the exact identity
 g_T(P)V_T(pj)/V_T(y)
 =g_0(P)V_0(pj)/V_0(y)
       product_{pj<=p<y, p|T}(1-g_0(p)).                  (R7)
The interval is [pj,y), not (pj,y] or [2,y). In particular the
pj factor is absent on a surviving tuple because pj does not divide T.
If some prefix prime divides T, its g_T summand is zero. Every multiplier
in (R7) lies in [0,1]. Thus, for EVERY rank and EVERY target,
 0<=E_{j,T}<=E_{j,0},
 W_T^+/V_T <= W_0^+/V_0,
 W_T^-/V_T >= W_0^-/V_0.                                 (R8)
These inequalities use normalized exits; analogous unnormalized claims
without the product in (R7) would have the wrong justification.

4. Uniform fixed-rank asymptotics, with an explicit compactness bound

Let b_min>0 be the minimum b on the selected compact parameter set.
Before a rank-j first exit every earlier full square is satisfied:
 A_i p_i<R, where A_i=p1...pi, for i<j. An admitted cube implies its
own square and, by order, the next square. For the lower tree the
initial square is automatic at y<=sqrt R. Consequently
 A_i^2<R A_{i-1},   A_i<R^(1-2^{-i}).
Failure at rank j gives pj^3>=R/A_{j-1}. Hence all prefix primes obey
 pj>=T^{c_j},    c_j=b_min/(3*2^{j-1})>0.                 (R9)
For j=1 this is simply p1^3>=R. Strictness at harmless boundary cases
is not needed for the lower bound in (R9).

The count of distinct p|T with p>=T^{c_j} is at most 1/c_j. Therefore
 sum_{p|T, p>=T^{c_j}} g_0(p)<=2 T^{-c_j}/c_j              (R10)
for all sufficiently large T. The multiplier in (R7) differs from 1
by at most this sum. The total harmonic mass of primes between
T^{c_j} and T^{t_max} is bounded in terms of j and the compact set,
as is V_0(pj)/V_0(y). Bounding all other slots by their total harmonic
mass, the mass of tuples lost because one prefix prime divides T is
O_{j,K}(T^{-c_j}); the same holds for the multiplier error. Thus
 E_{j,T}=E_{j,0}+O_{j,K}(T^{-c_j}).                         (R11)
This explicitly rules out a target whose many small prime divisors
silently change a fixed-depth limit.

For g_0, prime harmonic summation on this compact exponent domain gives
 E_{j,0}(T^b,T^t)=a_j(b/t)+o_{j,K}(1).                    (R12)
Here is a concrete justification of both the integral and uniformity.
Put u_i=log p_i/log y. The limiting integrand is
 1/(u1...u_{j-1}u_j^2)
on 0<u_j<...<u1<1, with previous parity-cube inequalities
sum_{i<k}u_i+3u_k<s and last sum_{i<j}u_i+3u_j>=s.
On the support (R9) the integrand is bounded and continuous. Prime
harmonic measures converge uniformly on fixed exponent intervals;
V_0(pj)/V_0(y)=t/(log pj/log T)+o_K(1) there. Approximate by a fixed
rectangular partition and discard thin strips around the finitely many
affine boundaries. Their volumes are uniformly O_j(strip width), since
their normals are fixed nonzero vectors. This proves uniform convergence
also when b,t and the strict boundary positions vary with T. Coincident
primes have mass O_{j,K}(T^{-c_j}) and hence do not change the ordered
integral. It is not necessary to assume that a discrete boundary is empty.

Alternatively, summing the largest prime first makes the integral obey
(R3): u=log R/log p1 has limits max(s,2+epsilon_j) and j+2; the measure
g_0(p1)V_0(p1)/V_0(y) tends to du/s and the smaller exit parameter is
u-1. At fixed rank this is an integral computation; convergence of the
infinite rank series is still the distinct input (S-c).

5. Two-sided sandwich and uniform rank tails

First take s in a compact subset of [2,infinity). For any fixed J let
 A_{J,+}(s)=sum_{j<=J, j odd}a_j(s),
 A_{J,-}(s)=sum_{j<=J, j even>=2}a_j(s).
Equations(R6),(R11),(R12) give
 W_T^+/V_T >=1+A_{J,+}(s)-o_{J,K}(1),
 W_T^-/V_T <=1-A_{J,-}(s)+o_{J,K}(1).                     (R13)
Equations(R8),(R5) give the OPPOSITE inequalities, uniformly in T.
Writing a_J^tail=A_+-A_{J,+} or A_--A_{J,-}, we obtain explicitly
 F(s)-a_J^tail(s)-o_{J,K}(1) <=W_T^+/V_T<=F(s)+epsilon_T,
 f(s)-epsilon_T<=W_T^-/V_T<=f(s)+a_J^tail(s)+o_{J,K}(1).   (R14)
For a desired error, choose J first using compact-uniform convergence
of (R4), then choose T using the fixed-rank estimates and(R5). This
proves(R1) on these ranges, including s=2. No two one-sided estimates
are subtracted as if they were equalities.

The same reasoning yields a useful UNIFORM bound for actual exit tails.
Use(R5),(R6) for g_0, then subtract its fixed finite exit sum. For either
parity, and with epsilon_{J,K}(T)->0 at fixed J,
 0<=sum_{j>J, chosen parity} E_{j,T}
 <=sum_{j>J} E_{j,0}
 <=a_J^tail(s)+epsilon_T+epsilon_{J,K}(T).                  (R15)
Therefore
 lim_{J->infinity} limsup_{T->infinity, T even}
       sup_{(b,t) in K} sum_{j>J}E_{j,T}=0.                (R16)
Equivalently, the tail bound holds for every even T above a common
threshold after fixing J. No estimate uniform in all J,T simultaneously
or effective rate as J grows with T is asserted.

This supplies the tail domination that finite-rank convergence alone
would lack. The proof does not assume the literal raw-density equality
in order to obtain it: the only full-rank bound used to establish(R15)
is the one-sided Lemma14.7 for the fixed local function g_0.

6. The normalization and strict scope at fixed positive cutoff

The normalized limit in(R1) is independent of the target singular
series because the same V_T appears in both its numerator and
denominator. For applications one may use, separately, the Euler
identity in own-route-uniform-singular-series-20261009.tex, which gives
 V_T(T^t)=2e^{-gamma} C_T/(t log T)(1+o_K(1)).
Together with the normalization in(S-c), a mass T/log T produces the
ordinary coefficient 4 Fbar(s)/t or 4 fbar(s)/t. This explains the
factor four without inserting a second singular-series factor.

The result is uniform on each fixed positive exponent compactum, not
for a family of cutoffs chosen as T varies. For residuals after a
fixed-depth prefix P, its primes are all at least the residual cutoff y,
whereas every residual prime is strictly below y. Thus (P,d)=1 exactly.
If (P,T)=1, g_T(Pd)=g_T(P)g_T(d); if not, the contribution is zero.
There is no additional deletion of prefix primes inside the residual
Euler product. A varying residual R=T^lambda/P is covered whenever
its exponents (lambda-log P/log T, log y/log T) stay in one of the
stated compact sets. This is the actual use after finite compact
selection, and does not require a progression-distribution result.

7. Low upper parameter and full versus rough densities

For 1<s=log R/log y<=3, the upper first cube requires p1<R^(1/3), so
 W_T^+(R,y)=W_T^+(R,R^(1/3))                              (R17)
as an EXACT equality of the same supported coefficients. The Euler
product ratio is
 V_T(R^(1/3))/V_T(y)=3/s+o_K(1).                          (R18)
For g_0 this is Mertens; the correction for p|T in the interval with
endpoints at fixed positive powers is 1+O_K(T^{-c}) by(R10). Applying
the already established equality at s=3 to(R17) yields F(s)=2e^gamma/s
on 1<s<=3. Combining the two ranges proves(R1+) on K+ in full.
The fixed-rank convergence and(R8) still hold here; using the equality
just obtained for g_0, the rank-tail conclusion(R16) also holds on K+.
No lower equality below s=2 is asserted.

Let w=T^omega<y, with fixed omega>0, and let W_{T,rough}^pm(R;y,w)
be the SAME raw tree restricted to divisor primes in [w,y). Set
 E^{<w}_T=sum of normalized full first-exit terms with last prime<w,
using the appropriate parity. Exact cancellation of the common rough
exits gives
 W_T^+ - V_T(w)W_{T,rough}^+ = V_T(y)E^{<w}_T>=0,
 V_T(w)W_{T,rough}^- - W_T^- = V_T(y)E^{<w}_T>=0.         (R19)
These are the upper/lower directions needed by the respective signed
compilers. At a FIXED omega, rough and full densities need not be equal;
one cannot replace (R19) by equality or insert F/f directly for a rough
polynomial without further error accounting.

There is also a rigorous ordered-limit error statement. Given J, choose
omega<min_{j<=J} c_j and below t_min. Then no full exit of rank<=J has
last prime<w, by(R9). Consequently E^{<w}_T is bounded by the tail in
(R15). It follows that
 lim_{omega->0} limsup_{T->infinity} sup_K E^{<w}_T=0.      (R20)
Here omega is fixed before T. This follows from positive exits and
their established uniform tails; it is not a claim of uniformity for
omega(T). It provides qualitative control when such approximation is
needed, while the existing compiler can use the one-sided(R19) directly.

8. Why literal equality permits subtree subtraction

At a prefix of even rank in an upper tree, the exact residual is an
ordinary upper raw weight. If old and new levels are R_old,R_new, the
raw saving is g_T(P)[W_T^+(R_old,y)-W_T^+(R_new,y)]. At an odd prefix
the residual has lower parity and the sign is reversed; the raw saving
is g_T(P)[W_T^-(R_new,y)-W_T^-(R_old,y)]. The lower-tree versions
reverse which full ranks receive these residual parities. Each identity
is a finite coefficient identity obtained by keeping the prefix fixed;
it is not a monotonicity claim for finite signed densities.

Only AFTER (R1) is proved may these exact differences be evaluated as
 V_T(y)[F(s_old)-F(s_new)+o_K(1)], or
 V_T(y)[f(s_new)-f(s_old)+o_K(1)],
with the outside prefix sign and g_T(P) retained. On a fixed positive
prefix compactum the total harmonic prefix mass and V_T(y) relative
to its reference normalization are bounded. Uniform errors can therefore
be summed/integrated. A finite policy supplies finitely many such uses.
The p<y endpoint, the empty continuation and the one ordering factorial
remain the same on the two sides. No new distribution input appears.

9. Findings and limits of the audit

The missing detailed steps can be supplied on the scope(R1): an explicit
fixed-rank exponent c_j, a target-uniform deletion estimate, a compact
fixed-depth integral justification, a two-sided sandwich, and uniform
positive rank tails. I found no remaining literal-density gap for the
actual g_T and compact ranges used after finite-policy selection.

There are three important boundaries. First, the classical convergence
theorem is indispensable and its beta=2 hypothesis has been checked;
finite-depth limits alone do not prove it. Second, an arbitrary
one-sided dimension-one local function need not have equality with F/f:
g identically zero is an immediate counterexample. We use the special
g_T and its fixed-depth closeness to g_0. Third, neither a fixed rough
polynomial nor an uncut noncompact residual family is silently assigned
the full raw equality. Equations(R19),(R20) and the separate compact/tail
arguments describe the required order of use.

The external inputs remaining in this LOCAL derivation are classical
Mertens estimates, Suzuki's positive-exit convergence theorem, and its
one-sided raw estimate, with their exact hypotheses above. No L23,
moving-residue distribution, Li count, H/h feedback, numerical integral,
or current K/c/b claim is used. No statement about their entire proof
chain is revalidated by this report. I authored the earlier local
compiler/primary-interface notes; this is a new direct source-and-proof
audit and not a claim of a second-person replay of those notes.
