High-rank HB/Mellin separation: an adversarial audit and explicit usable lemma
2026-10-10. New proof only; all old files, selected constants and state unchanged.

Conclusion and boundary

The high-rank smooth-weight step can be made explicit without choosing any
coefficient from progression-error signs. The lemma below supplies a uniform
projective integral norm and a pointwise Mellin-tail bound, with a concrete
order for all logarithmic precisions. It matches the medium HB block actually
used by the restricted retained-phase Proposition 5.2. No new obstruction was
found for that fixed smooth class or for the stated positive radial smoothing
used to pass to sharp positive families.

There is a genuine limitation: the same pointwise polylogarithmic separation
argument does NOT cover an arbitrary sharp coupled cutoff. A step across a
product wall is not uniformly approximable by continuous bandlimited functions
with error below 1/2 on the continuous rectangle. Current positive families
instead use fixed-tolerance outward smoothing, followed by T->infinity and
then tolerance->0. This does not assert arbitrary-log signed discrepancy for
every sharp coupled domain. No numerical or global improvement is claimed.

This report does not re-prove the spectral theorem, the full retained-phase
dispersion calculation, low-rank BV, group-factorial normalization, or the
ordinary/finite-table numerical inputs. Their scoped inputs remain explicit.

3. Explicit finite-dimensional separation lemma

Put z_j=log(n_j/U_j). Leave the hard individual intervals I_j in the arithmetic
coefficients. Let w_T(z) be the smooth geometric factor, including a fixed
compact cutoff equal to one on [0,log2]^m. All its supports lie in a fixed
compact rectangle. Define the finite mixed-derivative norm

 D_T = sum_{S subset {1,...,m}} || (prod_{j in S} partial_j^2) w_T ||_L1.

Assume D_T<=C*(log T)^E for fixed C,E. No derivative order beyond two in
each coordinate is needed here. Set G=prod_j(1-partial_j^2)w_T and use the
Fourier convention hat(w)(xi)=int w(z) exp(-i*xi.z) dz. Integration by parts
on the compact support gives

 |hat(w)(xi)| <= D_T * prod_j(1+xi_j^2)^(-1).               (M1)

Fourier inversion therefore gives the absolutely integrable separation

 w_T(z) = int hat(w)(xi) prod_j exp(i*xi_j*z_j) dxi/(2*pi)^m,
 int |hat(w)(xi)| dxi/(2*pi)^m <= D_T/2^m.                 (M2)

For H>=1 let w_H keep only |xi|_infinity<=H. Since
int_R (1+t^2)^(-1)dt=pi and int_|t|>H (1+t^2)^(-1)dt<=2/H,
the union bound on the m coordinates yields

 sup_z |w_T(z)-w_H(z)|
 <= m*D_T/(2^(m-1)*pi*H) <= m*D_T/H.                     (M3)

These are bounds on an integral superposition, not on a finite quadrature
whose discretization error would still need proof. The theorem can be applied
under this integral: at each fixed T all arithmetic sums are finite and the
Fourier measure has finite absolute mass, so Fubini is justified. The bounded
frequency box does NOT require paying H^m instead of the integral norm (M2).
No selection of Fourier coefficients based on arithmetic-error signs occurs.

4. Coefficients actually presented to the repaired theorem

For a retained frequency xi, the selected individual coefficient is precisely

 alpha_n = b(n)*1_{n in I}*1_{Pminus(n)>Zchart}*(n/U)^(i*xi_j),

where b is 1 or mu, with its fixed HB cutoff and possibly a normalized
logarithm. It remains one-bounded. Its multiplicative twist on a dyadic interval
has sup norm 1 and total variation at most |xi_j|*log2. A normalized logarithm
has bounded sup norm and variation. The full auxiliary, subinterval-uniform
rough-SW proof can consequently be partially summed with an additional factor
O(1+H); it requires correspondingly greater logarithmic precision, not a new
arithmetic hypothesis. An arbitrary arithmetic residue filter is not introduced.

The complementary coefficient is

 beta_m = 1_{m in [V,2V]} * sum_{prod_{i!=j}n_i=m} prod_{i!=j}
   [b_i(n_i)*1_{n_i in I_i}*1_{Pminus(n_i)>Zchart}*(n_i/U_i)^(i*xi_i)].

After normalizing the finitely many logarithms, |beta_m| is bounded by a fixed
divisor function uniformly in xi. It is not required to be SW. The existing
divisor-coefficient extension truncates by tau(m), independently of xi and
independently of progression errors. Its deletion uses the ORIGINAL fixed
divisor moment, whose exponent does not grow with the chosen cutoff precision.
All outer logarithmic normalizations are restored as explicit fixed log powers.

The three modulus factors and their support partition are unchanged by xi.
Their one-bounded coefficient class and all four size inequalities therefore
remain valid. The representation may differ between Type II LENGTH blocks,
as allowed by the exact modulus identity; it need not differ with frequencies.
It never depends on n, m, a chosen progression class, or observed error signs.

5. Do not confuse geometric Mellin variation with the retained arithmetic phase

The geometric norm (M2) is polylogarithmic. The reciprocal arithmetic phase
inside dispersion is a different object. For fixed d~D it is
 exp(2*pi*i*T*h/(q*d*r1*r2*n1)), with normalized size
 lambda=(T/x)*(D/d)*log^5 x.
When T/x may be x^kappa, its five-coordinate variation costs
x^(5*kappa+o(1)), not merely a polylogarithm. That power is already paid once
INSIDE the restricted P5.2 proof; it must not be dropped or counted again as
the geometric Fourier norm. The large-residue spectral calculation reserves
7*sigma/2-21*kappa/4-sigma/4>3*sigma for kappa<=sigma/100, and the subsequent
dispersion bridge deliberately uses weaker power savings.

For the outer Fourier modes alpha,beta above, the internal phase proof keeps
their twists as bounded coefficient sequences. It does not differentiate
those sequences along with the retained phase. The n1 cutoff/polarization is
after the dispersion use of SW. In particular (M2) does not authorize a new
arbitrary weight on Lemma5.1's outer q variable. P5.2 is invoked in its final
factorized-coefficient form, where this issue has already been handled.

6. Tail after the modulus sum, with v=T explicitly isolated

Let epsilon_H=m*D_T/H. After collapsing the fixed number of HB variables to
their complete integer v, their absolute coefficient is <=tau(v)^C times a
fixed logarithmic factor. Also |rho_T(l)|<=tau(l)^J and l<=T^theta_star,
theta_star<1 (the actual families have theta_star<3/5). For v!=T,

 sum_{l|v-T}|rho_T(l)| <= tau(|v-T|)^(J+1) up to a fixed divisor-order change.

Apply the independently supplied NONZERO shifted-moment estimate Z2. On one
retained source block x~U*V it gives x*log^C0 x, uniformly for T of the stated
polynomial size. C0 depends on fixed divisor orders and fixed scale parameters,
not on the requested Mellin-tail accuracy. Thus the nonzero counting tail is
O(epsilon_H*x*log^C0 x), before the known logarithmic chart counts.

At v=T, do not use any tau(0) bound and do not sum moduli separately with an
O(x) error. There are at most tau(T)^C contributing HB factorizations and the
modulus-weight sum is <=T^(theta_star+o(1)); hence the exceptional tail is
O(epsilon_H*T^(theta_star+o(1))). This is a first-moment bound with a fixed
power gap. The actual common rough projector>2 makes each HB product odd
when T is even, so this point is in fact absent, but that fact is not needed.

For the exact-coprime mean, drop its coprime indicator in absolute value and
use sum_{l<=T^theta_star}|rho_T(l)|/phi(l)<=log^C1 T. One may bound l/phi(l)
by O(loglog T) and use the Euler product for sum tau(l)^J/l. This gives an
O(epsilon_H*x*log^C2 x) mean tail, with no factor T^theta_star. At v=T the
mean has the same harmless divisor multiplicity, not a tau(0) term.

The logical order is: form the exact HB identity and the fixed Fourier
representation; bound the discarded integral absolutely after the modulus
sum, separating v=T BEFORE invoking Z2; apply restricted P5.2 to the retained
frequencies; recombine. One must NOT delete v=T from alpha or beta beforehand:
that is a coupled n*m=T filter and need not preserve the source coefficient
class. The existing dispersion repair treats that exceptional product in its
own proof. None of the Fourier interchange arguments alters that treatment.

7. A non-circular logarithmic budget

Fix the policy, number of slots, exponent margins, smoothing tolerance, chart
projector, and derivative norm exponent E first. Let J0 bound all known HB,
dyadic, atom-count and scalar-log losses, and C0 bound the tail moments above.
These are fixed independently of the final requested precision A.

Choose B>A+J0+C0+3 and H=(log T)^(B+E+2). Then (M3) is
O(log^(-B-2)T), and all nonzero/mean tails are O(T log^(-A-2)T).
The exact-product tail has a fixed power saving. The retained frequencies
are now a FIXED logarithmic range. Ask for rough-SW with enough additional
saving to pay their O(1+H) variation, and ask for the repaired source
discrepancy with exponent A+J0+E+3. Integrating that error against (M2) and
summing the fixed/logarithmic pieces leaves O(T log^(-A)T).

The divisor order of beta and the shifted-moment exponent were fixed before
B was selected. No maximum tau(n)^C=x^o(1) is multiplied into a merely
logarithmic remainder. The x^(5*kappa) phase loss belongs to the separate
spectral power margin in section 5, independent of A. Since x is within fixed
powers of T, log T and log x are comparable throughout this argument.

8. Which coupled geometries actually meet the lemma

On an HB chart, original group logarithms are constants plus sums of z_j.
For fixed epsilon>0 a smoothed exponent wall is a fixed smooth function of
((sum c_i log p_i/log T)-b)/epsilon. A smoothed radial/product wall is a
fixed smooth function of ((prod p_i/T)-y0)/epsilon. On retained rectangles
the product is O_k(T), including the part on which the original weight was
zero; repeated z derivatives therefore have fixed bounds. Fixed compact
cutoffs and the finite product of these smooth factors satisfy D_T=O_epsilon(1),
or the stated polylog bound when explicitly normalized logarithms occur.
Reciprocal original prime logarithms also have bounded normalized derivatives,
because EACH original group remains >=R_i/64>>T^a_star on a retained rectangle.

The discontinuous individual HB intervals, mu truncations and roughness
indicators stay in alpha/beta. They are never called smooth. They lie in the
explicit interval/rough coefficient hypotheses already supplied to SW/P.
The discontinuous modulus history choices have already been frozen on full
licensed atoms before this argument; no modulus-dependent hard cutoff is
hidden inside w_T.

For a raw coupled indicator 1_{z1+z2<=c}, any continuous approximation with
the claimed uniform error would have to approach both 0 and 1 at the wall.
Its uniform error on the continuous rectangle is at least 1/2. Thus smooth
Fourier truncation alone cannot prove the arbitrary-sharp extension. This
does not rule out separate Perron/boundary arguments in other problems.
