Fixed-d retained-phase E2: application of the ABL spectral input
10 October 2026. New local applicability audit; all older files unchanged.

0. Finding and independence boundary

The actual application is in the coprime, trivial-character, singleton-s
subclass of the external spectral results. I find no missing factor of
the dispersion parameter d in that subclass. This conclusion is based
on the parameter and norm calculations below, not on an earlier PASS.
It does not prove the classical spectral estimates from first principles,
or certify the whole moving-target distribution proposition.

I wrote the older packet-adversarial and S1-return notes. Thus this is a
new adversarial applicability check by a prior participant, not a claim
of independent authorship of the complete E2 proof. In particular I
re-read ABL v1 Lemmas 3.1/3.2, (3.1)--(3.8), and the precise DI statements
in Pascadi v3. The useful additional clarification is that the continuous
part can be supplied by ABL's cumulative quadratic-form statement itself;
one need not postulate individual L2 vectors of Eisenstein series.

The statements below retain the hypotheses (a,z)=1, a common m interval
across levels, and polynomially bounded scales. Removing any of those
hypotheses is outside this audit. Two literal endpoint distinctions are
also necessary: s=1 means the integer singleton, and a nonempty Y/nu
block can have length parameter between 1/2 and 1.

1. Dictionary and the exact disappearance of fixed d

Write d0 for the smooth dispersion factor, to distinguish it from an
oldform dilation. In the actual E2 array, d0~D, (a,d0)=1, with
D<=x^(2/log log x); d0 is not bounded independently of x. The residue a
can be T, with c*x<=T<=x^(1+kappa). All remaining scales are bounded by
a fixed power of x. These assumptions are those of the actual fixed-d
and common-parameter interfaces, not new enlarged ranges.

After the retained reciprocity phase has been separated and charged,
the bare L5.1 phase is

 e(a*h*(n1-n2)*inverse(n2*q*d0*r1*s1)/(n1*r2*s2)).       (1)

The retained gcd indicators make q*d0 invertible modulo n1*r2*s2.
The congruence n1-n2=f*q*d0 therefore changes (1) EXACTLY to

 e(a*h*f*inverse(n2*r1*s1)/(n1*r2*s2)).                  (2)

There is no Taylor deletion and no small-a estimate in this step.
The true f range is |f|<=2N/(d0*q0). Its enlargement to 2N/q0 is made
only after the positive Cauchy majorant has been formed. Hence it cannot
lose cancellation needed for that majorant. The coefficients and their
positive multiplicity bounds may still depend on d0; they are not
claimed identical for different d0.

Set b=n2*r1, c=n1*r2*s2. After the inner square, the new spectral indices
are z=s1*s2 and y=f*(h1*s1-h2*s2), with y!=0 treated here. The original
(a,s1*s2)=1 restriction is retained, so (a,z)=1. The geometric sum has
(c,b*z)=1. Product endpoints are removed before this square, and the
bounded weight omega_h is independent of b,c. Unit phases chosen after
grouping (z,y) change only beta(y,z). They do not enlarge its positive
coefficient envelope, or introduce b,c dependence.

In the notation of ABL (3.1), the actual map is

 q_ABL=1, r_ABL=z, s_ABL=1, Q_ABL=z, q_chi=1,
 Q_bad=(Q_ABL,(r_ABL*s_ABL)^infinity)=z, Q_good=1.       (3)

The dispersion d0 is neither q_ABL, q_chi nor an oldform dilation.
The restriction (c,z)=1 is the geometric coprimality used by the cusp
formula; (a,c)=1 is not required. The later outer sum retains 1/d0.
Nothing here removes the separately accounted D loss when comparing
the fixed-d estimate with the target normalization containing 1/D.

2. Completion and the coefficient class presented to the spectrum

Poisson summation in b gives, up to the fixed smooth normalization,

 (B/c) sum_m psihat(m*B/c) S(m*inverse(z mod c), a*y; c). (4)

The bar on z is essential. The cusp identity has the two cusps infinity
and 1 at level z, with cusp modulus c*sqrt(z), and a unit phase
e(-a*y/z). In the DI Fourier normalization, rho_DI(n)=sqrt(|n|)rho_ABL(n),
this is exactly the normalization needed in ABL (3.2), not a new factor
sqrt(a) or sqrt(nu).

The zero m term is treated separately by the Ramanujan-sum estimate.
It is not fed to an exceptional-spectrum large sieve. Its established
bound uses sum_(c~C) gcd(a*y,c)<<C*tau(|ay|), with ay!=0.

For nonzero m use ONE cutoff |m|<=(C/B)*x^delta and common signed dyadic
m intervals. If this cutoff is <1, the entire nonzero contribution is
a Schwartz tail. Otherwise an integer block can have parameter >=1/2
even if C/B<1; comparisons with the real number C/B are comparisons of
positive polynomials, not applications of a theorem to an empty block.

On a fixed block m~M, |y|~Y, z~Z, set

 Y0=4*pi*sqrt(|a|*M*Y)/(C*sqrt(Z)),
 v=m/M, w=|y|/Y, zeta=z/Z,
 t=(4*pi*sqrt(|a|*m*|y|)/(c*sqrt(z)))/Y0.

Then c/C=sqrt(v*w/zeta)/t. Thus normalized smooth derivatives in the
four-variable separation do not acquire a new power of a. Fourier
expansion gives a common m sequence 1_I(m)e(m*omega), and arbitrary
level-dependent n coefficients beta(n,z)e(k_n*n/(P*Y)+k_z*z/(P*Z)).
The latter retain their l2 norm. The interval I and omega do not depend
on z. Decaying Fourier coefficients pay the finitely many smooth
seminorms. The retained five-variable reciprocity variation has already
been charged upstream and must not be charged again here.

This checks the exact distinction needed by the two spectral inputs:
the n side permits arbitrary coefficients for each level; the stronger
level-averaged m estimate permits a common additive phase/interval,
not arbitrary level-dependent m coefficients or endpoints.

3. Coprime oldform reduction, including its norm

Let f be a newform packet at level z, and let v|z/q_f be its oldform
dilation. In ABL Lemma 3.1 put s=1 and n=a*nu, m=n0, where
nu|a^infinity and (n0,a)=1. Its integer

 K=gcd(z/gcd(v*q_f,z),(a*nu)^infinity)

equals 1 because (a,z)=1. This is uniform in the dilation and the
newform level. In DI normalization, the lemma consequently states

 rho_(f_v,1)(a*nu*n0)
 =lambda_v(a*nu)e(n0*(a*nu-1)/z)rho_(f_v,1)(n0).        (5)

The negative-argument formula has the corresponding minus signs.
The Hecke multiplier can depend on v. There is no valid need to replace
it by a common packet scalar. With Theta=7/32,
|lambda_v(a*nu)|<<(a*nu)^(Theta/2+epsilon), uniformly in the packet.

Combine (5) with the cusp phase e(-a*nu*n0/z). The resulting coefficient
is, in the positive-sign case,

 beta_nu(n0,z)=1_((n0,a)=1)*beta(nu*n0,z)*e(-n0/z).      (6)

Thus its phase no longer depends on a, nu or the oldform dilation, and
||beta_nu||_2^2<=sum_(z,n0)|beta(nu*n0,z)|^2. Sign changes do not change
this inequality. The Fourier modes from section 2 are included in beta.

For clarity, the packet norm step is as follows. ABL (3.3) writes an
orthonormal packet basis e_g=sum_(v|g)xi_g(v)f_v, with ||f_v||=1 and
sum_(g,v)|xi_g(v)|^2<<z^epsilon after divisor losses. For a functional
L_nu on the packet, |L_nu(f_v)|<=||L_nu||. Applying this to (5), then
the triangle inequality in nu, gives

 ||L_a|| << (aYz)^epsilon
             sum_(nu|a^infinity,nu<=2Y)(a*nu)^(Theta/2)||L_nu||. (7)

Pair this functional with the unchanged m-side functional in the
ORIGINAL orthonormal packet. Cauchy--Schwarz costs its original norm.
Every element of a packet has the same spectral parameter, so any
positive exceptional or regular spectral weight commutes with this
step. There is no extra factor equal to the level z. The nu divisor
count can alternatively convert (7) into the quadratic-form sum in
ABL Lemma 3.2, at a selectable x^epsilon cost.

The support condition (a,z)=1 is substantive. For instance, in the
integer formula with z=4, a=2, q_f=1, dilations v=1 and v=4 produce
K=4 and K=1. This is a negative control for an attempted K=1 argument
without coprimality, not a counterexample to ABL's general theorem.
The actual coefficient support excludes precisely this situation.

4. Continuous and regular spectra: the printed quadratic form suffices

An Eisenstein series is not an L2 cusp-form vector. No such vector is
used here. ABL (3.2) defines its cumulative, cusp-summed continuous
quadratic form E, including the Plancherel normalization. Lemma 3.2
explicitly extends the reduction to E, with the powers a^Theta and
nu^Theta removed. Its proof on PDF page 11 specifies the character-pair
basis and cites Young sections 8.5 and 9.1 for that normalization.

Under (3), the sum over K|Q_good in that lemma is a singleton. Therefore
for a spectral height U>=1, the actual continuous norm is bounded by

 E_a(U,z,Y,beta) << (aYz)^epsilon
                 sum_(nu|a^infinity,nu<=2Y) E_1(U,z,Y/nu,beta_nu). (8)

This is a direct application of the published cumulative quadratic form,
not an extra unproved per-Eisenstein-vector assertion. ABL (3.8), in the
SAME normalization, supplies the regular large-sieve bound

 E_1(U,z,L,b) << (zL)^epsilon (U^2+L/z)||b||_2^2.       (9)

Equivalently it is the continuous instance of DI regular large sieve;
Pascadi Lemma H writes the normalized form with mu(cusp1)=1/z. Using
the weaker common factor (a*nu)^Theta>=1 in (8) is permitted if one
wishes to keep the same polynomial as the Maass contribution. The
holomorphic analogue is also part of ABL Lemma 3.2; regular Maass uses
the displayed Hecke factors. Heights |t|<=1 use U=1, and higher shells
use these cumulative norms and the explicitly decreasing Bessel majorant.
This audit does not reproduce the previously separate shell summation.

ABL prints Y>=1. A nonempty transformed interval L=Y/nu can instead
lie in [1/2,1), in which case its only possible integer is 1. The proof
of (5)--(8) is a finite sum identity and remains unchanged: nu<=2Y,
orthonormal completion and the divisor count still apply. Alternatively
one can read (8) with its literal finite support rather than a new
application with a smaller input length. For (9), the DI statement in
Pascadi Lemma H explicitly permits length >=1/2. A singleton n=1 must
not be dropped, or placed without explanation in the block (1,2].

5. Exceptional inputs and endpoint completion checked against primary

Pascadi v3 Theorem A, (1.4)--(1.5), permits arbitrary coefficients and
length L>=1/2 at a cusp with mu=1/z. Its admissible weight includes
max(1,z/L,z^2/L^3). Our weight 2+Z/L is at most a fixed multiple of
max(1,z/L); the unused third term is unnecessary. Hence

 sum_exc (2+Z/L)^tau_j |sum b_n rho_(j,1)(n)|^2
 << (zL)^epsilon (1+L/z)||b||_2^2,  tau_j<=Theta.       (10)

For the unchanged m-side vector, Pascadi Theorem J, (3.34)--(3.35),
states the level average with a common e(m*omega), uniformly in omega,
up to weight X<<max(M,Z^2/M), and right side (ZM)^epsilon(Z+M)M.
For a common integer interval I in (M,2M], expand 1_I in a finite DFT
of period P>2M. Its coefficient l1 norm is O(log(2M)). Hilbert-space
Minkowski gives the square of that logarithm, not a maximum of endpoint
sums chosen separately at every level. A common pre-existing phase just
shifts every DFT frequency. Subsets of levels (a,z)=1 may be enlarged
to all levels after the positive squared norm has been formed.

Weights above that threshold are bounded using tau_j<=Theta. The
bounded positive exceptional cosh factor may be retained at constant
cost. These are precisely the two a-free inputs needed after (7).
Neither an arbitrary-coefficient version of Theorem J nor L23's more
general printed Theorems 9.1/9.5 is required.

6. Uniformity, limits, and what remains an external input

All norms above are uniform over the actual coefficient values, endpoint
choices and d0, subject to the same smooth seminorm bounds. The required
coprimality is kept before polarizations, not obtained by deleting an
unknown error afterward. No sum over possible a or possible d0 is hidden
inside a spectral constant. For any prescribed epsilon>0 and fixed
polynomial scale bounds, a-smooth nu<=2Y has at most x^epsilon elements:
Rankin bounds its count by (2Y)^delta product_(p|a)(1-p^(-delta))^-1;
the product is x^o(1) for fixed delta>0. Choose delta first. Thus the
divisor, packet and completion losses are selectable subpower losses.

The separate retained-phase loss x^(5*kappa+o(1)) and the outer D factor
are NOT included in this selectable epsilon assertion. They remain in
the existing explicit parameter budget. This note neither removes nor
recomputes them. In particular it does not assert uniformity for d0
outside the original subpower range.

Accepted external inputs: the normalized Kuznetsov/cusp identities;
ABL Lemmas 3.1/3.2 and their stated continuous normalization (with their
standard oldform/Young ingredients); DI regular large sieve; the precise
DI exceptional statements A/J as reproduced in Pascadi; and the bound
Theta=7/32. The argument above checks their actual hypothesis overlap.
It does not independently prove those classical theorems, estimate E1,
reconstruct all multiplicities, or certify the final density and sieve
constants. The retained-phase separation and positive Cauchy interfaces
are read inputs, with their restrictions explicitly preserved here.
