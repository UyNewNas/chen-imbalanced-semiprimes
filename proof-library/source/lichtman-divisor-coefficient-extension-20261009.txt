Divisor-bounded coefficients after the restricted convolution repair
2026-10-09. A conditional extension lemma; no global prime-count claim.

Assume the restricted one-bounded Proposition5.2 estimate now proposed:
theta=7/32, fixed c0*x<=|a|<=x^(1+kappa), kappa<=sigma/100, and B1--B4.
The alpha sequence has the source's rough support and full auxiliary
Siegel--Walfisz property. The claim below concerns the alpha_n,beta_m
coefficient bound only; it changes no modulus factorization or scale.

Let |alpha_n|<=tau(n)^B, |beta_m|<=tau(m)^B for fixed B. The modulus
coefficient after grouping its three factors has |rho_l|<=tau_3(l),
or any other fixed divisor-power bound used only for controlling the
deleted tails. The original three-factor modulus coefficient structure
and the one-bounded conditions on its factors remain required when
applying Proposition5.2; an arbitrary divisor-bounded rho is not thereby
admitted to that theorem. The total modulus support is l<=Dtot,
where Dtot<=x^(1-delta) for fixed delta>0. The latter follows from
B7 in the actual application. All moduli remain coprime to a.

1. Truncate by the divisor function, not by an arbitrary coefficient value

For a fixed large C put
 alpha'_n=alpha_n*1_{tau(n)<=log^C x},
 beta'_m=beta_m*1_{tau(m)<=log^C x}.
The rough alpha' retains auxiliary SW by L23 Lemma4.3 / Maynard I
Lemma13.7, taken as an explicitly named input. The cutoff based solely
on |alpha_n| would not follow from that lemma and is not used here.
Divide each truncated sequence by log^(BC) x to make it one-bounded.
After applying the restricted theorem with an adequately enlarged
logarithmic exponent, multiply back by log^(2BC) x. This operation
only costs a fixed logarithmic power.

2. Count the removed congruence contribution absolutely

For b=mn, use tau(n),tau(m)<=tau(b) and at most tau(b) factorizations.
If either divisor cutoff fails, Markov's elementary inequality gives
  sum_{mn=b} |alpha_n beta_m|*1_{a cutoff fails}
       <= 2*log^(-C) x * tau(b)^(2B+2).                  (C1)
For b!=a, the sum of absolute modulus weights with l|(b-a) is at
most a fixed power tau(|b-a|)^J. Hence the removed counting term is
  << log^(-C)x * sum_{b~x,b!=a}
          tau(b)^(2B+2)*tau(|b-a|)^J
  << x*log^(C_B-C)x,                                    (C2)
uniformly for polynomial-sized a, by the nonzero shifted-moment
bound Z2 of lichtman-zero-product-dispersion-repair-20261009.txt.
Here C_B is fixed before the truncation parameter C is chosen.

If b=a>0, do not write tau(0). Its full, untruncated contribution
is at most
  tau(a)^O_B(1) * sum_{l<=Dtot}|rho_l|
       <= Dtot*x^o(1) <= x^(1-delta+o(1)),               (C3)
which is negligible for every fixed logarithmic target. This is
an estimate of the original first moment, not of a variance.

3. Remove the corresponding coprime mean

The unrestricted mass in (C1), summed over b~x, is
x*log^(C'_B-C)x by the usual divisor moments. Also
  sum_{l<=Dtot}|rho_l|/phi(l) << log^(C''_B)x.
For example split off l/phi(l)<<log log(3l) and use the Euler-product
bound for sum tau(l)^J/l. Dropping the coprime indicator only increases
the absolute bound. Therefore the removed mean also has arbitrary
logarithmic saving after choosing C large enough.

4. Precise conclusion and remaining scope

The one-bounded restricted estimate extends to fixed divisor-bounded
alpha,beta with the same roughness and auxiliary SW hypotheses, using
the source's verified-for-this-purpose divisor-function SW truncation
lemma and the independently reviewed moving shifted-moment estimate.
No x^o(1) is multiplied into a merely logarithmic theorem remainder;
subpower factors occur only in the exact-product term (C3), where a
fixed power margin is available.

For applications with additional intervals or smooth factors, assume
the requisite interval/twist-uniform SW statements separately or prove
them for the concrete prime factors. This note does not infer arbitrary
interval stability from a single dyadic SW statement. It also does not
prove that a particular finite modulus compiler supplies B1--B4, or
replace the one/two-long-variable and low-rank alternatives. Those are
the subsequent project-specific distribution checks.
