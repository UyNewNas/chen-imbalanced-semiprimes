% Sharper one-dimensional upper costs, same original functional.
\subsection{Switched one-dimensional costs without delay interpolation}
The three ordinary upper costs are $K$ times integrals of
\[
 \frac{\log((1-2t)/t)+A(1/t-1)-1}{t(1-t)},\quad
 \frac{\log(2-3t)}{t(1-t)},\quad
 \frac{\log((1-t-d)/d)}{t(1-t)},
\]
on $[d,1/3]$, $[a,1/3]$ and $[h,d]$, respectively.
Here $A(s)=s\bar F(s)$ and $A=1$ up to three.
For $d\le t\le1/4$, the only extra $A$ contribution equals
\[
 A(1/t-1)-1=\int_2^{1/t-2}\frac{\log(u-1)}u\,du.
\]
All arguments in this identity stay in the initial ordinary branches
because $2<1/d-2<3$. The integrands are nonnegative, so Fubini gives
the exact expression
\[
 \int_d^{1/4}\frac{A(1/t-1)-1}{t(1-t)}dt
 =\int_2^{1/d-2}\frac{\log(u-1)}u
     \log\frac{1-d}{d(u+1)}\,du.
\]
This removes delay-table interpolation from the numerical task.

All four resulting one-dimensional integrands are smooth on their
closed intervals. For each rational cell of width $\delta$, evaluate
the midpoint and enclose the second derivative throughout the cell.
The signed midpoint remainder is contained in
$\delta^3[\inf g'',\sup g'']/24$. Explicit differentiation of the
logarithms and $1/[t(1-t)]=1/t+1/(1-t)$ supplies those bounds;
the implementation does not differentiate a numerical interpolant.
The Fubini integrand uses the ordinary product second-derivative rule.
Every logarithm, division and sum is rounded outward.

Replacing the three old upper costs by these new upper bounds changes
the complete coefficient by at least
\[
 \frac{2(U_7^{\rm old}-U_7^{\rm new})+
 (U_8^{\rm old}-U_8^{\rm new})+(U_9^{\rm old}-U_9^{\rm new})}{4},
\]
where each old and new symbol is its certified upper estimate.
This is replacement within the explicit complete functional, with
all other terms fixed. It does not add two estimates of the same
integral. The operation applies to both finite defect tables because
these three costs depend only on the unchanged $a,h,d,K$.
