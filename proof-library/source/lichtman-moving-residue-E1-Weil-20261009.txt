An independent E1 input in the project's large-moving-residue range
2026-10-09. Mathematical proof supplement, not a global K/c/b restoration.

The reference actually checked is Maynard I v2, arXiv:2006.06572v2,
Lemma17.2 (Second exponential sum estimate), local source lines6445--6652.
L23 Lemma4.7 and Maynard II Lemma6.10 cite an older Lemma17.3 numbering;
we do not infer their entire stated range from that citation. Instead
use the actual, somewhat stronger size conditions of Lemma17.2 and show
that they suffice for the present application.

1. Uniformity of the directly used bound

Let NM~x. For q~L,r_i~R the sum has denominator phi(q*r2)*q*r1,
the phases e(a*h*inverse(n1)/(q*r1)), and Fourier cutoff
  H1 ~ L*R/M * log^5 x.
For arbitrary one-bounded lambda_{q,r}, alpha_n, the source proves
  |Z| << N^2/L * x^(-epsilon_W)                           (W1)
provided
  N*L < x^(1-4*epsilon_W),
  N*L^(5/2)*R^3 < x^(2-4*epsilon_W),
  N^2*L*R < x^(2-4*epsilon_W).                            (W2)
Fixed multiples of H1, fixed smooth dyadic q weights, and fixed
dyadic support enlargements are permitted by its displayed proof
with strict power margins. Coefficients bounded by x^o(1) can be
normalized at an x^o(1) cost here, because (W1) is a power bound.

The dependence on a is checked in the actual proof, not read from a
fixed-residue implicit constant. Cauchy is in n1,n2,q,r2, followed by
a smooth n1 majorant. After expanding, the inner completed n sum has
modulus q*r1*r1' and frequency a*ell, where ell=h*r1'-h'*r1.
Retain (a,q*r1*r1')=1 up to this point, as supplied by the original
coefficient support. Thus
  (a*ell,q*r1*r1') = (ell,q*r1*r1').                       (W3)
The Weil/completion bound is
  x^o(1) [L^(1/2)*R + N/(L*R^2)*(ell,q*r1*r1')].          (W4)
This holds uniformly in all a coprime to this modulus; no reciprocal
phase is deleted and no derivative proportional to a occurs.
The ell=0 count and the subsequent positive gcd sums are independent
of a. Coprimality with a may be dropped only after applying (W3).
This establishes the uniformity of the cited proof in the required
residue aspect. The Weil bound itself is an external theorem.

One printed condition in the source's first Cauchy display also needs
care: it inserts (r1,r2)=1, although the original E1 statement does not
impose it. Start that Cauchy step directly from the original sum and
retain only (r1,a*n1)=1 in its inner r1 sum. The bounded coefficient
may depend on r2. After expanding and bounding the coefficients, the
same completed n sum (W4) results; none of the later ell/gcd counts
uses (r1,r2)=1. The unused restriction is therefore not inserted.

2. Size conditions implied by the actual large-residue application

Use the conditional Proposition5.2 bridge B1--B4, with margin sigma,
and set theta=7/32. In this supplement assume additionally |a|>=c*x
for a fixed c>0. This is true in the project when a=T and the full
prime/product being distributed has size at most a fixed multiple of T.
It is not asserted for the entire range 1<=|a|<=x^(1+kappa).

Put n=log_x N, q=log_x Q1, u=log_x Q2, v=log_x Q3. Up to O(1/log x)
from fixed constants, B1--B3 give
  q-n < -sigma,
  2*n+u+2*v < 1-8*sigma,
  n+q+5*u+2*v < 2-theta-15*sigma.                          (W5)
The exact nonnegative linear combination
  (35/16)*(q-n)+(23/16)*(2*n+u+2*v)
       +(5/16)*(n+q+5*u+2*v) - v/2
    = n+(5/2)*q+3*u+3*v                                  (W6)
therefore gives
  n+(5/2)*q+3*u+3*v
    < 1021/512 - (147/8)*sigma + O(1/log x).              (W7)
Here 1021/512=2-3/512. The positive gap 3/512 is specific to using
theta=7/32 and the actual lower bound |a|>=c*x.

The other two Weil conditions follow from
  n+q < 1-9*sigma,
  2*n+q+u+v < 17/10-(29/2)*sigma,                        (W8)
where the second uses B7: q+u+v<7/10-(13/2)*sigma and B2:2n<1-8sigma.
These inequalities have enough slack for epsilon_W=sigma, fixed
dyadic constants, and the subpower D factors below. This deliberately
uses the actual theta=7/32; it makes no claim for theta=0 or an entire
general theta-family. Conditions B4 and the later spectral input are
not needed for this particular E1 verification.

3. Fixed d and summation over d

Use the dyadic small-factor range d~D=x^o(1), total rough length R,
with D*R~Q2*Q3. For fixed d put q'=d*q and L=d*Q1.
The E1 denominator and congruences become exactly those of (W1).
Unlike Lemma5.1, the directly used Lemma17.2 ALLOWS arbitrary
q'-dependent coefficients. Set
  lambda_{q',r}=1_{d|q'} Lambda_{d,r}
and absorb the nonnegative smooth q weight by its square root.
Hence restriction to multiples of d is legitimate here. It is not
inferred by restricting a cancellation bound for an unweighted sum.
The actual Fourier cutoff differs from L*R/M*log^5 x only by D/d=O(1).

For its three size products one has
  N*L = N*Q1*x^o(1),
  N*L^(5/2)*R^3
    << N*Q1^(5/2)*(Q2*Q3)^3 * D^(-1/2),
  N^2*L*R << N^2*Q1*Q2*Q3.                               (W9)
Thus (W7)--(W8) imply (W2) with epsilon_W=sigma for sufficiently
large x. The divisor bound on Lambda, and all normalization costs,
are x^o(1). For each d the conclusion is
  |E1(d)| << N^2/(d*Q1) * x^(-sigma+o(1)).               (W10)
Summing over d~D uses sum 1/d=O(1), and then paying D=x^o(1) gives
  |E1| << N^2/(D*Q1) * x^(-sigma/2)                      (W11)
for sufficiently large x. The weakened exponent includes all fixed
coefficient normalizations and dyadic decompositions.

4. What this supplies, and what it does not

Equation (W11) is a directly sourced and parameter-checked replacement
for the E1 input in the project's near-linear large-residue range.
Combined with an E2 bound N^2/(DQ1)*x^(-sigma/2), the preliminary
dispersion square root gives x^(1-sigma/4) before logarithmic factors,
or the convenient weaker x^(1-sigma/5) after them. This matches the
power saving needed in the general conditional bridge.

This is not a repair of Lemma4.7 for every a or of the whole source
Theorem1.7. The preliminary zero-product/shifted-moment repair and
the spectral E2 input have their own explicit proof obligations.
No original numerical K,c,b record is promoted by this note.
