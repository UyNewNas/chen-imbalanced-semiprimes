Result and scope

The needed Barban--Davenport--Halberstam type estimate follows directly
from character orthogonality, the classical primitive-character large
sieve, and the supplied full auxiliary SW estimates. It need not be
imported through BFI Theorem0(a) or taken on faith from Maynard I
Lemma13.8. The derivation below also handles an additional, arbitrarily
large fixed coprimality filter d0, with an explicit tau(d0)^(2C0) cost.
The constants and modulus threshold are uniform in d0. That cost must
subsequently be paid; it is not a free filter-stability assertion.

For the actual normalized medium rough mu/1 factor C0=1 and its squared
norm is at most a fixed multiple of N. Thus the local mean-square input
has all the strength required at Qmean<=8 QD with QD<=N*x^(-delta),
provided N is a fixed positive power of x. The target T affects only a
uniform coefficient family and optional outer restrictions. It does not
become a large residue in this mean-square theorem, which sums over ALL
primitive residue classes.

This is not a reproof of preliminary dispersion's main-term/Gram
reduction, its zero-product corrections, the spectral estimate or all PP.
Their use of the local lemma must retain the exact mean and the stated
filter factors. The root/GB main-term repair is a separate component.
This reviewer authored the previous rough-SW and dispersion-bottom audit;
the present work supplies the previously external mean-square step, not
a claimed independent second review of those earlier proofs.

1. A precise local lemma

Put L=1+log(2N). The complex sequence a_n is supported in a fixed real
interval of length at most N, contained in [N,2N]. Allow a subinterval
by extending its coefficient by zero. Assume, with constants uniform in
the coefficient family,

  sum_n |a_n|^2 <= C_norm N L^D,                            (H1)

for fixed D>=0, and, for each B>0, every integer k>=1, q>=1 and
primitive b modulo q,

 |sum_{n=b mod q,(n,k)=1}a_n
   -phi(q)^(-1)sum_{(n,kq)=1}a_n|
       <= C_B N tau(k)^C0 L^(-B).                         (H2)

Here C0>=0 is a FIXED exponent, independent of B. Only logarithmically
bounded q are needed in the proof, but the actual input supplies all q.
Constants C_B may depend on B and the fixed family parameters, including
its allowed Mellin frequency exponent. They may not depend on the
particular target, interval endpoints, k or q within that family.

For any d0>=1 define a_n[d0]=a_n*1_{(n,d0)=1} and

 D_d0(q,b)=sum_{n=b mod q}a_n[d0]
          -phi(q)^(-1)sum_{(n,q)=1}a_n[d0].

Then for every fixed A>0 and w>=0 there is a fixed C such that

  sum_{q<=Q} tau(q)^w sum_{b mod q,(b,q)=1}|D_d0(q,b)|^2
       << tau(d0)^(2C0) N^2 L^(-A),                       (BDH)

whenever Q<=N L^(-C). Both C and the implied constant are independent
of d0, and the statement is uniform in the whole coefficient family.
A sufficient, deliberately generous choice is

  d_w=2^ceil(2w),     C=2A+2D+d_w+4.

Increasing C to an integer is harmless. No factor growing as N^o(1)
is multiplied into the logarithmic remainder.

2. Remove the auxiliary-filter issue by an exact normalization

Set a_tilde_n=a_n[d0]/tau(d0)^C0. Since
tau(k*d0)<=tau(k)tau(d0), applying (H2) with auxiliary integer k*d0
shows that a_tilde satisfies the SAME (H2) constants C_B and exponent
C0. Its squared norm is no larger than (H1). This uses the exact
equivalence of the two coprimality conditions to (n,k*d0)=1; no
coprimality between k and d0 is assumed. Thus it suffices to prove
(BDH) for d0=1, with uniform constants, and multiply back by
tau(d0)^(2C0). This proves uniformity even when d0 depends on T or N.

3. Exact characters, including induced characters

For d0=1 put A_b=sum_{n=b mod q}a_n over primitive b. Its average is
exactly phi(q)^(-1)sum_{(n,q)=1}a_n. Orthogonality gives

  V(q):=sum_b^* |D_1(q,b)|^2
       =1/phi(q) sum_{chi mod q,chi!=chi0}|sum_n a_n chi(n)|^2.
                                                               (P1)

Every nonprincipal chi has a unique primitive inducing character
chi_star of conductor f>1, with q=f*k. Even when (f,k)>1,

  chi(n)=chi_star(n)*1_{(n,k)=1}.                           (P2)

For q=1 the variance is exactly zero. There is no missing coprimality
in (P2): chi_star already vanishes
when (n,f)>1, and the extra restriction supplies exactly the additional
prime divisors of q. The conductor1 case is precisely the principal
character already removed in (P1). Also

  phi(f*k)>=phi(f)phi(k),

as is checked prime by prime, including shared prime powers. These
facts allow an upper bound obtained by summing f,k independently with
f*k<=Q. They do not replace the coefficient by a k-free sequence.

4. Large conductors from the classical large sieve

The only large-sieve input is the primitive-character inequality

 sum_{f<=F} (f/phi(f)) sum_{chi_star mod f}^*
     |sum_{n in J} c_n chi_star(n)|^2
       <=(F^2+length(J)+O(1)) sum_{n in J}|c_n|^2.          (LS)

This is the Bombieri--Davenport form recorded and proved as Theorem18.2,
equation(18.2.1), in Kedlaya's author course notes. Its adjacent text
specifies primitive characters. The classical large sieve remains an
external standard theorem; (BDH) is derived from it here.

Choose R0 between L^b0 and 2L^b0 at a dyadic value. For fixed k and
a dyadic conductor block R<f<=2R, R>=R0, apply (LS) to the SINGLE
sequence c_n=a_n*1_{(n,k)=1}. It is independent of f throughout that
application and has squared norm at most C_norm N L^D. Equations
(P1)--(P2), phi(fk)>=phi(f)phi(k), and 1/f<=1/R give

 contribution(k,R)
   << (N L^D/phi(k)) * (N/R+R).                            (P3)

The restriction f<=Q/k can be discarded inside its last dyadic block.
The geometric sums satisfy sum_R 1/R<=2/R0 and sum_R R<=2Q/k. Summing
over k<=Q/R0 yields

 V_large << N L^D [ (N/R0) sum_{k<=Q}1/phi(k)
                         +Q sum_{k>=1}1/(k phi(k)) ]
         << N^2 L^(D+2)/R0 + NQ L^D.                      (P4)

For completeness, k/phi(k)<=tau(k) gives
sum_{k<=Q}1/phi(k)<=sum tau(k)/k<=(1+log Q)^2, while
sum_{k>=1}1/(k phi(k))<=sum tau(k)/k^2=zeta(2)^2<infinity.
These are fixed logarithmic or absolute factors. Applying the large
sieve to all imprimitive characters as though they were primitive
would not justify (P4); the fixed-k restriction is essential.

5. Small conductors from FULL auxiliary SW

Fix 1<f<=R0 and a primitive nonprincipal chi_star. Partition its
twisted sum with auxiliary k into primitive classes modulo f.
Their common mean cancels because sum_b^* chi_star(b)=0. By (H2),

 |sum_n a_n*1_{(n,k)=1} chi_star(n)|
       <= phi(f) C_B N tau(k)^C0 L^(-B).                  (P5)

There are at most phi(f) primitive characters. Dividing by phi(fk)
and using the preceding totient inequality gives

 V_small << N^2 L^(-2B)
       sum_{f<=R0}phi(f)^2 sum_{k<=Q/f}tau(k)^(2C0)/phi(k).

Let j_aux=ceil(2C0+1), d_aux=2^j_aux. The elementary inequalities
tau(k)^j_aux<=d_{2^j_aux}(k) and k/phi(k)<=tau(k) imply

 sum_{k<=Y}tau(k)^(2C0)/phi(k)
       <=(1+log Y)^d_aux.

Since sum_{f<=R0}phi(f)^2<<R0^3, the usable bound is

  V_small << N^2 L^(3b0+d_aux-2B).                        (P6)

The power R0^3 is explicitly retained. The k exponent is fixed before
the requested SW exponent B is selected. An auxiliary exponent that
grew uncontrolled with B would not be covered by this parameter choice.
For the actual rough mu/1 sequence C0=1, so d_aux=8 works.

To obtain sum_{q<=Q}V(q)<<N^2 L^(-H), choose

  b0=H+D+3,  Q<=N L^(-H-D-2),
  B >= (H+3b0+d_aux+4)/2.

Then (P4) and (P6) give the asserted estimate, with spare log powers.
All choices are fixed constants. There is no circular choice between
the auxiliary divisor exponent and the desired SW saving.

6. The divisor weight in the MODULUS needs no shifted Shiu theorem

For each q<=N, the exact primitive-class variance satisfies

 V(q)=sum_b^*|A_b|^2-|sum_b^*A_b|^2/phi(q)
      <=sum_b^*|A_b|^2
      <=(N/q+1) sum_n |a_n|^2
      <<N^2 L^D/q.                                      (P7)

The second inequality is Cauchy within each class, whose number of
integers is at most N/q+1. This does not require a pointwise divisor
bound or an average theorem in a progression. It follows that

 sum_{q<=Q}tau(q)^(2w)V(q)
       <<N^2 L^(D+d_w),  d_w=2^ceil(2w).

Cauchy across q gives

 sum_{q<=Q}tau(q)^w V(q)
  <= (sum V(q))^(1/2) (sum tau(q)^(2w)V(q))^(1/2).

Use section5 with H=2A+D+d_w+2. Its sufficient modulus threshold is
Q<=N L^(-2A-2D-d_w-4), and the result is even O(N^2 L^(-A-1)).
Weakening proves (BDH). This supplies the weighted version that Maynard
deduces from his unweighted citation, with all costs visible. In the
actual one-bounded case D=0. More generally any fixed divisor bound on
a_n supplies (H1) by elementary divisor moments, if that case is needed.

7. A printed mean in Lemma13.8 must NOT be used literally

The statement of Maynard I Lemma13.8 and its first variance formulas
retain 1_{(n,q)=1}/phi(q), correctly. In the last displayed definition
of S2 in its proof (local extraction around lines4209--4215), the
coprime indicator is absent. That literal alternative has no arbitrary-
log saving, even for a_n=1 on N<n<=2N with N even: at q=2 the sole
primitive class has N/2 terms but the written unfiltered mean is N,
giving variance N^2/4. The sequence1 satisfies the source's SW condition.

This is a local formula discrepancy, not a counterexample to the actual
Lemma13.8 statement. The direct proof (P1)--(P7) uses its correct
coprime mean throughout and does not rely on the defective display.
Neither BFI Theorem0 nor that S2 display is an input to this derivation.

8. Exact interface for the current preliminary D application

The normalized individual medium coefficient is rough1 or rough mu,
with its arbitrary individual interval, fixed normalized logarithms
and retained polylog Mellin twist. The separate explicit rough-SW
proof supplies (H2) with C0=1 uniformly over that family, and its
one-bound gives (H1) with D=0. Its source interval scale is N=U;
the complete source x=UV satisfies N>=x^xi for fixed xi>0. Therefore
log N and log x are comparable with fixed constants. The common
chart roughness window and target dependence change none of these
constants. They do not introduce an arithmetic class filter in a_n.

D requires a modulus range Qmean<=8QD, with QD<=N*x^(-delta), for
fixed delta>0. Consequently 8QD<=N/(1+log(2N))^C eventually for
EVERY fixed C used above. No new numerical or sieve-level margin is
required. The factor8 and fixed smooth endpoint constants are harmless
because a positive power gap was already reserved.

The source's final displayed (14.6)--(14.7) uses a weight tau(s)^(3B0)
and the exact original-alpha variance with s=qde; at E=1, s=qd. The
local theorem applies with any fixed w, including 3B0. If the repaired
main-term calculation instead keeps

  a_n[r]=a_n*1_{(n,r)=1},

apply the AUXILIARY version directly. With C0=1 its price is tau(r)^2;
with a common source notation C0<=B0 it is at most tau(r)^(2B0).
This is uniform for all r, not merely for small r. Do not replace this
by a claim that every filtered sequence has the same unnormalized SW
constants for free.

For example, if the separately proved Gram/Cauchy reduction gives

  E <= M log(x)^C1/(QD R)
       *sum_{r~R}tau(r)^(2B0)
          *sum_{s<=8QD}tau(s)^(2B0+1)
             *sum_b^*|D_r(s,b)|^2,                       (P8)

then (BDH) with C0<=B0 bounds the r-sum by

  N^2 log(N)^(-A) sum_{r~R}tau(r)^(4B0)
       << R N^2 log(x)^(-A+C2).

Thus E<<MN^2/(QD)*log(x)^(-A+C1+C2). Increase A first to pay the
fixed C1,C2 and the outer dispersion Cauchy cost. No sum over R
individual logarithmic errors is replaced by a maximum x^o(1);
the displayed 1/R and the divisor MEAN are essential.

Equation(P8) is an INTERFACE example, not an assertion proved by this
mean-square note. The new exact Gram/main-term component must establish
its normalization and coefficient weights separately. This report
neither assumes it to prove (BDH), nor infers a complete D theorem from
(BDH). This keeps the two components non-circular. Primitive residue
restrictions involving T may be dropped from the nonnegative mean-
square bound; T itself is not a character conductor.

9. Endpoints and uniformity boundaries

Any one subinterval I is included in a_n and the bound is in the
ambient scale N, not in |I|. It is uniform over all such I when (H2)
is. One may also index the entire family by T, the original chart,
Mellin frequency, or an auxiliary filter d0, with the common constants
already specified. The theorem does NOT place sup_I inside the sum
over moduli for an independently chosen endpoint at each modulus.
That would require a separate maximal/variation argument. The actual
Fourier coefficient is fixed while moduli are summed; in D, partial
summation endpoints are frozen before subsequent uses that need this
property. Polylogarithmically many separate applications can be paid
by a stronger requested logarithmic saving.

Other exclusions: arbitrary arithmetic weights on a_n, uncontrolled
growth of the number of HB slots or of C0, a modulus-dependent choice
of a_n, and a Q range without the stated logarithmic or actual power
gap are not proved. Classical large sieve and classical unrough
Mobius SW remain explicit external inputs. No beyond-half prime
distribution, spectral conclusion, low-rank convolution BV, numerical
integral, or global K/c/b claim is obtained by this note alone.
