Adversarial review of the retained spectral packet and its actual completion
9 October 2026. New independent local review; no earlier file is modified.

0. Scope, independence, and outcome

I previously reviewed the retained smooth phase's five-variable variation,
the moving-target scale budget, and downstream sieve interfaces. I did not
author the oldform-packet correction in the two documents examined here.
This review independently rereads the indicated primary spectral sources
and supplies the interval and actual Poisson-completion calculations below.
It is not an independent reproof of Deshouillers--Iwaniec spectral theory,
the Atkin--Lehner/oldform theorems used by Assing--Blomer--Li (ABL), or the
entire dispersion argument. No numerical sieve certificate is used as a
proof of any analytic assertion in this report.

Outcome: no additional large-residue power loss or coefficient-class
obstruction was found in the ACTUAL coprime packet subclass. Its local
bound follows from precisely stated classical spectral inputs, the ABL
packet calculation, and the direct completion in section 6. This conclusion
does not certify unrestricted L23 Lemma 9.6/Theorem 1.8, arbitrary weights
in the original modulus q, the whole Proposition 5.2, or a prime-counting
theorem. It does not restore any project-wide unconditional status.

There are two genuine literal problems in the printed proof of L23
Theorem 9.1, described in section 8. They are not counterexamples to its
statement. The needed special spectral estimates can instead be obtained
from the explicit a-free classical inputs in Pascadi, as in section 3.

1. The packet statement that is actually justified

Use DI-normalized Fourier coefficients. Set Theta=7/32 and
tau_j=2 sqrt(1/4-lambda_j) on the exceptional spectrum; 0<tau_j<=Theta.
ABL/Pascadi's Ramanujan exponent is Theta/2. Confusing these conventions
would change a power, so all formulas below use Theta.

Fix a level q=rs, (r,s)=1, and assume (a,q)=1. In a packet H_f generated
by one newform f and its degeneracy maps v_d(z)=f(dz), normalize all v_d
in the SAME level-q norm. ABL explicitly gives ||v_d||=1 and an
orthonormal basis e_g=sum_(d|g) xi_g(d)v_d with divisor-bounded xi_g(d).
The number of vectors and the needed matrix norms are q^epsilon for any
prescribed epsilon>0. This does not assert that the v_d are orthogonal.

Write n=h n0, h|a^infinity, (n0,a)=1. In ABL Lemma 3.1 the integer K
equals 1: q0=1 and the residue is coprime to rs. For each d its exact
coefficient identity therefore has the form

  rho_(v_d,1/s)(a h n0)
    =lambda_d(a h) e(n0(a h-1) sbar/r) rho_(v_d,1/s)(n0),

with the corresponding signs for negative Fourier arguments, and
|lambda_d(a h)| << (|a|h)^(Theta/2+epsilon). The Hecke eigenvalue MAY
depend on d. No argument needs it to be constant in the oldform packet.
The important point is that the displayed additive phase does NOT depend
on d when K=1.

For b'_n=e(-an sbar/r)b_n in the positive-argument Kuznetsov expression,
define

  b*_(n0,r,s;h)=1_((n0,a)=1) b'_(hn0,r,s)
                          e(n0(a h-1)sbar/r).

In that sign convention the two phases even cancel to e(-n0 sbar/r),
but cancellation is not required: |b*|<=|b_(hn0,r,s)| is sufficient.
The opposite sign is treated consistently by conjugation and ABL's
signed identity. There is no deletion of a Bezout phase in this step.

Let L_a be the original Fourier functional on H_f and L_h the functional
with coefficients b*. Expanding in e_g, using the identity on each v_d,
and then using |L_h(v_d)|<=||L_h||_(H_f*) gives

  ||L_a||_(H_f*) << (|a|Nq)^epsilon
       sum_(h|a^infinity,h<=2N) (|a|h)^(Theta/2) ||L_h||_(H_f*).   (P)

One way to verify (P) is to apply the triangle inequality in h to the
vector indexed by g, then bound its d-to-g matrix by its Frobenius norm.
Every loss is a fixed power of a divisor bound. This avoids an incorrect
pointwise assertion about an individual orthonormal oldform e_g.

Pair L_a with the m-side vector A_f in the ORIGINAL orthonormal packet:
|<A_f,L_a>|<=||A_f||||L_a||. All members of this packet have the same
spectral parameter, so any nonnegative scalar spectral weight commutes
with this step. Apply (P), then Cauchy--Schwarz over packets and levels
separately for each h. The m-side vector remains the Fourier sum for the
same common interval. No new m-dependent Hecke coefficient is inserted.

For h<=x^B and |a|<=x^B, the number of h|a^infinity is x^epsilon for
every chosen epsilon after adjusting constants. Explicitly, Rankin with
delta>0 bounds it by x^(B delta) prod_(p|a)(1-p^(-delta))^(-1); the latter
product is x^o(1), by splitting primes at log x. Choose delta first in
terms of the desired total epsilon. Thus the subsequent Cauchy--Schwarz
in h does not conceal a small-a hypothesis.

2. Regular, holomorphic, and continuous pieces

The same finite packet estimate applies to regular Maass forms. After
dyadic spectral decomposition, use the classical regular large sieve
with its (T^2+N/q) factor and the standard Bessel-transform decay. These
are the stated external regular-spectrum inputs (L23 Theorem 8.2 and
Lemma 8.3 in the DI normalization), not consequences of the exceptional
estimate alone.

For holomorphic forms one uses the weight-normalized degeneracy maps
d^(k/2)f(dz), exactly as in the proof of ABL Lemma 3.2; Deligne permits
Theta=0 there, and retaining the larger Maass bound is harmless.

An Eisenstein series is NOT an L2 vector in H_f. For the continuous part
the appropriate input is ABL's continuous quadratic-form version of
Lemma 3.2, on each character-pair oldform fiber, with the Plancherel
measure retained. ABL PDF p. 11 identifies the orthogonal oldform fiber
and the Atkin--Lehner pseudo-eigenfunction property, citing Young
sections 8.5 and 9.1. That a-free fiber normalization and the continuous
regular large sieve remain named external inputs. The n^(it)/m^(it)
factors appearing in the continuous Kuznetsov formula have modulus one;
the regular estimate permits arbitrary coefficients. No interval-side
exceptional improvement is claimed for these continuous fibers.

3. The two needed exceptional estimates and interval completion

The fixed-level input F is Pascadi Theorem A restricted to
X << max(1,q/L), at a cusp with mu=1/q:

  sum_exc X^tau_j |sum_(n~L) c_n rho_(j,a)(n)|^2
       << (qL)^epsilon (1+L/q)||c||_2^2,  L>=1/2.             (F)

The coefficient sequence is arbitrary complex and may differ by level.
The factor 1/cosh(pi*kappa_j) sometimes included in the exceptional
spectral measure is positive and bounded above and below by absolute
constants, since it is 1/cos(pi*tau_j/2) and tau_j<=7/32. It changes
none of these norm comparisons.
For the packet term h put L=N/h and X0=1+RS/L. At q=rs in its dyadic
range, 1+X0=2+RS/L<=3 max(1,q/L). Therefore (F) applies at exactly the
n-side weight used. Nonempty h terms have L>=1/2. It gives
x^epsilon(1+N/(hRS))||tilde b(h)||_2^2 after summing over r,s.
No general large-X version of L23 Theorem 9.1 is needed.

The level-average input J is Pascadi Theorem J, uniformly in omega:

  sum_(q~Q)sum_exc X^tau_j |sum_(m~M)e(m omega)rho_(j,infinity)(m)|^2
       << (QM)^epsilon (Q+M)M,
  X << max(M,Q^2/M),  Q,M>=1/2.                            (J)

To treat a fixed integer interval I inside (M,2M], choose an integer
P>2M. Its exact finite Fourier expansion on that dyadic block is
1_I(m)=sum_(k mod P)c_k e(km/P), with
c_k=P^(-1)sum_(v in I)e(-kv/P). The geometric-series estimate gives
sum_k|c_k|=O(log(2M)). Take the triangle inequality in the Hilbert norm
indexed by (q,j), with coordinate weights X^tau_j, before squaring.
Applying (J) frequency by frequency costs O(log^2(2M)), absorbed by a
separately smaller epsilon. This proves the same (J) bound for every
common I, including partial, empty, and singleton intervals.
An existing common phase e(mt) is included by replacing k/P with
t+k/P in (J); its uniformity in omega is used at exactly this point.

For a common bounded-variation weight w(m), Hilbert-space partial
summation costs ||w||_infinity+Var(w) in norm. In particular m^(it)
costs O(1+|t|), paid by the smooth transform's rapid decay. This is not
an exchange of a pointwise supremum with the spectral sum.

Set X*=max(M,Q^2/M). Since X*<=(Q+M)^2/M<=4X*, positivity and
tau_j<=Theta extend the interval estimate to every Y>0 as

  S_I(Q,Y,M) << (QM)^epsilon
     [1+(MY/(Q+M)^2)^Theta](Q+M)M.                       (J*)

For q=rs the multiplicity of each level is at most tau(q), paid by
x^epsilon, not by Q. At infinity the identity scaling matrix is used.
The second cusp may vary with the factorization of q; it occurs only
on the arbitrary-coefficient n side.

IMPORTANT LIMIT: the DFT proof does not authorize arbitrary intervals
I_q chosen separately at each level or eigenvector. Their completion
coefficients would depend on the Hilbert coordinate. Section 5 explains
why the actual Lemma 5.1 application does not require that stronger claim.

4. Reconstructing the local spectral polynomial

First give the general r,s bookkeeping. Put
X=C S sqrt(R)/(4 pi sqrt(|a|MN)), L=N/h, X0=1+RS/L.
Up to an absolute constant,

  1+X^tau <= 2 (1+X/sqrt(X0))^tau (1+X0)^(tau/2).

Use this split in the packet pairing, (F) on the n side, and (J*) on
the m side with Y=(1+X/sqrt(X0))^2. The additional exceptional factor is

  [C^2 S^2 R/((RS+M)^2(L+RS))]^(Theta/2),

because the factor (|a|h)^(Theta/2) from (P) cancels the corresponding
(|a|h)^(-Theta/2) from the level-average bound. The ordinary exceptional
part has factor (|a|h)^(Theta/2). Summation over h and replacement of
||tilde b(h)|| by ||b|| are paid only after these operations.

For the actual application S=1, q0=1, the resulting Kloosterman sum L
on m~M obeys the following convenient squared bound (absolute constants
and independently selectable x^epsilon losses are suppressed):

 |L|^2 <= x^epsilon * M*C^4*R/(|a|MN+C^2 R) *
   { sum_h (|a|h)^Theta
       (N/h+R+|a|MN/C^2)(M+R+|a|MN/C^2)||tilde b(h)||_2^2
     +(C^2 R)^Theta(N+R)^(1-Theta)(M+R)^(1-2Theta)||b||_2^2 }. (K)

The first product includes regular, holomorphic, continuous, and the
unimproved exceptional term. The second is precisely the interval-side
exceptional improvement. This calculation reproduces the restricted
polynomial in L23 Theorem 10.2; it does not import its false same-j step.

For a general smooth amplitude with normalized derivative bounds, one
can make the separation step explicit: change the c coordinate to the
normalized Bessel argument, retain normalized m,n,r coordinates, insert
fixed compact smooth cutoffs on the resulting fixed-size box, and take
its Fourier expansion. Its coefficients have integrable polynomial
decay of arbitrarily high fixed order. Each term has a COMMON m phase,
a cusp-side coefficient phase, and a scalar level weight. The latter
can be placed on the arbitrary n vector. Bessel-transform seminorms
grow polynomially in the expansion frequencies and are absorbed by
using additional derivatives. The change uses log derivatives of
sqrt(|a|mn)/(c sqrt r); their normalized bounds do not grow with |a|.
This proves the smooth-amplitude extension used in (K). It does not
allow arbitrary nonsmooth coefficients jointly depending on m and q.

5. Why the actual modulus and interval manipulations satisfy that class

In L23 Lemma 5.1 keep d fixed and use fdq=n1-n2; then |f|<=2N/(dQ).
The original q interval becomes a condition on n1-n2 and f. Product
endpoints are first separated by the displayed Fourier identity on
PDF p. 15. Cauchy--Schwarz is then applied in f,n1,n2,r1,r2,s2. Only
after this positive square is formed are the congruence and endpoint
conditions discarded, as explicitly done on PDF p. 16.

Combining b=n2*r1 and c=n1*r2*s2 then gives a full positive b,c box,
with divisor-bounded multiplicity and a smooth majorant g(b,c). The
inner s1,h sum is independent of the chosen representation of b,c.
It is therefore legitimate to bound that multiplicity by x^epsilon
before summing; this does not leave arbitrary rough b coefficients.
In particular the future Poisson dual m side has common interval/phase
coefficients, not level-dependent endpoint choices.

The original coprimality (s1,a)=1 is RETAINED. Expanding the square gives
z=s1*s2 with (z,a)=1. After removing absolute values by unit phases the
coefficient b_(y,z) is arbitrary complex, but vanishes unless (z,a)=1
and has the same l2 majorant as its counting coefficient. Thus the
completion uses (C,D,N,R,S,q0)=(C,B,Y,Z,1,1), precisely the class above.
No extension to arbitrary original-q weights is used. The five-variable
retained smooth phase must already have been reduced to these permitted
rectangular instances by its separate variation argument; it is not
silently inserted as a new rough spectral coefficient here.

6. Direct Poisson completion for q0=1, S=1

This calculation removes an avoidable citation-only step in the reviewed
documents. Let (a,r)=1 and consider

  V=sum_(n~N,r~R)b_(n,r) sum_(c~C,(c,r)=1)
       sum_(d~D,(d,c)=1)g(c,d,n,r)e(an inverse(rd)/c).

Poisson in d is exact:

  sum_(d,(d,c)=1)g(c,d,n,r)e(an rbar dbar/c)
     =1/c sum_(m in Z) ghat_(c,n,r)(m/c) S(m rbar,an;c).  (C0)

The identity between the two Kloosterman conventions uses (c,r)=1.
The Fourier transform has size O(D) and rapid decay in |m|D/C.

Zero frequency. S(0,an;c) is a Ramanujan sum and
|S(0,an;c)|<=gcd(an,c). Moreover
sum_(c~C)gcd(an,c)<=O(C tau(|an|)), by the divisor expansion of gcd.
As n>=1 and a!=0, no tau(0) occurs. Its total contribution is at most

  x^epsilon D sum_(n,r)|b_(n,r)|
      <=x^epsilon D sqrt(NR)||b||_2.

This gives exactly the D^2 NR term in the squared reciprocal-sum bound.
It does not require (a,c)=1 or a small residue. The divisor estimate is
uniform when |a| and all other scales are bounded by fixed powers of x.

Nonzero frequencies. Truncate |m|<= (C/D)x^delta, using sufficiently many
derivatives and the trivial Kloosterman bound for the tail. The tail is
arbitrarily small compared with the stated polynomial bound. Divide
the retained positive/negative frequencies into dyadic intervals M.
After removing the prefactor D/C, their amplitudes have normalized
derivative seminorms at most x^(O(delta)); the exponent delta is chosen
independently small. Apply (K) with M<= (C/D)x^delta. Bounded ranges
M<1 cause no problem: a nonempty positive-integer block may be assigned
M>=1/2; if even this exceeds the truncation there is only the tail.

Here is the exact polynomial calculation with M<=C/D; restoring the
x^delta slack costs only a separately selectable subpower. Write
T=|a|MN/C^2 and N'=N/h. Then

 R(N'+R+T)(M+R+T)/(R+T)
   =R(R+T+N'+M)+R N'M/(R+T)
   <=(N'+R)(M+R)+RT.

Multiplying (K) by D^2/C^2 gives, for its first term,

 D^2 M [(N'+R)(M+R)+R T]
       <= C(N'+R)(C+DR)+|a|NR.

For its second term, bound R/(R+T)<=1 and use 1-2Theta>=0:

 D^2 M (C^2 R)^Theta(N+R)^(1-Theta)(M+R)^(1-2Theta)
       <= C(CD sqrt R)^(2Theta)
                   (N+R)^(1-Theta)(C+DR)^(1-2Theta).

Consequently

 |V|^2 << x^epsilon J^2,
 J^2 = sum_h (|a|h)^Theta
          [C(N/h+R)(C+DR)+|a|NR]||tilde b(h)||_2^2
       +[C(CD sqrt R)^(2Theta)(N+R)^(1-Theta)(C+DR)^(1-2Theta)
            +D^2 NR]||b||_2^2.                          (C1)

Dyadic summation adds logarithms only. This is the actual S=1 instance
of the reviewed Theorem 1.8 polynomial. ABL PDF p. 13 warns that a factor
S^(-1/2) in an older general completion was erroneous; the calculation
above neither uses it nor claims the unrestricted S-dependent statement.

7. Returning to the counting norm and natural scale

Set B=NR, C=NRS, Z=S^2, Y=NR^2S^3/M, F=N/Q, and
H=x^o(1) QR^2S^2/M, now reverting to the ORIGINAL Lemma 5.1 letters.
In (C1) substitute the spectral R=Z, N=Y, D=B. The retained condition
(a,z)=1 supplies the only residue-level coprimality required above.

The count coefficient comes from y=f(h1*s1-h2*s2)!=0. Its norm obeys
||tilde b(h)||_2^2 << x^epsilon (HS)^2 F/h. The equal-difference count
uses nonzero multiples of h/c in |k|<=O(HS), giving O(HSc/h), with no
spurious +1 term. For unequal differences, split d1=(s1,s2), reduce
the s pair, and count the common difference multiplier in O(Hd1/S);
the result is x^epsilon H^3 S F/h times
sum_(d1<=O(S))(d1,h)/d1=x^o(1). Since H/S<x^o(1)(Q/N)D0<1, this is
at most the equal-difference bound. The individual k need not be of
size HS; nonzero k and an upper bound suffice.

Using Y/Z=D0<1 and BZ>=C, (C1) gives

 J^2 << x^epsilon [|a|^Theta(CBZ+|a|Y)Z
                     +C^(1+2Theta)BZ^(2-2Theta)+B^2YZ](HS)^2 F.

The exact scale relation is CBZ=xY=N^2R^2S^3 up to the original fixed
dyadic constants. Also B^2<x. Therefore the |a|Y term MUST be retained;
it produces 1+|a|/x, rather than being absorbed by a small-a convention.
The outside factor after the earlier Cauchy--Schwarz is
x^epsilon N/(Q R S^(3/2)); the two square roots give the fourth root
of the last spectral polynomial. In the notation of the reviewed audit:

 |W| << (N^2/Q)x^epsilon
       {D0^(1/2)+[(1+|a|/x)D1+D2]^(1/4)}.

Thus the extra large-a cost here is kappa/4 when |a|<=x^(1+kappa).
The smooth retained-phase variation's 5 kappa cost is a separate earlier
operation. All divisor/log/frequency losses above can fit a prescribed
epsilon allowance because there are finitely many operations and every
scale is polynomially bounded in x. Neither 5 kappa nor the fixed
spectral margin may be relabelled as an arbitrary o(1).

8. Adversarial findings and final dependency boundary

(a) L23's printed same-j Lemma 9.6 is not justified by ABL's norm lemma.
    The packet derivation (P) is a repair only for the coprime q0=1 class.
    When (a,rs)>1, K can vary and the common transformed functional used
    above is no longer established. That stronger assertion is unreviewed.

(b) L23 Theorem 9.1's proof chooses Y=4 pi mu N and a test supported in
    [Y,2Y], but m,n in (N,2N] can yield an argument inside this interval.
    The claimed empty geometric side does not follow. Moving the support
    to [16 pi mu N,32 pi mu N] fixes this constant-scale issue.
    Also, for 0<Y<1 and tau_j<=Theta_q one has
    Y^(-tau_j)<=Y^(-Theta_q), not the displayed reverse comparison.
    Keeping tau_j gives the correct inequality
    X^tau_j<=[1+(XY)^Theta_q]Y^(-tau_j).
    These are proof-line defects, not counterexamples to the spectrum.
    Sections 3--4 bypass both lines using the explicitly stated F/J inputs.

(c) A level-dependent interval assertion is not proved by common-interval
    DFT. Actual positive Cauchy--Schwarz and smooth completion avoid needing
    it. Arbitrary original modulus weights must not be added later.

(d) The Eisenstein part requires its actual continuous-fiber theorem; a
    casual reference to an L2 packet of Eisenstein functions is incorrect.
    ABL supplies the relevant continuous quadratic-form statement as an
    external input, and the regular bound suffices there.

Dependency order verified here:
  ABL 3.1 + normalized oldform basis -> (P);
  classical F/J + common-interval DFT -> exceptional weighted norms;
  standard Kuznetsov/Bessel/regular large sieve + (P) -> (K);
  exact S=1 Poisson + Ramanujan/divisor bound -> (C1);
  actual positive endpoint removal + counting norm -> restricted Lemma5.1.

The retained-phase variation, preliminary dispersion identities, Siegel--
Walfisz input, full Proposition5.2, prime-product compiler, and all final
sieve/count implications remain outside this local conclusion. Their
proofs cannot be inferred from this packet review. No source named PASS
was used in place of the calculations above, and no global K,c,b was
changed or promoted.
