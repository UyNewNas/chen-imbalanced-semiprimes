\subsection{Restoring transverse variation in the common-affine bound}
\label{sec:eighth-joint-affine-curvature-20261009}

Retain the notation and hypotheses of
\texttt{eighth-joint-affine-moment-pilot-20261009.tex}:
ten positive affine factors on a physical eight-simplex, the
nonnegative affine gap-weighted probability measure $\nu$,
$a_j=\ell_j/m_j$, $P=\sum_ja_j/10$, $E_\nu P=1$ and
$U=\max_{\operatorname{Vert}}P$. Let $T_{10}$ denote the exact degree10
common-affine binomial-moment gap there. All geometry, caps and volumes
remain the frozen winner9 seed; only its integral estimate is refined.

\paragraph{A pointwise quadratic remainder.}
For each $j$ set
\[
 V_j=\max_{v\in\operatorname{Vert}}\{a_j(v),P(v)\}>0.
\]
At a fixed point of the simplex, the entire segment joining $a_j$ to
$P$ lies in $(0,V_j]$. The second derivative of $-\log x$ there is
at least $V_j^{-2}$. Taylor's integral remainder therefore gives
\[
 -\log a_j+\log P+\frac{a_j-P}{P}
 \ge\frac{(a_j-P)^2}{2V_j^2}.
\]
The linear terms cancel pointwise since $\sum_j(a_j-P)=0$. Hence
\[
 \prod_j a_j^{-1}
 \ge P^{-10}\exp\!\left(\sum_j\frac{(a_j-P)^2}{2V_j^2}\right)
 \ge P^{-10}\left(1+\sum_j\frac{(a_j-P)^2}{2V_j^2}\right).
\]
Since $P\le U$ and the squared remainder is nonnegative, expectation
and the already proved $E_\nu P^{-10}\ge1+T_{10}$ yield
\[
 E_\nu\prod_ja_j^{-1}\ge1+T_{10}+Q_T,
 \qquad
 Q_T=U^{-10}\sum_{j=1}^{10}
          \frac{E_\nu(a_j-P)^2}{2V_j^2}\ge0.
 \tag{C}
\]
The density integral is consequently at least $J_T(1+T_{10}+Q_T)$.
This is a pointwise-derived refinement of the common-affine inequality;
it is not a sum of that inequality and the old factorwise logarithmic
increment. Correlations between all factors remain allowed.

\paragraph{Exact signed second moments.}
Scale the nine vertex gaps to integers $g_i\ge0$, with
$G=\sum_i g_i>0$. For an arbitrary signed affine $b$ with vertex
values $b_i$, put $s=\sum_i b_i$, $q=\sum_i b_i^2$,
$p=\sum_i g_i b_i$ and $w=\sum_i g_i b_i^2$. The same
Dirichlet mixture of total parameter ten gives
\[
 E_\nu b^2=\frac{G(s^2+q)+2sp+2w}{110G}.
 \tag{D2}
\]
This follows by averaging
$[(\sum_i b_i+b_k)^2+(\sum_i b_i^2+b_k^2)]/(10\cdot11)$
with weights $g_k/G$. It is valid for negative $b_i$; no positivity
of individual cross moments is assumed. For $b=a_j-P$, the source
also checks $Gs+p=0$, equivalent to $E_\nu b=0$.

The integer implementation uses the exact common denominator $L$ of
the normalized vertex values from the plain certificate. Write
$a_j(v_i)-P(v_i)=b_{ji}/L$, $U=B/L$, and
$V_j=v_j/L$. If $N_j$ is the numerator
$G(s^2+q)+2sp+2w$ formed with the integer $b_{ji}$, then
\[
 Q_T=(L/B)^{10}\sum_j\frac{N_j}{220Gv_j^2}.
\]
The powers of $L$ cancel inside each quadratic ratio. Every $N_j$ is
checked nonnegative by exact integer arithmetic. The author evaluates
(D2); a separate pairwise barycentric-moment implementation can replay
it without sharing this arithmetic formula.

\paragraph{Rounding and accounting.}
The total $T_{10}+Q_T$ is formed exactly before a single downward
rounding to $2^{-80}$. Multiplication by the exact reconstructed
$J_T$ is then rounded downward to $2^{-96}$:
\[
 \widehat T=2^{-80}\lfloor2^{80}(T_{10}+Q_T)\rfloor,
 \quad
 b_C=2^{-96}\lfloor2^{96}J_T(1+\widehat T)\rfloor.
\]
The accepted simplex value is $\max(b_{\log},b_C)$, where $b_{\log}$
is the already adopted factorwise log6--exp3 floor. Because $Q_T\ge0$
and rounding is monotone, this value also dominates the saved plain
P10 maximum on every simplex. Thus the only admissible total addition
is the new summed maximum minus the old log sum. The plain P10 gain
must not be added again.

The complete run then processed all $11{,}026$ original simplices in

 B_{\log}=\frac{475182339168330434688559}{2^{96}},\qquad
 B_{C,\mathrm{raw}}=\frac{474139291291580412934007}{2^{96}},

\[
 B_{C,\max}=\frac{237629874802202295777015}{2^{95}},\qquad
 B_{C,\max}-B_{\log}
 =\frac{77410436074156865471}{2^{96}}
 \simeq9.77057066800\cdot10^{-10}.
 \tag{R}
\]
The additional gain over the unadopted plain P10 maximum is
$4637903256434772219/2^{92}$, but it is not separately added to (R).
The raw curvature sum is still below the old log sum, so per-simplex
maximization remains essential. Relative to the old seed log sum,
